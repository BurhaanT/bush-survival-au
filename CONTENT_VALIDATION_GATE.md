# Universal content validation gate

## Working illustrated edition exception — D-031–D-034, 7 September 2026

The founder has requested readable, practical personal content with pictures while formal approval remains unfinished. A working chapter may therefore include source-supported conditional species preparation and a rights-recorded working image before independent sign-off. This is an authoring status, not a safety exemption.

Before inclusion, compare the exact claim with multiple relevant sources; record where they share an underlying authority and where access was incomplete. Inspect the actual picture against the words, verify provenance and reuse terms, and show a preceding warning naming missing diagnostic views or technique limitations. Give an honest evidence-confidence label and, where the founder requested it for consequential unapproved content, a clearly labelled editorial percentage estimate for the accuracy of the wording. The percentage is not a calibrated statistic, survival chance, success rate or approval. Keep warning, caption and source attached in Markdown and the reader.

An incomplete plant photograph cannot approve a specimen. Several angles still cannot replace a matched lookalike panel, specimen authentication or treatment-history evidence. A plausible diagram cannot prove physical performance. Unsupported harmful procedures remain excluded. No content or image gains Approved status through this exception; all field-ready gates below still apply.

**Effective:** 4 September 2026  
**Applies to:** every factual statement, instruction, decision rule, table, map, caption, photograph, illustration, icon and recommendation in the Victorian Bush Survival Field Guide.

## Non-negotiable rule

### 7 September 2026 clarification — drafting is not release approval

At the founder's request, source-supported instructions may now be written in the canonical chapters and assembled into a **clearly marked working manuscript before independent approval**. The earlier outline-only drafting restriction is superseded. Do not require full release approval merely to produce useful review prose.

Before a high-consequence section, include a visible Markdown warning box stating the evidence confidence, exact unresolved issue, review status and practical limit. Use High, Moderate, Low or Insufficient as defined in the book's opening chapter. These are author assessments of evidence agreement, not calibrated probabilities. Because the founder explicitly requested an estimated percentage for consequential content still awaiting authority review, also give a clearly labelled **editorial estimate of wording accuracy** where a defensible source-comparison basis exists. State that it is subjective and uncalibrated. Never state it as the odds of safety, survival, treatment success, identification or technique performance. If no percentage is defensible, give a percentage for the accuracy of the warning or prohibition itself and state that the outcome is unknown. A warning must precede the affected advice and remain attached through layout.

This permission does not admit a speculative dangerous procedure just because it has a warning. Where evidence cannot support a safe novice instruction, explain that boundary and the supported alternative. No image, species identification, consumption recipe or medical improvisation becomes acceptable through a disclaimer alone.

The **field-ready** gate below is unchanged. A marked working manuscript may contain unresolved review items; an approved/usable field edition may not. The edition flag remains NO until the release checks actually pass.

Nothing enters the usable combined Markdown or PDF merely because it is plausible, traditional, widely repeated, visually convincing or present in another survival book.

Each content unit must be traceable in [CONTENT_REGISTER.md](CONTENT_REGISTER.md), assigned a consequence level, checked against the required number and quality of sources, assessed for Victorian applicability, reviewed for conflicts and limitations, and given an explicit approval status.

Incomplete material belongs in research notes and registers—not in a field-ready edition.

## Content status

| Status | Meaning | May appear in usable edition? |
|---|---|---|
| Proposed | Topic or visual suggested; no evidence review. | No |
| Researching | Sources collected but checks incomplete. | No |
| Cross-checked | Minimum independent sources obtained; conflicts recorded. | Only low-consequence content, after editorial approval |
| Specialist review required | Source checks complete but appropriate human review is outstanding. | No |
| Field/usability test required | Factually supported, but performance or interpretation has not been tested. | No where failure is consequential |
| Approved | All applicable checks completed for the named edition/version. | Yes |
| Restricted | Useful only with a visible limitation, condition or regional/seasonal boundary. | Yes, exactly as approved |
| Rejected | Evidence is weak, conflicting, unsafe, unlawful, impractical or out of scope. | No |
| Superseded | Previously approved content has been replaced or made stale. | No |

## Consequence levels

### Level 1 — low consequence

Examples: historical context that does not guide action, benign definitions, non-critical descriptions and general orientation.

Minimum gate:

- Two independent sources.
- At least one authoritative or primary source where reasonably available.
- Publication/access dates recorded.
- Victoria relevance checked.
- Wording does not imply more certainty than the evidence.
- Editorial approval recorded.

### Level 2 — operational consequence

Examples: equipment selection, ordinary knots, non-critical shelter improvements, packing recommendations, low-risk weather interpretation and routine route planning.

Minimum gate:

- At least two independent substantive sources; three where context varies materially.
- At least one current Australian or Victorian authoritative source where applicable.
- Assumptions, environmental limits, prerequisites and failure modes recorded.
- Contrary evidence actively searched for and resolved or disclosed.
- Technique reproduced or usability-tested where the instruction asks the reader to perform something.
- Image/diagram checked against the final written sequence.
- Reviewer with relevant practical competence identified for material where failure could strand or injure the reader.
- Approval and review date recorded.

### Level 3 — high consequence

Examples include:

- edible plants, fungi, algae, animal foods and drinking-water claims;
- plant identity, toxicity, preparation and lookalikes;
- first aid, illness, injury, medication and evacuation decisions;
- fire lighting/use and bushfire-related advice;
- stay-versus-move, navigation, river crossing and terrain decisions;
- shelter advice in alpine, flood, falling-limb, lightning or extreme-temperature conditions;
- trapping, fishing, wildlife handling and any technique constrained by law;
- emergency communications and rescue signalling;
- traditional medicinal practice or any claim that could displace evidence-based treatment.

Minimum gate:

1. **Three independent checks:**
   - one current authoritative Australian or Victorian primary source;
   - one independent authoritative or peer-reviewed confirmation;
   - one additional credible source, dataset, standard, field manual or specialist confirmation that tests the same claim in context.
2. **Exact scope:** identify region, environment, season, life stage, equipment, user skill and conditions under which the advice is and is not valid.
3. **Contradiction search:** deliberately seek disconfirming evidence, outdated guidance, known failures, lookalikes and contraindications. Record the result even when no conflict is found.
4. **Currentness:** record the guidance or law date and define a trigger for rechecking it.
5. **Specialist review:** an appropriately qualified human must review the exact final wording and its visuals. The register records name/role, version, date, scope and unresolved reservations.
6. **Performance/usability check:** where an instruction can be safely tested, perform or observe it under representative conditions. Where testing would be unsafe or unlawful, use qualified review and documented evidence instead.
7. **Safe uncertainty:** if a novice cannot reach a reliable decision, the approved instruction must be “do not use/consume/attempt” rather than a guess.
8. **Final cross-check:** citations, warnings, measurements, captions and linked decision rules are rechecked after layout, because design changes can alter meaning.

Level 3 material cannot be marked Approved solely by this project’s authoring process. It requires the relevant human specialist review before it appears as usable guidance in the final PDF.

## Source independence and hierarchy

Three websites repeating the same original statement are one source, not three.

Prefer, in order appropriate to the topic:

1. Victorian/Australian legislation, emergency services, health authorities, recognised standards, botanical institutions and land/water managers.
2. Peer-reviewed research, systematic guidance and official datasets.
3. Specialist organisations, textbooks and field manuals with identifiable expert authorship and edition dates.
4. Reputable secondary works used to interpret or locate primary evidence.
5. Community reports used only for lived experience, failure cases, terminology and questions—not as sole authority for safety, medical, legal or species claims.

SEO articles, anonymous summaries, copied checklists and AI output are discovery aids only. They do not count toward the source minimum.

## Universal claim checklist

Every content unit must answer:

- What exact action or belief could this cause?
- What happens if it is wrong, incomplete or used outside its context?
- Is it current in Victoria?
- Are the sources genuinely independent?
- Did we seek evidence against it?
- Are units, quantities, time ranges and definitions unambiguous?
- Are exceptions and stop conditions visible at the point of use?
- Does a linked image or caption change the apparent instruction?
- Does it conflict with another chapter?
- Is a stressed novice likely to interpret it as intended?
- Who, if anyone, must independently review it?
- What event or date will trigger re-verification?

## Edible plant and natural-food gate

In addition to the Level 3 gate:

- Accepted scientific name and synonyms must be checked at the edition date.
- Victorian distribution must be confirmed through VicFlora/Royal Botanic Gardens Victoria or another appropriate authoritative record.
- Whole plant, diagnostic parts, habitat, scale, season and growth habit must be described.
- Usable part, maturity stage and preparation must be explicit.
- Toxic parts, dose/quantity issues, allergens and known contraindications must be recorded.
- Dangerous and plausible Victorian lookalikes must be shown or the entry rejected.
- Identity must be checked from multiple authoritative botanical records, not from a single photograph.
- Edibility/toxicity/preparation must be independently checked separately from identity/distribution.
- No universal edibility test is to be presented as a safe substitute for positive identification.
- Fungi are presumptively excluded from food advice unless an exceptional expert-reviewed case is approved.
- If colour or another feature is diagnostic, the entry cannot be issued in a format that obscures it.
- Traditional food knowledge requires the cultural gate below as well as botanical and food-safety checks.

## Image and diagram gate

Every visual is a factual assertion about appearance, scale, order, location or effect.

- Assign an image ID in [IMAGE_REGISTER.md](IMAGE_REGISTER.md).
- Record creator, original source, capture date where known, licence/permission and required attribution.
- Confirm that the depicted species, technique, equipment and environment match the caption.
- For species identification, use verified photographs or expert-reviewed scientific illustrations. Never use AI-generated botanical or animal-identification imagery.
- For consequential technique diagrams, compare the visual against all approved steps, warnings, body positions, dimensions and completion tests.
- Cross-check diagnostic species features against at least two authoritative visual/specimen sources and obtain botanical review of the final panel.
- Show scale and multiple diagnostic views where needed; a pretty whole-plant image is not enough.
- Maps must record source data, date, generalisation and limitations.
- AI-generated visuals may be used only as clearly segregated non-factual concepts during design development, never as evidence and never in the usable guide unless every depicted factual element is independently reconstructed and reviewed.
- Recheck the final placed/cropped image; cropping and captions can invalidate an otherwise correct asset.

## Technique and survival-strategy gate

For every practical technique:

- State the survival problem it addresses and its priority relative to rescue, temperature, water and injury.
- State when to use it, when not to use it and safer alternatives.
- Specify materials, dimensions or quantities only when supported and meaningful.
- Record construction/action sequence, completion test and common failure modes.
- Check the technique against Victorian weather, fuel, terrain, fire rules, land status and plausible user equipment.
- Search for injury reports, known failure cases and contradictory professional guidance.
- Do not recommend wildlife harm, water poisoning, dangerous toxins or unlawful methods as ordinary survival instruction.
- A field test never overrides authoritative safety guidance; authoritative guidance does not eliminate the need to test whether a novice can follow the page.

## Medical and first-aid gate

- Use current Australian guidance and terminology.
- Record the clinical guideline version/date.
- Separate recognition, immediate action, contraindication, monitoring and evacuation/000 thresholds.
- Present two parallel action paths wherever equipment affects care:
  - **With a first-aid kit:** name the needed item from the defined comprehensive kit, what it does, how to use it, required training and limits. Owning an item does not mean someone knows how to use it.
  - **Without a first-aid kit:** also applies if the kit is missing the listed items. State the safest evidence-supported action without specialist supplies, or with a checked ordinary material. Explain what cannot be replaced safely and whether missing equipment makes rescue or medical care more urgent.
- Both paths must preserve the same priorities: scene safety, emergency communication, clinically established care, monitoring and evacuation.
- The no-kit path must not recommend invasive improvised procedures, unverified plant/chemical remedies, unsafe substitutes, or an action whose main effect is to delay professional help.
- “Comprehensive kit” is a defined project inventory, not an assumption that every commercial kit contains the necessary item.
- Cross-check each medical page against the equipment chapter so named supplies, quantities, maintenance and expiry advice agree.
- Do not present traditional remedies as clinically established treatment.
- A qualified Australian clinician must review the exact final page, including diagrams and captions.
- Review again after layout and before each PDF edition.

## Legal and current-rules gate

- Record the jurisdiction, land-manager context and access date.
- Link the reader to the current authoritative source where a rule can change.
- Do not imply that an emergency automatically makes every fire, wildlife, fishing, plant-collection or access action lawful.
- Use visible date/version labels for volatile rules.
- Recheck all legal content immediately before generating a field-ready PDF.

## Aboriginal knowledge and ICIP gate

Public availability is not permission for commercial or personal republication.

- Identify Country/Traditional Owner group and geographic context; do not generalise knowledge as pan-Aboriginal.
- Identify the cultural authority able to authorise the proposed use.
- Record whether knowledge is public, restricted, gendered, seasonal, sacred or otherwise context-limited.
- Obtain and preserve consent for the exact wording, image, attribution, distribution and edition where required.
- Follow the contributor/organisation’s preferred authorship, review, benefit and withdrawal arrangements.
- Do not infer permission from expired copyright, museum access, an old ethnography or another author’s publication.
- If an appropriate authority cannot be identified or does not consent, exclude the material.

## Chapter gate

### Plain-language check

Apply [EDITORIAL_STYLE_GUIDE.md](EDITORIAL_STYLE_GUIDE.md) to every reader-facing page and visual, including material already written. Use familiar words, explain necessary technical terms where they appear, put actions in a clear order and keep each warning beside the step it affects. Existing material has no exemption because it was drafted before this rule.

Check that a new reader can explain the instruction in their own words and identify the first action, next action, stop condition and missing-equipment option. Test performance with harmless props where appropriate. Record actual results; an automated readability score is not proof of understanding.

Simplifying the words must preserve the claim's conditions, quantities, sequence and uncertainty. If the action or meaning changes, reopen its review before approval. The authoring process may record an editorial check but cannot claim an independent reader or specialist test happened.

### Action-first emergency-use check

For any chapter a stranded reader may open during an incident:

- Put the main safe action or decision before background, source discussion and unusual cases.
- Start each urgent problem with a short **Do this now** route, then concise `if … then …` branches, stop rules and observable checks.
- Keep each controlling warning before or beside the action it limits. Compress repeated warning boilerplate, but never remove the uncertainty, condition or safer fallback.
- Give each method once and link back to it. Do not make the reader reconcile several versions of the same procedure.
- Separate **stranded now** content from **learn before travel** and reference material.
- Move internal IDs, evidence reconciliation, routine image rights and project QA history out of the emergency reading path while retaining them in the registers.
- Test whether a new reader can reach the correct first action quickly without being coached. Record the time, route taken, errors and any dangerous interpretation in `VALIDATION_LOG.md`.

A shorter page does not pass this check if it loses a condition or makes an incomplete technique appear usable. A longer reference section may remain in the project, but it must not obscure the emergency route.

A chapter may enter the approved, field-ready combined Markdown edition only when:

- every content unit has an ID and allowed status;
- no Level 3 item is awaiting specialist review;
- all citations resolve to the recorded source;
- cross-chapter conflicts have been checked;
- images have cleared accuracy and rights review;
- the chapter has a visible edition/review date;
- a novice usability pass is complete where relevant;
- the plain-language check passes, with comprehension-test results recorded where required;
- the action-first emergency-use check passes where the chapter may be used during an incident;
- unresolved research remains outside the usable chapter.

## Edition and PDF gate

Before generating a field-ready PDF:

1. Freeze a source version and record it.
2. Recheck volatile medical, legal, emergency-service, fire, weather and communications material.
3. Confirm every included content/image ID is Approved or appropriately Restricted.
4. Assemble the Markdown in manifest order and verify headings, links, citations and indexes.
5. Generate the PDF without manually editing the PDF itself.
6. Render every page to images and visually inspect typography, crops, warnings, colour, tables, maps and page transitions.
7. Extract PDF text and check that no content disappeared, duplicated or became unreadable.
8. Recheck high-consequence spreads after layout.
9. Record the final review date, edition, file hash and known limitations.

Any later correction is made in the canonical Markdown/source registers, followed by a complete rebuild and the same PDF checks.
