# Before you go: preparation and kits

> **WARNING — CAPABILITY CHECKLIST, NOT A CERTIFIED KIT**
>
> Conditions, group size, health needs and remoteness change what is enough. **Evidence confidence: High** for carrying communication, protection, water and first aid; **Moderate** for any one packing choice. No list or brand promises survival.

## Carry the functions, not just the objects

A large vehicle kit is no help after you become separated from it. Keep critical items on your body or in the bag that stays with you.

| You must be able to… | Carry and practise… |
|---|---|
| Call for help | Charged phone plus an independent emergency device suitable for the area |
| Give your location | Offline map and coordinates; paper map and compass only with practised skill |
| Stay warm, dry or shaded | Rain/wind layer, insulation, ground layer and deployable shelter |
| Carry safe water | Enough drinking water, sound containers and a tested backup treatment system |
| See and be seen | Headlamp, spare power, whistle and bright signal material |
| Give first aid | Supplies matched to the people, risks and personal action plans |
| Wait longer | Food, essential medicines, spare clothing and a written contact plan |

Do not buy complexity that nobody can operate.

## Water and fire need complete systems

> **WARNING — FIELD HANDLING REVIEW OUTSTANDING**
>
> **Wording confidence: 95%** for the authority-backed rolling-boil and contamination limits; **80%** for the exact field-handling sequence. These are editorial estimates, not a guarantee that a water source or treated batch is safe.

For boiling, carry an intact heat-safe pot, a lawful heat source with correct fuel, a stable cooling place and a clean closable drinking-water container. Keep untreated-water gear away from clean openings, caps, threads and rims. Never use a vessel that held fuel, coolant, pesticide, detergent or another chemical.

Fire is a separate legal and safety decision. This edition gives no dependable zero-equipment way to light a fire or make suspect water safe. Carry and practise the complete system. See [Water](07-water.md) and [Fire](08-fire.md).

## First-aid supplies must match the pages

A broad kit may include gloves, a breathing barrier, absorbent and non-stick dressings, broad elastic and triangular bandages, tape, shears, saline, a rigid eye shield, splinting material, an emergency wrap, pencil and paper.

Add each person's prescribed medicines and current action plans. Check storage and expiry. Adrenaline devices, asthma relievers and diabetes treatments must match that person and product.

Tourniquets and specialist dressings need specific training. More equipment does not create skill.

See the [first-aid kit register](../../FIRST_AID_KIT_REGISTER.md) and [First aid](06-first-aid-and-evacuation.md), which also gives no-kit actions.

## Leave a usable trip plan

Give a reliable person:

- the area, route or operating location;
- names, vehicle details and relevant medical needs;
- emergency devices carried;
- the expected contact or return time; and
- the exact action to take if you are overdue.

Update them when plans change and when you return. A note only on your phone is not a contact plan. Check a borrowed beacon's registration and contact details. Use only the maker's self-test; never trigger a live distress alert as a test.

Sources: [Bushwalking Victoria trip contacts](https://bushwalkingmanual.org.au/trip-planning/trip-emergency-contact/) and [AMSA beacon registration](https://beacons.amsa.gov.au/registration/index.asp).

## Practise before relying on it

Before relying on equipment:

- Retrieve coordinates and the correct map without mobile data.
- Locate the device's emergency instructions without triggering SOS.
- Set up the shelter and find first-aid supplies in poor light.
- Check cables, batteries and storage instructions.
- Demonstrate the exact water-treatment process without drinking suspect test water.

Record failures and fix them. These checks are proposed; the project has not performed them for your equipment.

## At departure

Check current weather, warnings, closures, fire restrictions and local water information for the exact area. Reconsider the trip if conditions exceed your skills or equipment. This guide is a backup to preparation, not a reason to accept a risk you would otherwise avoid.
