# Preparation and kits

> **WARNING — CAPABILITY CHECKLIST, NOT A CERTIFIED KIT**
>
> **Evidence confidence: High for the need for communication, protection, water and first aid; Moderate for any individual packing selection.**
>
> Conditions, group size, medical needs and remoteness change what is sufficient. This list does not promise survival with a particular weight, brand or number of items.

## Keep the essential functions with you

A complete vehicle kit is of little help if you become separated from the vehicle. Decide what must remain on your person or in the bag you actually carry away from it.

| Function | What to be able to do |
|---|---|
| Call for help | Use a working phone and an appropriate independent emergency device for the area |
| Say where you are | Open the correct offline map, read coordinates, use paper map/compass skills |
| Keep warm and dry | Put on rain/wind protection, dry insulation and ground protection; deploy shelter |
| Manage heat | Create shade, protect from sun and retain sufficient potable water |
| See and be seen | Use a headlamp, spare power, whistle and contrasting signal material |
| Give first aid | Find and use the supplies needed for the group's risks and personal plans |
| Drink safely | Carry known potable water in reliable containers and use an appropriate tested treatment backup |
| Wait longer | Access extra food, medicines, clothing and a written contact plan |

The table describes tasks rather than a shopping list. Do not buy complexity that nobody in the group can operate.

### Do not mistake “minimal” for “nothing”

> **WARNING — GERMS ONLY; FIELD HANDLING REVIEW OUTSTANDING**
>
> **Estimated confidence in the accuracy of this wording: 95% for the authority-backed rolling-boil and contamination limits; 80% for the exact field handling sequence.** These are editorial estimates, not a probability that any source or treated batch is safe. Every Chapter 7 source and treatment gate and every Chapter 8 heat-source gate must still pass.

The [rolling-boil route in this guide](07-water.md#decide-the-treatment-route-before-collecting) needs:

- an intact unsealed heat-safe cooking pot that the heat source permits;
- a lawful heat source and correct fuel that you can operate exactly as instructed;
- a stable non-combustible place where the pot can cool safely; and
- a clean cup or clean closable drinking-water container when water must be served or stored.

A separate untreated collector is useful when it lets you collect from safe ground, but it is not mandatory. A thermometer, transfer ladle, specially preserved dry rim and separate cup for every person are also not required. Those extra project controls were withdrawn because they made the only route impractical without authority validation.

Keep raw-water equipment away from the inside, opening, cap, threads and rim of anything used after treatment. Wash and dry drinking vessels before departure, keep them closed or wrapped, use cooled water promptly when practical, and keep stored water closed. Never use a vessel that held fuel, coolant, pesticide, detergent or another chemical.

The heat source remains a separate legal and fire-safety decision. Chapter 8 currently applies a conservative 10 L or connected-hose gate to this guide's provisional carried-stove route. That is not part of the microbial treatment method and is not presented as a universal legal minimum for every non-Total-Fire-Ban stove use. Current land rules and the exact appliance instructions control. Campfire boiling remains unavailable because this edition has not validated an exact authorised grate, pot support and sustained-boil handling cycle.

The simplest permitted campfire route also needs more than a match. It requires current permission, a currently authorised fireplace, separate deliverable extinguishing water, a non-combustible turning tool, permitted dry fuel, continuous capable supervision and a safe exit. This guide supplies no dependable zero-equipment way to light a fire or make suspect water safe. Carry and practise the complete system instead of assuming the bush will provide the missing part.

**Evidence and limits:** See [Water](07-water.md), [Fire](08-fire.md), S-203–S-221, S-288–S-290, S-454, S-457, S-458 and S-486–S-487. These sources support the hazard limits and rolling-boil/cool/use principle; they do not validate every portable handling step or the campfire sequence.

## First-aid supplies must match the pages

A broadly equipped kit may include gloves, a breathing barrier, absorbent and non-stick dressings, broad elastic bandages, triangular bandages, tape, shears, saline, an eye shield, suitable splinting material, an emergency wrap and a pencil/notepad.

Add personal medicines and current action plans separately. Adrenaline, asthma relievers, diabetes treatment and other prescribed emergency medicines must match the person and device. Storage conditions and expiry dates matter.

Tourniquets and specialist dressings require the relevant training and instructions. More equipment does not make a novice capable of using it. Do not add medicines or improvised procedures merely to make the kit look comprehensive.

See the [first-aid kit register](../../FIRST_AID_KIT_REGISTER.md) for the detailed research status, and [First aid](06-first-aid-and-evacuation.md) for what is possible without supplies.

## Leave a usable trip plan

Give a reliable person the area, route or operating location, people, vehicle details, expected contact/return time, medical issues relevant to rescue and emergency devices carried. Agree what they will do if you are overdue.

Update that person when plans change and when you return. A note stored on your own phone is not a contact plan for someone who cannot access it.

For a borrowed beacon, check registration and trip/contact details. Use the manufacturer's self-test only as directed. **Do not test the live distress/SOS function by creating a false emergency.**

**Sources:** Victoria Police S-021, [Bushwalking Victoria trip contacts](https://bushwalkingmanual.org.au/trip-planning/trip-emergency-contact/), [AMSA beacon registration](https://beacons.amsa.gov.au/registration/index.asp), S-224–S-226.

## Practise where mistakes are harmless

Before relying on equipment:

- Retrieve coordinates and the correct map without mobile data.
- Locate the device's emergency instructions without triggering SOS.
- Set up shelter in benign conditions and identify wind/rain entry.
- Find first-aid supplies in poor light and practise techniques through appropriate training.
- Confirm charging cables, spare batteries and device storage instructions.
- Demonstrate the treatment steps for the exact water system without drinking test water from a suspect source.

These are proposed checks, not tests already performed by this project. Keep a short record of what failed and correct it before treating the system as ready.

## Check the actual departure conditions

Look up current warnings, closures, fire restrictions, local water information and weather for the area—not just a nearby town. Reconsider the trip if the conditions exceed your equipment or skills.

Do not let a new book, beacon or knife justify entering conditions you would otherwise avoid. The guide is a backup to preparation, not a reason to need rescue.
