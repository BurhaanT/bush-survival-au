# How to use this guide

## If this is happening now

1. Get away from immediate danger only as far as needed.
2. For severe bleeding, abnormal breathing or serious illness, use [First aid](06-first-aid-and-evacuation.md) and call **000**.
3. If you are lost, stop unnecessary movement and use [The first minutes](02-first-minutes.md).
4. Try to get help with [Emergency communication](03-emergency-communication.md).
5. Protect people from cold, heat, rain, wind and the ground.

Do not read the book from the beginning. Use the contents, follow the numbered actions, then read the detail that applies to your situation.

## Read each section in this order

- **Do this now:** the next safe actions.
- **If/then:** the branch that matches what you see.
- **With a kit / without a kit:** different equipment, where it changes the action.
- **Do not:** actions likely to make the emergency worse.
- **Check:** what to watch while you wait or work.

When emergency services can communicate with you, describe the real conditions and follow their directions. Repeat back anything unclear and report changes.

## What a warning means

> **WARNING — REVIEW OUTSTANDING**
>
> A warning appears before consequential advice that still needs specialist or practical review. It states the evidence confidence, what is uncertain and the safest supported limit.

**High** means the core wording agrees with current authoritative guidance. **Moderate** means the principle is supported but its use here involves judgement. **Low** means the evidence is indirect or incomplete. **Insufficient** means this guide withholds a dependable procedure.

An **estimated percentage confidence in wording accuracy** is a conservative editorial judgement, not a measured result. It is never the chance that treatment works, a plant is correctly identified, an action is safe or someone survives. Sources and full review notes are for checking before a trip; they are not extra emergency steps.

There is no fixed “rule of threes”. Deal first with whatever can cause serious harm soonest, then keep communication, protection, water and care going together.
