# Stay or move

> **WARNING — SPECIALIST REVIEW OUTSTANDING**
>
> **Evidence confidence: High for staying findable after seeking rescue; Moderate for a novice's judgement about immediate danger or retracing.**
>
> **Uncertainty that matters:** there is no defensible universal distance, time limit or bearing that makes a walkout safe. This chapter does not authorise a river crossing, steep descent, fire-front escape or an injured person's self-evacuation. Those can create a worse emergency.

## Separate three different decisions

**Stopping to work out your position** is not the same as committing to wait for rescue. **Moving a few steps out of immediate danger** is not the same as walking out. **Following a responder's agreed route** is not the same as guessing where a road might be.

Name which decision you are making before anyone starts moving.

## You have tried to get help or reported a position

Do not treat a position fix, queued message, sent icon, automated acknowledgement or reply from a friend as proof that emergency services have your full details or that rescuers are coming. Use the status ladder in [Emergency communication](03-emergency-communication.md#know-what-the-device-status-really-means).

After you have tried to get help, activated a beacon or reported a position, stay at that known or reported place unless it becomes immediately dangerous or a responding service directs otherwise. A known position gives searchers something to work towards. Wandering changes that target.

Remain together. Choose nearby shelter and signals without turning the group into separate search parties. If shelter is not exactly at your reported point, tell rescuers where you actually are.

If you must leave immediate danger, take the communication device and essential protection if you can reach them without delaying escape. Tell responders the new position as soon as possible. A note at the old location may help if leaving one is straightforward, but do not remain in danger to write it.

**Sources:** [Victoria Police](https://www.police.vic.gov.au/outdoor-and-bush-safety), [Bushwalking Victoria](https://bushwalkingmanual.org.au/emergencies/lost/) and [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/articles/gtgemergencyplan.htm); S-021, S-052, S-056. The last source is an independent international cross-check, not Victorian operational authority.

## You have only just become uncertain

Stop and compare the map, signs and last certain point. Ask someone else to check your interpretation. A familiar-looking animal track, gully or clearing is not enough to establish location.

A short retrace is only a candidate when you genuinely recognise the route back and can follow it without creating a new hazard. Do not retrace into rising water, a steep scramble, darkness beyond your ability, fire or conditions the group cannot tolerate. Do not use “I think it was that way” as confirmation.

If you cannot establish a dependable return route, stop attempting to solve uncertainty by walking further. Seek help and prepare to wait. The book deliberately gives no invented “walk for ten minutes” rule.

## Someone is missing

Stop the remaining group spreading out. Work out who is missing, where and when they were last definitely seen, and what they were carrying. Call 000 for a missing-person emergency and ask for Police.

Give the person's clothing, age, relevant medical needs, last known position and any agreed destination. Keep the rest of the group accounted for. Do not launch an improvised off-track search that could lose more people.

> **WARNING — SEPARATION EXCEPTIONS NOT SETTLED**
>
> **Evidence confidence: Insufficient for a universal messenger or search-party procedure.** Sources give different advice for groups with no working communication. Terrain, injury, supervision and the chance of being found change the risk. This edition cannot safely tell every lone helper to abandon an injured person or every group to split.

## A vehicle or fixed shelter is available

A vehicle can make you easier to describe and can contain valuable protection, water and equipment. That does not make it safe in floodwater, under unstable trees, in extreme heat or in a bushfire.

Tell responders whether you are with the vehicle and whether it is accessible. Do not leave it merely because a road looks close on a small map. Do not run an engine or combustion appliance in an enclosed or poorly ventilated space to make a shelter warmer.

## Waiting is active work

Use the time to improve insulation, preserve clean water, make your position visible and record the person's condition. Listen for calls and answer signals. Keep track of the last successful contact.

Do not set an arbitrary rescue deadline and decide you must leave when it expires. Weather, terrain and other incidents can delay rescue. Report deteriorating conditions through any working contact rather than assuming the search has ended.
