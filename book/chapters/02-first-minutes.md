# The first minutes

>

>
> **Limit:** there is no fixed order that overrides an immediate danger or critical injury. Use the medical page at once when needed; do not finish an inventory before helping someone.

This card is a routing aid, not a countdown. Its blocks make the page easier to scan; they do not override current danger, lifesaving care or live instructions from Triple Zero and responders.

## 1. Stop making the situation worse

If you are unsure where you are, stop moving while you think. Put equipment down where it will not roll or blow away. Bring the group within speaking distance, provided doing so does not expose anyone to danger.

Say plainly: “We are stopping to work out what is wrong.” Ask each person what they noticed. Do not spend the first useful minutes deciding who is to blame.

Look around before sitting down. Fire, rising water, traffic, unstable ground and falling branches can make the present position dangerous. Get away from an immediate threat only as far as necessary to reduce that exposure. Do not convert a short move out of danger into an unplanned walk through unknown country.

## 2. Check the people

Ask whether anyone is injured, having trouble breathing, unusually confused, faint, very cold or very hot.

If there is **obvious life-threatening bleeding**, apply firm pressure promptly and get emergency help. Current ANZCOR bleeding guidance gives that bleeding priority over airway and breathing interventions. If someone else can help, have them call while you control it.

For an unresponsive person, follow [the emergency first-aid sequence](06-first-aid-and-evacuation.md#unresponsive-or-not-breathing-normally). Occasional gasps are not normal breathing. Do not delay CPR to search for a pulse, unpack the whole kit or complete the rest of this chapter.

For any other serious illness, injury or breathing difficulty, call **000**, ask for **Ambulance**, and use the matching action in [First aid and evacuation](06-first-aid-and-evacuation.md). With or without a first-aid kit, do not delay urgent care while searching for equipment.

**Source check:** [ANZCOR bleeding guidance](https://www.anzcor.org/home/first-aid/guideline-9-1-1-first-aid-for-management-of-bleeding), [ANZCOR CPR guidance](https://www.anzcor.org/home/basic-life-support/guideline-8-cardiopulmonary-resuscitation-cpr), corroborated by St John first-aid guidance; S-072–S-078.

## 3. Start getting help

If you are unable to find your way back and need rescue help, call **000** and ask for **Police**. For a medical emergency ask for **Ambulance**. Describe both problems if someone is ill and you are also lost.

Do not wait for darkness or a complete equipment failure before reporting a rescue emergency. You do not need perfect coordinates to make the first call: explain what you know and what you do not know.

Use [Emergency communication](03-emergency-communication.md) for the call, location details and alternatives when a phone cannot connect.

**Source check:** [Victoria Police](https://www.police.vic.gov.au/outdoor-and-bush-safety), [ACMA](https://www.acma.gov.au/emergency-calls) and [Bushwalking Victoria](https://bushwalkingmanual.org.au/emergencies/lost/); S-021, S-043, S-052.

## 4. Make a short written picture of the situation

Once urgent care and the first attempt to get help are underway, use a notebook or phone note:

| Write down | Make it concrete |
|---|---|
| People | Names, number present, injuries, medicines that may be needed |
| Position | Current displayed coordinates, last certain place, what is uncertain |
| Time | When trouble began, when you last knew your position, remaining daylight as observed or shown by a reliable device |
| Protection | Dry clothing, rain cover, shade, sleeping insulation, usable shelter |
| Supplies | Drinking water actually present, ready-to-eat food, battery levels |
| Contact | Who has been called, whether they answered, directions received |
| Changes | Worsening weather, water rising, smoke, increasing confusion or pain |

This table is an original organising aid. It is not a diagnostic score. A blank entry means “unknown”, not “safe”.

## 5. Prepare to wait

Protect people from wind, rain, cold ground or sun using [Temperature and shelter](05-temperature-and-shelter.md). Put bright equipment where it can be seen without abandoning shelter or moving into danger. Keep the communication device accessible.

Share jobs when there are enough capable people: one can speak to rescuers, another provide care, another prepare shelter. Tell everyone what is happening. Keep children with a responsible adult. Do not send people wandering in different directions looking for a track.

## 6. Check again whenever something changes

Ask: Is everyone still responsive and breathing normally? Is bleeding controlled? Is anyone getting colder, hotter or less able to think? Is the waiting place becoming dangerous? Have rescuers received the new information?

A delay in rescue is a reason to improve protection and keep checking, not proof that the alert failed. Do not assume “no helicopter yet” means “we must walk out”.
