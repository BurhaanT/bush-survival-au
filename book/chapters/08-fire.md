# Fire

> **WARNING — FIRE-SAFETY REVIEW OUTSTANDING**
>
> **Estimated confidence in the accuracy of this chapter's wording: 95% for the cited Victorian restrictions and avoidance principles; 75% for the untested small-fire sequence; 90% for the warning that friction fire is not a dependable emergency fallback.** These are conservative editorial estimates, not legal advice, odds of success or field-test results. No responsible success-rate estimate is available for an untrained person's friction fire.
>
> Fire can remove every other survival option. This chapter separates **escaping a bushfire** from **choosing to light a small fire**. Permission to camp is not permission to light a fire, and a book cannot confirm today's conditions.

## Do this now

1. **Can you see or smell smoke, see fire, or receive a fire warning for this area?** If yes or unknown, do **not** light anything. Check the current warning if possible, call 000 for immediate danger and follow emergency-service instructions. Use [Smoke or an unexpected fire](#smoke-or-an-unexpected-fire).
2. **Is there no fire threat, and are you considering lighting a stove or campfire?** Every live rule, site, weather, equipment, water, supervision and exit gate in this chapter must be **yes**. One **no or unknown** means no fire.
3. **If fire is unavailable, change systems:**
   - communicate with 000, satellite SOS or a PLB as appropriate;
   - signal with a whistle, torch, mirror or bright material—not a signal fire;
   - use shelter, ground insulation, wind protection and dry layers;
   - use carried safe water and ready-to-eat food; and
   - keep a torch or headlamp for light.

Fire is optional. Rescue, exposure protection, drinking water and signalling must not depend on it. This guide has no dependable zero-equipment ignition method.

## Smoke or an unexpected fire

Stop and check the current warning for the actual area if you can. Read the action statement and issue time, not just the coloured symbol. Report an immediate fire emergency to **000**.

Follow emergency-service instructions and leave a fire-risk area early when a safe departure is still possible. Do not drive or walk towards smoke to investigate. Do not assume an empty warning map means there is no fire.

If the route is threatened, do not invent an escape bearing from wind direction, slope, smoke colour or the apparent edge of a map polygon. Tell 000 where you are, what is blocking you and what shelter is actually nearby.

**Sources:** [CFA fire danger ratings](https://www.cfa.vic.gov.au/warnings-restrictions/fire-bans-ratings-and-restrictions/about-fire-danger-ratings), [VicEmergency](https://www.emergency.vic.gov.au/), Parks Victoria S-220/S-299; S-291–S-295. Live warnings are separate from this dated manuscript.

## Trapped in a car by smoke or flames

> **WARNING — LAST RESORT, NOT A SAFE PLAN**
>
> **Estimated confidence in the accuracy of this wording: 90% for actions shared by current CFA and FRV guidance; 60% for engine, fan and air-conditioning timing and the after-front exit judgement because the current official pages do not state one identical sequence.** These are subjective editorial estimates, not probabilities of safety or survival. A vehicle is not a fire refuge. Do not choose to remain in a fire-risk area because this section exists.
>
> CFA says a car is a last resort and the chance of survival can still be low. Leaving early is safer. No sound, elapsed time or single temperature sensation proves that it is safe to get out.

If you encounter smoke or flames:

1. **Turn back and drive away only while that is still safe.** Do not drive into or through smoke or flames. If you cannot drive away and a building is very close and safely reachable, CFA says a building is likely to protect better than a car.
2. **If the car is your only reachable shelter, park in the best clear place you can reach without driving farther into danger.** Get off the roadway. Keep away from bushes, trees and other vehicles. Prefer gravel, an oval, a closely grazed or already-burnt paddock, or the far side of a brick wall or rocky outcrop. Face the oncoming fire front only if you can do so without a dangerous manoeuvre.
3. **Make the stopped car visible.** Turn on the headlights and hazard lights. Call **000** if you can do so without delaying the protective actions below. Give the exact location and say that people are trapped in a vehicle by bushfire.
4. **Close up the passenger space.** Close every door and window. Close the air vents and turn off the cabin fan. **When the fire is close, turn the engine off.** Do not restart the engine or ventilation while the fire front passes.
5. **Get low and cover.** Everyone moves below window level, as close to the floor as practical. Cover completely with a **dry pure-wool blanket** if available. Do not wet the blanket: extreme heat can turn its water to steam and cause burns.
6. **Reduce smoke exposure without trusting a mask.** A P2 mask or cloth may reduce some particles, but it does not remove carbon monoxide or other fire gases and does not make the air safe. Drink carried water only if doing so does not delay getting low and covered.
7. **Stay low and covered while the front passes.** Expect extreme heat, smoke, embers, broken glass and burning or popping vehicle parts. If the inside catches fire or staying becomes immediately life-threatening, this guide cannot name one exit route that is safe in every setting; keep 000 updated if contact remains possible.
8. **After the front passes, test before uncovering.** CFA advises raising a hand under the blanket and feeling the air. If it feels hotter than badly sunburned skin, remain low and covered. When it is safe enough to leave, stay under the dry blanket and move to safer already-burnt ground while checking for flame, embers, falling trees and traffic. Call 000 if this has not already been done.

**Unresolved official-source difference:** CFA's dedicated 22 September 2026 car page gives the close-front engine trigger. CFA's 17 September summary and FRV's 15 July page put engine shutdown earlier. The current dedicated page gives no separate air-conditioning instruction. This draft follows the newer, more specific CFA car page for the engine trigger and does not invent an air-conditioning step; CFA/FRV confirmation is still required.

This is not permission to drive into vegetation or continue searching for a perfect clearing through smoke. It is a bounded last-resort sequence for an ordinary enclosed car, not a caravan, open vehicle, damaged vehicle or firefighting appliance.

**Sources:** [CFA — Sheltering in a car](https://www.cfa.vic.gov.au/fire-safety/your-fire-plan/last-resort-options/sheltering-in-a-car), [CFA — Last resort options](https://www.cfa.vic.gov.au/fire-safety/your-fire-plan/last-resort-options), [CFA — Staying safe while travelling](https://www.cfa.vic.gov.au/fire-safety/your-fire-plan/make-your-fire-plan/staying-safe-while-travelling), [FRV — Plan and prepare](https://www.frv.vic.gov.au/plan-and-prepare), Victorian Health smoke limits S-294 and the dated comparison S-488–S-489.

## On foot, with fire upon you and no safer refuge

> **WARNING — LAST RESORT ONLY; FIRE AND AQUATIC REVIEW OUTSTANDING**
>
> **Estimated confidence in the accuracy of this wording: 95% that it reflects current CFA last-resort principles; 65% for its completeness across an unknown fire, route, terrain and body of water.** These are subjective editorial estimates—not a probability of safety or survival. Every option below can still cause serious injury or death. Leaving a fire-risk area early is safer than relying on this section.

If fire is already upon you and you cannot reach a safer planned shelter or leave safely:

1. **Call 000 now.** Say that you are caught in a bushfire. Give your location, what is around you and what is blocking you. Follow the call-taker. If no phone or two-way satellite contact is possible, activate a PLB or satellite SOS as its instructions direct.
2. **Do not try to outrun the flames.** Do not run through flames, enter an enclosed drain, climb into a tank or light a counter-fire.
3. **Reduce exposure to flame and radiant heat.** If you can move without crossing flames or another immediate hazard, CFA advises going to lower ground rather than uphill and putting as much distance as possible between you, the fire and vegetation.
4. **Use the best shielding or low-fuel space you can actually reach.** Current CFA examples include a cleared area or rocky outcrop, a ploughed or eaten-out paddock, a reserve, ground that has already burnt, or the far side of a solid concrete tank, brick wall or boulder. Keep reassessing; none is guaranteed safe.
5. **Cover exposed skin.** Keep sturdy shoes on. Use long natural-fibre clothing and a dry pure-wool blanket if carried. A dry cloth or mask may reduce some particles but does not protect against carbon monoxide or make smoke safe to breathe. Do not use a wet cloth: CFA warns that heated water may become steam and burn skin.
6. **After the fire front passes, keep watching for heat, flame, embers and spot fires.** Move onto burnt ground only when it is actually reachable without crossing a new immediate hazard. Keep following 000 or emergency-service directions.

### A body of water is not an automatic answer

CFA lists a beach, swimming pool, dam or river as a possible last resort only when fire is upon you and no other shelter is available. Entering water can add drowning, cold, current, unstable-bank, submerged-object and difficult-exit hazards. This guide does **not** give a universal instruction to enter, wade, swim across, submerge in or remain in water. Do not climb into a water tank. Choose between the options that are actually present with 000 if contact is possible; do not leave secure land merely because water appears on CFA's example list.

**Sources:** [CFA camping and outdoor recreation](https://www.cfa.vic.gov.au/fire-safety/your-fire-plan/make-your-fire-plan/camping-and-outdoor-recreation) and [CFA last-resort options](https://www.cfa.vic.gov.au/fire-safety/your-fire-plan/last-resort-options), S-484–S-485. Both were checked 29 September 2026. This is a plain-language synthesis, not CFA approval of the book or a route chosen for the reader's location.

## Before lighting anything

Go straight to the [conservative go/no-go rule](#the-books-conservative-gono-go-rule). Every gate must be **yes** before ignition. If permission, place, weather, control or need is unknown, use the no-fire actions at the start of this chapter.

Use your exact location to identify the responsible organisation—such as Parks Victoria, Forest Fire Management Victoria, a council or a private owner—and the fire district. Check the current Total Fire Ban, municipal Fire Danger Period, any separately declared period when fires are prohibited on that public land, park or forest closure, local rule and every site sign. A remembered rule or yesterday's screenshot is not a current check.

Catastrophic Fire Danger Rating days can trigger closures of parks and state forests in the affected district. Check the live closure for the exact place. If you are already stranded, contact 000 and follow current directions. Do not begin an unsafe dash merely because a closure applies.

On a Total Fire Ban day: **NO FIRE IN THIS GUIDE.** Put out any existing fire before the ban begins. Do not light a campfire or use a solid-fuel or liquid-fuel cooking fire.

Victorian law contains a narrow exception for some gas or electric appliances used solely to prepare a meal. This personal guide does not tell the reader to use that exception. It does not treat water treatment, warming, drying or signalling as meal preparation. The authorised CFA Act and a current CFA page use a **3 m** clearance for the exception, measured from the appliance's outer perimeter, while a 2026 Conservation Regulator brochure says **1.5 m**. That conflict is recorded for specialist review; it is not a reason to light an appliance on a Total Fire Ban day from this guide.

**Sources:** [CFA Can I or Can't I?](https://www.cfa.vic.gov.au/warnings-restrictions/fire-bans-ratings-and-restrictions/can-i-or-cant-i), [Victorian campfire rules](https://www.vic.gov.au/campfires-stoves-and-bbqs), [Parks Victoria campfire safety](https://www.parks.vic.gov.au/get-into-nature/safety-in-nature/visitor-safety-tips-in-parks/campfire-safety); S-215–S-221, S-290. This is a conservative summary, not legal advice or an emergency exemption.

### Before using a carried stove to boil water

>
> **Estimated confidence in the accuracy of these checks: 90% for the fail-closed safety principles; 70% for completeness across unknown stove designs and land rules.** These are editorial estimates, not a probability of safe use. The exact current manual, current fire rules and an undamaged compatible stove control. This edition has not selected or tested a stove.
>
> The 10 L step below is this guide's **conservative provisional rule**, not a claim that one statewide law imposes 10 L on every non-Total-Fire-Ban stove use. The current State-forest page tells stove users to follow regular campfire safety rules; some park and site rules expressly require 10 L; and the Total Fire Ban meal-appliance exception expressly requires 10 L or a connected hose. Written CFA/Parks Victoria/DEECA clarification for treatment-only boiling is still needed. On a Total Fire Ban day this guide allows no stove use.

Use a carried stove for the water chapter only when **every** item below is yes:

1. It is not a Total Fire Ban day, and current rules, closures and signs permit that exact stove and purpose at that exact place.
2. You can identify the stove and correct fuel, have its current instructions, and know that neither is damaged, leaking, recalled or incorrectly connected.
3. The stove will remain outdoors in open air on a stable, level surface that cannot burn. It is outside every tent, vehicle, caravan and enclosed or partly enclosed shelter.
4. Every clearance in the stove instructions and the current rule can be met. Dry vegetation, spare fuel, shelter fabric and the exit are outside those clearances.
5. The pot is within the stove's stated size and weight limits and will remain stable through boiling and removal.
6. At least 10 L of separate extinguishing water is already deliverable beside the stove, or an immediately usable hose is connected. If this would consume drinking water you need, do not use the stove.
7. A capable adult can watch it continuously, and a safe exit remains usable.
8. You can light, operate, shut down and let the exact stove cool by following its instructions without improvising.

Any **no or unknown** answer means the stove route is unavailable. This book does not give fuel-connection or ignition steps for an unnamed stove. Never improvise a windscreen around a fuel canister, change fuel, repair a leak in use or bring the stove into shelter.

## The book's conservative go/no-go rule

> **WARNING — FIRE GO/NO-GO SCREEN AWAITS OPERATIONAL REVIEW**
>
> **Estimated confidence in the accuracy of the screen: 95% that the numbered limits match the cited CFA and Victorian rules as checked 8 September 2026; 75% for the completeness of its untested decision wording.** These are editorial estimates, not a probability of safety or correct use under stress. Current declarations, closures, land rules and signs control. A Victorian fire authority or responsible land organisation has not approved this adaptation.

Some general state-forest rules allow other arrangements. This personal guide uses a narrower rule because a stranded reader should not have to design a fireplace under pressure. Confirm these ten gates in this order:

1. **No nearby fire threat:** no smoke, visible fire or fire warning is nearby. If one is present, use the unexpected-fire section.
2. **Moderate rating and explicit current permission:** the Fire Danger Rating for the exact district is confirmed as **Moderate**, and the current Total Fire Ban, municipal Fire Danger Period, any public-land period when fires are prohibited, closure, responsible-organisation rule and site sign checks all permit this exact fire. **High, Extreme, Catastrophic, No Rating, unavailable information or any uncertainty means no fire in this guide.** The absence of a ban is not permission.
3. **A currently authorised fireplace:** use a supplied public fireplace, or another fireplace that the current rule for that exact site specifically authorises. An abandoned rock ring, old fire scar or improvised pit does not qualify. A commercially made portable firepit may be used only where the current rule expressly permits it. In national and state parks, follow the supplied-public-fireplace rule. Do not dig a trench or construct a rock fireplace from this book.
4. **Low, steady wind:** for this guide, wind must be below **10 km/h** every time and must not be increasing. CFA says moving small branches indicates wind above that limit. Increasing wind, moving embers or doubt means no fire.
5. **A safe site and full clear area:** measure at least **3 m from the fire's outer edge in every direction and from its highest point upward**. Keep that whole ground and air space clear of anything that can burn, including tents, prepared wood, spare fuel and gas bottles. Reject deep leaf litter or rotting plant matter, exposed roots or organic soil, a slope that could carry burning material, dangerous limbs above, and any likely ember path towards vegetation, shelter, fuel or the exit. Keep the fire no larger than **1 m × 1 m**; this is a ceiling, not a target. A stricter current rule wins.
6. **Usable extinguishing water:** at least **10 L** is already beside the fireplace in one or more usable vessels, or an immediately usable hose is connected. A nearby stream, lake or tank without a way to deliver its water does not count. Ten litres is a minimum condition, not a promise that it will control every fire. If the water is unavailable or would consume drinking water you need, do not light the fire.
7. **A turning tool:** a suitable non-combustible tool for turning wet fuel and exposing hidden heat is beside the water. Without it, do not light the fire.
8. **Continuous capable supervision:** an adult can remain awake, alert, able to intervene and in uninterrupted sight of the fire. Injury, cold or another urgent task that prevents this means no fire.
9. **A genuine need:** shelter, insulation, ready-to-eat food and non-fire signalling will not solve the need.
10. **A safe exit:** an exit away from the fireplace and surrounding hazards is available and remains usable.

If the fire spreads or the exit becomes threatened, move away from danger first. Call **000** as soon as you can. If there is no phone connection, activate satellite SOS or a PLB as its instructions direct. Do not delay escape to complete the extinguishment sequence. If any other gate becomes no or unknown while the fire remains contained and the exit remains safe, stop adding fuel and put the fire out immediately.

### “Minimal equipment” still includes fire-control equipment

A fire is never a safe **no-equipment** method in this guide. The simplest route this edition describes uses a carried lighter or waterproof match and a labelled solid campfire firelighter, but those items only provide ignition. The go/no-go rule still requires a currently authorised fireplace, at least 10 L of separate extinguishing water already in usable vessels or an immediately usable hose, a suitable non-combustible turning tool, currently permitted fuel, a capable watcher and a safe exit. If any part is missing, use the no-fire plan.

Hand-drill and bow-drill fire are sometimes described as “no-equipment” techniques. That description hides the selected materials, manufactured parts, preparation, practice, dry tinder, time and effort they require. They are recorded below as cultural and technical context, not as a dependable emergency instruction.

## Use a carried ignition only after every condition passes

> **WARNING — THIS PORTABLE SEQUENCE HAS NOT PASSED A LIVE-FIRE TEST**
>
> **Estimated confidence in the accuracy of this guidance: 95% for the preparation and control principles; 75% for the untested exact sequence below.** These are editorial estimates, not a probability of ignition or control. Use it only after every go/no-go condition above passes. A carried lighter or waterproof match is the preferred low-burden ignition; “primitive” does not mean easier.

1. Put at least 10 L of separate extinguishing water in usable vessels, or an immediately usable connected hose, and a suitable non-combustible turning tool within reach.
2. Use only the supplied public fireplace or the exact fireplace specifically authorised by the current site rule.
3. Use only dry, untreated wood that was lawfully obtained **and** that the current rule for this exact place permits. Bringing wood from home does not by itself make it permitted. Do not cut or strip a standing plant.
4. Before making a flame, read the current label on the commercial **solid campfire firelighter product**. Use this book's pictured build only when that label permits the stated amount, placing the firelighter on the fireplace base, arranging match-thin kindling loosely over and around it, and lighting it with the carried lighter or waterproof match. If the label is missing, differs or does not cover this use, the pictured route is unavailable.
5. Prepare dry, permitted kindling in three piles. The working sizes are: no thicker than a wooden match; about pencil-thick; and about thumb-thick. Each pile should make a loose bundle about as wide as a drink can, and every piece must fit fully inside the fireplace. Keep all three piles outside the 3 m clear area until each is needed. These common-object sizes are a working synthesis, not a Victorian legal measurement.
6. Only after the label check in step 4 passes, put the solid firelighter on the fireplace base and loosely lean the match-thin pieces over and around it, leaving visible gaps for air. Keep the pencil- and thumb-thick piles ready but outside the clear area.
7. Light the firelighter with the carried lighter or waterproof match, exactly as the firelighter label directs. Keep your face, hair, sleeves, hands, shelter fabric and every fuel container away.
8. When the match-thin pieces are burning on their own, add pencil-thick pieces a few at a time without crushing the air gaps. When those burn on their own, add thumb-thick pieces slowly. Do not add a thicker piece until the smaller stage is burning steadily.
9. Add only the small amount needed for the immediate purpose. Keep every part of the fire inside the authorised fireplace and far below the one-metre ceiling.
10. If a prepared stage goes out, moving embers appear or control becomes doubtful, stop adding fuel and use the extinguishment sequence. Do not keep striking matches into a smoky pile.
11. Never pour, spray or discharge petrol, diesel, kerosene, methylated spirits, aerosol, gas, liquid firelighter or stove fuel onto a campfire. Use an appliance fuel only inside its exact appliance and as its manual directs.

**Method cross-check:** Parks Victoria tells campers to bring firewood, kindling, a fire starter and waterproof matches or a lighter. A US Army survival manual and two government outdoor-fire guides independently use the same small-to-larger kindling progression and the need for air gaps. Their dimensions are technique evidence only; Victorian rules and the conservative ten-gate screen control. See S-455. This exact portable sequence has not been live-fire tested for this book.

## A permitted fire still needs an exit plan

Use only the currently authorised fireplace and fuel, with the required clear area, site checks and deliverable extinguishing water already present. Keep the fire far below the maximum size needed for control and continuously attended by a capable adult. Stop adding fuel well before departure. Do not leave it burning while you sleep.

For a stove, use the correct fuel and the exact manual. Keep it outside, stable and away from shelter fabric. Never improvise fuel, enclose a gas canister in a windscreen or use a damaged or leaking appliance.

Never use a stove, charcoal, lantern or other fuel-burning appliance inside a tent, vehicle, caravan, or enclosed or partly enclosed shelter. Opening a flap or window does not make this safe. Use it outdoors and exactly as its manufacturer directs.

## Put a small wood campfire completely out

> **WARNING — SMALL WOOD CAMPFIRE ONLY; SEQUENCE AWAITS LIVE-FIRE REVIEW**
>
> **Estimated confidence in the accuracy of this guidance: 95% for water-not-soil and cold-before-leaving; 80% for this untested exact sequence.** These are editorial estimates, not proof that hidden heat is gone. Ten litres is a minimum condition, not a guaranteed extinguishing dose. Keep an escape path and call 000 if the fire escapes control.
>
> The water-turn-check sequence below applies only to the small permitted wood campfire described in this chapter. Do **not** apply it to a leaking or burning stove, gas or pressurised canister, liquid fuel, electrical item or cooking fat. Move away, call 000, warn others and follow the exact product or emergency-service directions. Do not generalise vegetation-fire water instructions to those hazards.

At any point, if the fire spreads or the exit becomes threatened, move away from danger first. Call **000** as soon as you can. If there is no phone connection, activate satellite SOS or a PLB as its instructions direct. Do not delay escape to finish these steps.

1. Stop adding fuel well before sleep, departure or another task.
2. Use a suitable tool to spread and turn material inside the fireplace without scattering it.
3. Pour water slowly over every coal and partly burned piece. Keep clear of steam. Stir and turn the wet material, then add more water.
4. Look and listen for smoke, steam or hissing. Without touching ash or coal, hold a hand near every turned part to check for heat.
5. **If heat, smoke, steam or hissing remains:** check that enough water remains in the usable vessels or connected hose. If it does, add water, turn and check again. Continue this loop.
6. **If the water is insufficient while any heat or other sign remains:** remain only while it is safe. If the fire spreads, the exit is threatened or any other danger develops, move away first; then call 000 or activate satellite SOS or a PLB as instructed. If remaining there is safe, do not leave the fire and do not cover it with soil or dirt. Call 000 and say that you cannot completely extinguish it. If there is no phone connection, activate satellite SOS or a PLB. If no method connects, stay with the fire only while safe, keep people and burnable material away, and keep signalling for help. Do not scatter it or declare it out.
7. **If repeated turning and near-hand checks find no heat:** turn everything again and repeat the near-hand check at every part. Only after no nearby heat is felt, cautiously touch the **wet ash and visibly unburnt wood** to confirm they are cool. Never touch a coal, charred material or anything that may still be hot.
8. Any warmth or doubt means the fire is not out. Return to the water-turn-check loop. If the water is insufficient, use step 6.
9. The fire is completely out only when the near-hand check finds no heat at any turned part, the wet ash and visibly unburnt wood are cool, and there is no smoke, steam or hissing. **Only then may you leave.**

Do not use soil or dirt to put out the fire. Hidden embers can remain hot and reignite.
