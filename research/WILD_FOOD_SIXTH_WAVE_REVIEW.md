# Wild-food sixth-wave review

**Date:** 8 September 2026  
**Scope:** Current Victorian samphire/glasswort taxonomy, nitre-bush, and safety audits of the existing common-nardoo and Bulbine-lily exclusions.  
**Review status:** Internal source, wording, image-rights and visual review. Chapter 11 now contains four non-actionable pages and IM-132–IM-143 are registered supporting views. No botanist, toxicologist, food-safety specialist, nutritionist, conservation/legal specialist, Traditional Owner organisation or untrained reader has approved the pages.

## Outcome first

This pass delivered four useful reader pages, but **no new emergency food instruction**:

1. Common nardoo is an illustrated danger page explaining why food history is not a novice detoxification method.
2. Samphire is a recognition/non-harvest page explaining the common-name, contamination and threatened-species traps.
3. Nitre-bush is an advanced-recognition page. It is the only candidate in this wave with a plausible future conditional ripe-fruit path, but that path is not open now.

4. Bulbine lily is a cultural/stewardship page. It remains unsuitable for emergency digging or preparation guidance.

Sources S-412–S-423, findings R-421–R-430 and queue items SR10-01–SR10-06 control this review. SP-031/SP-032 are new research records; SP-016/SP-017 are strengthened existing records.

## Confidence vocabulary

- **High:** strong agreement in appropriate authoritative sources for the precise proposition stated.
- **Moderate:** useful evidence exists, but provenance, scope or independent corroboration limits transfer.
- **Low:** weak or indirect evidence; not enough to support action.
- **Insufficient:** evidence needed for the claim or instruction was not located or is not usable in the required form.

These labels are editorial evidence-strength summaries, not probabilities that a specimen is safe to eat.

## Gate applied

Each candidate was tested against separate questions:

1. Does current VicFlora taxonomy identify the exact Victorian taxon and older names?
2. Can a stressed novice distinguish it from consequential local alternatives without tasting or destructive harvest?
3. Does separate evidence establish the exact food part and preparation rather than merely repeat an “edible” label?
4. Is there a defensible serving or toxicological boundary?
5. Does any nutrition figure match the exact species, part, fresh/cooked state, provenance and sample?
6. Can pollution, conservation status, tenure and harvest impact be managed without unsafe shortcuts?
7. Is Traditional Knowledge kept with the named people, Country, source and permission boundary?
8. Would a future image set show every decisive feature and limitation rather than create false confidence?

A subject may pass the usefulness test while failing the eating gate. That is the outcome for all four subjects here.

## Samphire/glasswort

### Identity, distribution and season

S-412 supports the current accepted name *Salicornia quinqueflora* and the common Victorian subsp. *quinqueflora*. Older sources often use *Sarcocornia quinqueflora*. It is common in coastal and estuarine saltmarsh and occurs occasionally at inland saline or brackish lakes. VicFlora records flowering mainly in autumn.

Subsp. *tasmanica* is not interchangeable: VicFlora treats its certain Victorian occurrence as limited to the Mt Martha landfill area and possibly introduced. *Salicornia blackiana* and a diverse Victorian *Tecticornia* flora defeat a generic segmented-stem shortcut. Kew places FSANZ-listed *Tecticornia lepidosperma* outside Victoria.

**Evidence strength:** High for taxonomy, range and the common-name ambiguity. Insufficient for novice picture-only identification.

### Food, taste and calories

FSANZ's July 2026 Record of Views lists *S. quinqueflora* and non-Victorian *T. lepidosperma* as non-traditional foods that are not novel. That regulatory novelty view does not establish specimen identity, a safe wild site, preparation or serving. The same entry says wild-harvested material requires monitoring for metal contaminants.

Secondary/regional sources report young fleshy shoots used raw, blanched or pickled and describe a salty or slightly peppery taste. This is culinary-history context only. Taste cannot identify a plant or clear pollution.

No defensible fresh Victorian *S. quinqueflora* kJ/kcal result was located. A peer-reviewed Kimberley study analysed freeze-dried material from different *Tecticornia* taxa and found site-variable composition and high dry-basis sodium. Those numbers must not be relabelled as fresh Victorian samphire.

**Evidence strength:** Moderate for broad culinary history; Insufficient for field preparation, serving, fresh energy or sodium.

### Conservation, law and contamination

S-414 records seven Victorian *Tecticornia* taxa as FFG Endangered. Suitable habitat can form part of the federally listed Subtropical and Temperate Coastal Saltmarsh ecological community, and beaded glasswort is feeding habitat for the orange-bellied parrot on the mainland. Parks, protected-flora and tenure rules remain separate gates.

Metals or chemical contamination cannot be diagnosed by colour, smell or taste and cannot be assumed removed by rinsing, blanching or ordinary cooking. Landfill, industrial, road, mine, sewage, farm-runoff or unknown treatment history is a stop condition.

**Decision confidence:** High for education-only/non-harvest. No generic samphire-harvest instruction should be written.

## Nitre-bush

### Identity, range and season

S-415 supports *Nitraria billardierei* as a rigid, often tangled shrub to about two metres, with hairy branchlets, clustered narrow leaves, small white flowers and oblong drupes containing one hard stone. VicFlora places it mainly on saline flats and floodplains in north-western Victoria, often on clay or gypsum-rich soil, with isolated Portland–Nelson limestone/dune occurrences. Flowering is recorded mainly September–December. An exact ripe-fruit season was not established in this pass.

VicFlora, PlantNET and Flora of Australia call the ripe fruit edible. That support applies to the ripe fruit; it does not establish that the hard stone, seed, unripe fruit, leaf or another part is edible.

**Evidence strength:** High for identity, Victorian distribution and the ripe-fruit food record; Insufficient for novice field action.

### Taste, calories and lookalike

A lower-tier WA source describes the flesh as light and slightly sweet; other informal descriptions vary. Taste is not identification. No defensible fresh-fruit kJ/kcal or realistic gathered-yield result was located.

African boxthorn, *Lycium ferocissimum*, is a consequential superficial lookalike. The source comparison in S-416 supports these plate requirements:

- nitre-bush hairy branchlets versus boxthorn's hairless young growth;
- nitre-bush narrow acute clustered leaves versus broader, more rounded boxthorn leaves;
- small white nitre-bush flowers versus tubular white/lilac, purple-marked boxthorn flowers;
- oblong nitre-bush drupe with one hard stone versus round orange-red boxthorn berry with many seeds.

These distinctions still require a botanist-approved matched Victorian plate. They are not a field key by themselves.

**Decision confidence:** High for education-only status. A future conditional ripe-fruit route remains possible only after botanical, food-safety, legal/conservation and novice no-consumption gates pass.

## Common nardoo

S-418 supports the exact taxon and the central danger: thiaminase I can destroy vitamin B1. Historical food use depended on sophisticated knowledge and processing. The reviewed institutional summaries do not provide a novice-validated endpoint showing that a field batch has been made safe.

The book must not reconstruct a detoxification recipe from fragments, imply that grinding or heating alone is enough, or offer an energy figure that makes experimentation appear worthwhile. A future page should show wet habit, four-part leaves and sporocarps, but only to support recognition and avoidance of improvised use.

Museums Victoria's **Dullum Dullum** record remains specifically Wemba Wemba context through Bunjilaka. It is not generic Victorian terminology, direct current permission or a survival recipe.

**Evidence strength:** High for the thiaminase hazard and non-action decision; Insufficient for safe field processing, serving and energy.

## Bulbine lily

S-417 keeps *Bulbine bulbosa* separate from the other four Victorian *Bulbine* species, including Endangered *B. crassa*. VicFlora now treats the former “south-east coastal” entity as a synonym of *B. crassa* rather than an unresolved taxon. Harvesting the underground food removes or severely damages the plant. Outside flowering, a novice faces a weak strap-leaved monocot identification problem; digging to expose an organ is not an acceptable way to resolve that uncertainty.

Bunjilaka records Woi wurrung **Pike** and says the underground part was probably cooked before eating. “Probably cooked” is valuable historical context but not a process specification. It gives no preparation time, temperature, endpoint, serving or residual-hazard test. The existing old 251 kJ/100 g raw-bulb row is not a safe cooked-food or realistic field-return value.

**Evidence strength:** High for the conservative non-harvest decision, Moderate for the bounded historical-food context and Insufficient for field preparation, serving and energy.

## Traditional Knowledge and ICIP

The Gunaikurnai and Victorian Government Lake Tyers joint plan records broad *Sarcocornia* species as important food plants on Krauatungalung Country. It does not establish the exact current *Salicornia* species or a preparation, so it remains a brief place-specific cultural record rather than an eating instruction. No suitable Victorian Traditional Owner-controlled species/preparation source was located for nitre-bush; none is invented or imported from another region.

The Bulbine and nardoo names and food history remain with the exact Bunjilaka Woi wurrung and Wemba Wemba contexts. DJAARA's separate public **bam, barm** Bulbine and **gitjawil dhurung** nardoo language records remain explicitly Dja Dja Wurrung. A museum or public corporation page is not an open reuse licence or authority to reconstruct omitted methods. Botanical and toxicological review remain separate even when cultural scope is authorised.

Before any wider distribution or expansion, the relevant Traditional Owner organisations or authorised knowledge holders must decide wording, attribution, ICIP scope, consent and benefit. Separate Nations' records must not be combined into generic “Aboriginal survival knowledge.”

## Image implementation and remaining gaps

S-419 preserves the first candidate screen. The implemented set uses twelve rights-recorded files under S-420 and IM-132–IM-143:

| Subject | Implemented supporting views | Why the set is still not final |
|---|---|---|
| Samphire | Three views: spreading habit, jointed close view and Ricketts Point Victorian context | No authenticated matched *Salicornia*/*Tecticornia* comparison, flower/seed macro, pollution test or useful scale |
| Nitre-bush | Flowering branch, fruiting shrub and a Victorian African-boxthorn flower/spine warning | No matched Victorian whole shrubs, cut one-stone versus many-seed fruit or complete measured plate |
| Bulbine lily | Population habit, flower and capsule | Non-Victorian, unvouchered views; no measured leaf base, diagnostic filament macro, mature seed or attached underground organ |
| Common nardoo | Mixed habit, four-part frond and sporocarps | No authenticated Victorian matched four-species set, reliable scale or one plant connecting every feature |

A final set would require:

- samphire: Victorian whole patch, green/red seasonal stems, segment/flower detail and matched local *Salicornia/Tecticornia* exclusions;
- nitre-bush: whole shrub, hairy branchlet, leaf, flower, ripe fruit, single stone and matched African boxthorn;
- nardoo: wet and dry habit, measured four-part leaf and sporocarps;
- Bulbine: flowering whole plant, leaf bases, flower/capsule and a cultivated/vouchered underground-organ view that does not encourage wild digging.

Every implemented file is recorded in `IMAGE_REGISTER.md` and the asset manifest with creator, URL, licence, attribution, locality, modifications and limits. Multiple photographs reduce omissions but do not authenticate the reader's specimen.

## Release decision

**Working recognition pages only; do not add field-eating instructions.**

The conservative decisions have High support. The action pathways have Insufficient evidence. No recipe, serving, taste test, destructive harvest or field identification is approved. The old raw-Bulbine calorie row is retained only as historical scale; fresh samphire and nitre-bush energy remains unknown and nardoo is not assigned an actionable value. The images are supporting working views, not approved plates. `FIELD_READY_BUILD=NO` remains controlling.
