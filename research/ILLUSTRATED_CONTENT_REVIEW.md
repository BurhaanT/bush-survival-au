## Food value and taste

The comparison distinguishes fresh food, dry material, historical values, commercial-food proxies and unavailable data. Warrigal greens are shown as a very low-energy vegetable. One Victorian-grown muntries sample has a current wet-basis energy result. Lilly pilly uses a visibly limited historical raw-fruit value; blackberry uses a current commercial-food proxy. Native raspberry, native pigface, ruby saltbush, prickly currant-bush, cherry ballart and common apple-berry are not assigned calories without suitable evidence. Deidentified native-grain results are not assigned to kangaroo grass. One-plant dry-basis coast beard-heath data are not converted into fresh edible-flesh calories. Old cumbungi, former-*Triglochin procera* and raw-Bulbine figures are labelled historical scale only and are not treated as safe servings, modern prepared-food values or harvest returns. Fresh Victorian samphire and nitre-bush energy remains unknown; nardoo receives no actionable value. Bower spinach, kangaroo apples and sea celery receive no borrowed calorie value. Purslane's low-energy proxy is retained only to explain why its other hazards are not worth accepting.

Taste is never used to decide identity. It appears only to help a reader reject an unexpected or spoiled result after a plant is already known. Watching animals, skin rubbing, lip contact, cooking or taking a tiny taste is not presented as a safety test.

## Traditional Owner knowledge and ICIP

The chapter uses the Federation of Victorian Traditional Owner Corporations' native-foods strategy as its governance frame. Material is named by Country and organisation. The murnong page provides brief, high-level context from Wadawurrung, Gunditjmara, Taungurung, Dja Dja Wurrung and Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung sources. Gunditjmara muntrie continuity is cited without claiming an unverified language-to-species equation. The fourth wave keeps Dja Dja Wurrung Buwatj, Coranderrk/Woiwurrung BUATH and Bunganditj Ngoor-le separate. The fifth and sixth waves retain their place-specific Yorta Yorta, Gariwerd, Gunaikurnai, Woi wurrung, Dja Dja Wurrung and Wemba Wemba boundaries. The seventh wave keeps museum-recorded Djabwurrung Burne-burne separate from Gunaikurnai Garawed; attributes the rounded-noon-flower leaf account to Bunurong; keeps separate Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung, Bunurong and Taungurung-linked chocolate-lily statements even where they conflict; records Dja Dja Wurrung Gitjawil matom only as a third-party published attribution; and invents no old-man-saltbush attribution. YYNAC, DJAARA, Gunaikurnai and Bureau-hosted material retains its specific permission boundary. TLaWC plant detail is not repeated because its ICIP notice requires prior written consent. The wording is marked personal-copy only pending direction before wider use. The Martang Djab Wurrung role in a separate study remains a research relationship, not approval of this guide.

No cultural image, medicinal remedy, harvest location, seasonal calendar or detailed preparation method is copied. Public access is not treated as blanket permission.
