## Outcome first

This pass adds useful recognition and cultural context, but **no new emergency eating recipe**.

- Bower spinach is an education-only comparison and pre-preparation stop check for Warrigal greens; IM-200 cannot clear a first-time specimen.
- Kangaroo apples are a do-not-eat hazard-recognition page.
- Cumbungi and water-ribbons are education-only, non-harvest wetland pages.
- Sea celery remains in the withheld table.
- Thirteen source-labelled photographs improve the number of views, but none of the new sets is a complete identification plate.
- Old cumbungi and water-ribbon energy figures are shown only to explain scale and uncertainty. They are not safe servings, modern species values or realistic field returns.

The manuscript now has nineteen pictured food-relevant or hazard-recognition entries, supported by 61 plant photographs and one botanical drawing. The whole edition has 70 visuals: 63 external images and seven original diagrams.

## Gate applied

Every candidate had to answer separate questions:

1. Can current Victorian taxonomy establish the exact plant or group?
2. Can a stressed non-expert distinguish it without tasting, damaging or entering danger?
3. Is the usable part and preparation supported independently from the plant name?
4. Is there a safe serving or at least enough evidence not to imply one?
5. Does the calorie figure match the exact species, part, fresh/cooked state and sample?
6. Are conservation, land-access, contamination and collection rules dealt with?
7. Is cultural knowledge kept with its people, Country, source and permission boundary?
8. Do the images show useful features under recorded reuse terms, without claiming more than they show?

A candidate that fails an important question can still earn a warning or education page. It cannot earn an eating instruction.

## Identity and toxicity findings

### Bower spinach and Warrigal greens

VicFlora and *Flora of Australia* support the comparison in S-400. The useful distinction is not “one leaf looks greener”. Growth form, flower attachment and mature fruit on the same plant must agree. D-064 now places that comparison and IM-200 before every Warrigal preparation step. IM-200 contains no botanical drawing; it only orders the checks and makes no, unknown, missing or inconsistent information stop the route. S-490/IM-202 add one rights-cleared Victorian fruit close-up, but it has no scale, its maturity is not expert-confirmed and there is no matched mature-fruit pair, so the guide still cannot clear first-time identity. Food pages in S-401 disagree about the part or fruit stage used, and none supplies a field-safe serving or species-specific energy value. The Warrigal boiling method therefore stays with already-known, positively identified Warrigal greens and is not copied across the genus.

### Kangaroo apples

S-402 establishes the two accepted Victorian species and the flower/fruit differences. VicFlora also warns that ripe fruit can be necessary to identify some *Solanum*, which means fruit colour alone cannot be an eating key. S-403 separates three facts that must not be blended:

- a historical Lake Condah ripe-fruit record exists;
- an older safety review still reports solasodine in ripe fruit; and
- Schedule 23 applies to food that is sold, processed or handled for sale, or imported.

The schedule is not misrepresented as a personal-possession offence or a diagnosis after one fruit. Combined with the unresolved alkaloid dose and identification risk, it supports a conservative do-not-eat page.

### Cumbungi

S-405 records native *Typha orientalis* and *T. domingensis* plus naturalised *T. latifolia* in Victoria. VicFlora says leaves and other non-flowering parts do not reliably separate them. Tiny flower structures may be required, and an over-mature head may have lost them. The photographs therefore introduce a group and life stages, not a species key.

Wetland digging can destroy the plant and evidence needed for identification, disturb habitat and expose a person to unstable banks, deep mud and contaminated water or sediment. The page gives no digging or preparation sequence.

### Water-ribbons

S-408 records six Victorian *Cycnogeton* species and the taxonomic split of the older broad *Triglochin procera* group. *C. dubium* and *C. microtuberosum* are FFG Endangered. The available pictures show one source-labelled *C. procerum* example above water and its fruit. They do not show the underground stem, roots and tubers or compare them with Victorian relatives. Because digging would both damage a plant and expose the hidden feature, the missing view cannot be solved by telling a stranded reader to pull it up.

### Sea celery

S-410 records native *Apium prostratum*, its two overlapping Victorian varieties and many intermediate populations. FSANZ's “traditional, not novel” view is a regulatory food-classification record, not field identification, a safe part, serving, calorie result or Aboriginal attribution. S-411 records a similar native annual celery and the wider parsley-family danger, including highly toxic hemlock. Hemlock is treated as a catastrophic family-level false positive, not falsely described as a close mature twin.

These values are kept visible because the reader asked for calorie context. Their limits are equally visible. A laboratory or old-table number is not the energy a stranded person can actually obtain after search time, wetland access, trimming, preparation, fuel, water and discarded material.

## Cultural knowledge and ICIP

Cultural authority, botanical identity and toxicology answer different questions. Respect for Traditional Knowledge means giving it proper weight **and** not stripping it from Country, people or permission.

The page uses only short, attributed context:

- Dr Wayne Atkinson's 2005 public submission is identified as a Yorta Yorta-authored historical source. Personal communications, processing details and other knowledge in that submission are not reproduced.
- YYNAC's current Whole-of-Country Plan says its information may be used only with documented YYNAC permission. Its plant detail is not copied.
- The Bureau-hosted Gariwerd calendar records Water Ribbon in a food context, names the custodial language groups used on the page, and states that Elders/Directors of Gariwerd gave permission for the Bureau to host it. That is not permission for this guide to reproduce artwork, a calendar or a recipe.
- TLaWC material was a relevant research lead, but its ICIP notice requires prior written consent to deal with website content. Its plant detail is not repeated.
- Wurundjeri Woi Wurrung material says water-ribbons were collected, not necessarily eaten, and retains Traditional Owner intellectual property. No food claim is inferred.
- Eastern Maar material records seasonal growth, not food use, and credits Brambuk material. Its wording and artwork are not copied.
- The Warrnambool *T. orientalis* study records project-specific Elder assistance and blessing. That research relationship is not community authorship or approval of this guide.

This wording is for the private copy only. Before any wider sharing, the relevant organisations, knowledge holders and content owners must decide whether the attribution, scope and reuse are acceptable.

### Do not copy or infer

- named personal recollections or processing details from the Atkinson submission;
- YYNAC plan content without documented permission;
- TLaWC plant, bushfood, medicinal, language, image or cultural content without prior written consent;
- Wurundjeri Woi Wurrung or Eastern Maar wording, maps, seasonal panels, artwork or images;
- academic figures, photographs or research data without rights;
- “Elders approved this guide” from permission given for another project or host;
- one generic “Aboriginal method” made by joining separate Nations' records;
- generic cumbungi as *T. orientalis*, or generic Water Ribbon as *C. procerum*;
- any field harvesting or preparation sequence reconstructed from restricted or incomplete cultural records.

## Confidence presentation

The original fifth-wave warnings use **High, Moderate, Low or Insufficient**, followed by the reason. D-042 later permits a clearly labelled subjective estimate of wording accuracy for consequential working material. IM-200 therefore displays 95% source-wording and 60% new-layout estimates, while the adjacent warning displays 92% wording confidence. These are editorial estimates only—not probabilities of correct identity, food safety, preparation success or survival—and they do not change any approval.

- SR09-02: kangaroo-apple ripe-colour pictures mistaken for eating clearance.

- SR09-04: old calorie or taste evidence mistaken for a field ration or preparation.
- SR09-05: cultural information reused beyond its permission or source scope.

## Release decision

**Keep in the personal working reader; do not promote to field-ready.**

The pages are useful because they teach what to inspect, what the pictures cannot prove and when not to act. They do not make the guide safe for first-time wild-plant identification. Upgrade requires complete local diagnostic sets, independent botanical and food-safety review, conservation/legal review where needed, culturally authorised scope and a novice test in which no participant consumes a specimen.

`FIELD_READY_BUILD=NO` remains controlling. No field-ready PDF is generated from this work.
