## Main finding

The chapter needs two clearly separate parts: avoiding exposure before a fire threatens, and reducing harm when leaving is no longer possible. The second part cannot promise a safe escape or shelter. It must distinguish being on foot, in an enclosed car and in a building. A bushfire shelter is not automatically suitable for flood, lightning, smoke alone or ordinary overnight cold.

Current CFA advice, South Australian fire-service advice and historical CSIRO research support this separation. They do not provide a fully settled, novice-tested Victorian decision tree for every trapped person. Several exact instructions still need fire and rescue specialists to resolve. [S-296, S-297, S-300, S-302, S-306]

## What the book should explain

### 1. Before fire threatens

Parks Victoria directs people away from bushfire-prone parks and forests under forecast Catastrophic conditions, with some lower-risk sites treated differently. Its advice distinguishes leaving beforehand from deciding how to move after fire is nearby. It also warns that personal notification or closure signs may be absent. Under Extreme conditions, it advises reconsidering a visit and leaving early if already there. [S-299]

Book implication: record the actual destination, route, relevant fire district, current warning and early-leaving plan. Do not translate a calendar, a green map or the absence of a ban into permission to stay. Use the existing weather and communication sections rather than duplicate them. This is an editorial structure, not a new route instruction. [WEATHER-001–008]

### 2. Heat can harm you without flames touching you

Radiant heat is heat reaching the person from the fire. Distance, cover for exposed skin and a solid barrier may reduce exposure, but do not guarantee survival. Do not invent a safe distance, safe flame height or fixed exposure time. [S-298, S-306]

The CSIRO dataset includes deaths in the open, vehicles and buildings. Its reported associations with nearby vegetation are not clearance specifications. A count of deaths lacks the number of people exposed in each type of shelter, so it cannot establish which shelter has a particular survival percentage. Its old FFDI-based rating language must not replace today's Australian Fire Danger Rating System. [S-302]

### 3. A car is a last resort, not a plan to drive through fire

CFA's car advice is conditional on being unable to turn around and drive to safety. It discusses reducing exposure by position and shielding, closing the car, staying below windows under wool blankets, and switching off ventilation and engine as fire approaches. The detailed sequence still needs review for an ordinary enclosed car, limited mobility and a group. Do not silently extend it to a tent, open vehicle, caravan, camper trailer or damaged vehicle. [S-296, S-306]

Two important questions remain:

- **Where to stop:** the instruction to leave the roadway must be read with CFA's warning that a hot exhaust can start a grassfire. Grass and short vegetation cannot be treated as a guaranteed safe parking surface. [S-305]
- **When to leave the car:** CFA's web page does not include the exit step in its July 2022 PDF. CFS and the RFS leaflet refer to the sound of the fire passing. These are not identical instructions. We cannot invent an all-clear from silence or label all burned ground safe. A specialist must define how to handle a failing vehicle and continuing outside danger. [S-296, S-300, S-301, S-306]

Do not draw a turn towards a fire that implies driving towards it or making a dangerous manoeuvre to achieve an ideal orientation.

### 4. Being on foot needs its own decision

CFS and the RFS leaflet discourage trying to outrun flames and discuss nearby clear or solid shielding features. These descriptions do not establish a safe walking distance, slope direction, hollow, road or stream for every Victorian fire. [S-300, S-301]

Keep the exact route and shelter-selection decision under fire/search-and-rescue review. Do not supply a compass direction based on wind, smoke or the usual direction of a Victorian wind change. Do not teach running through flames, backburning, firefighter shelter deployment or digging a survival pit.

### 5. A building can fail

CFA distinguishes prepared shelter from just any building. Its guidance requires attention to changing conditions and escape options; its PDF warns against being trapped in a room without another exit. This is not a basis for telling a novice to defend an unknown hut, enter a roof space or remain in a burning room. [S-297, S-306]

The page must distinguish shelter from outside bushfire heat from the separate emergency of the shelter itself burning. Generic “stay inside” advice must not override that change. Similarly, the smoke-only advice to close a house applies when the fire is not directly threatening it. [S-294]

### 6. Shelter names are not interchangeable

In Victoria, a **Neighbourhood Safer Place — Bushfire Place of Last Resort** may be an open area. It is not an early-leaving destination or a promise of emergency staff, medical help or protection from other hazards. Councils can add or remove sites. [S-303]

A **Community Fire Refuge** is a specifically designated building, activated and opened in response to significant local fire. It is not simply any community hall or fire station. CFA does not guarantee safe travel to it or staff attendance. [S-304]

A final edition needs a current official lookup and a place to record verified local options before a trip, not a permanent statewide list. Do not import the South Australian label “Bushfire Safer Place” as if it meant the same thing as a Victorian last-resort site. [S-300]

## Conflicts and unsafe shortcuts

| Issue | Finding and decision |
|---|---|
| Cloth over the face | CFA's radiant-heat page suggests a scarf/handkerchief can filter smoke. Victorian Health says these do not filter the fine particles that harm lungs. Do not repeat a cloth-as-respirator claim. P2 protection depends on fit and does not remove carbon monoxide. [S-298, S-294] |
| Blanket substitution | CFA specifies wool and its backup page says dry wool. No equivalence has been established here for foil blankets, synthetic sleeping bags or dampened substitutes. Exact material and use remain review questions, not equipment approval. [S-297, S-306] |
| Water as a refuge | CFA mentions water among high-risk last resorts and excludes water tanks. This is not a generic instruction to enter a river or dam. Flood, drowning, access and cold risks must be reconciled with the existing no-crossing and rescue rules. No immersion procedure is approved. [S-297; EP-006, EP-010] |
| Grassfire “safe gap” | A statement that a narrow fuel break may fail does not prove a wider one will stop a fire. Neither green grass nor a width copied from fire-use law defines a survival zone. [S-305] |
| Drink regardless of medical condition | General fire-preparation hydration language cannot override first-aid restrictions for impaired consciousness, swallowing difficulty or particular illnesses. Keep medical conditions visible at the point of use. [EP-003, EP-005, EP-009] |
| Burning has stopped nearby | It does not follow that the route, structures, air or water are safe. Keep post-fire access under current authority and medical guidance; do not add a return time or a “burned ground is safe” symbol. [S-294, S-306] |
| An official PDF must be current in every detail | CFA V6 is dated July 2022. The current resource page was checked, but its download failed during this pass. The separately accessible V6 and current web pages were compared; V6 is not being labelled the latest verified edition. The older RFS leaflet remains a comparator only. [S-301, S-306] |

## First-aid links: with and without a kit

The fire page should identify the need for help and link to the shared pages for response/breathing, burns, heat illness, smoke exposure, asthma and other relevant injury. It must not create competing treatment sequences.

**With a first-aid kit:** use only the exact reviewed condition page, listed items and the person's own prescribed plan where relevant. A respirator, burn dressing or extinguisher does not make entry into fire safe.

**Without a first-aid kit:** preserve the same urgent priorities. Do not add medicinal plants, wet-cloth breathing claims, invented burn preparations or unsafe movement to collect equipment. Where the evidence does not settle an action, the review draft must say that it is missing.

These are instructions for the authoring team, not approved casualty treatment.
