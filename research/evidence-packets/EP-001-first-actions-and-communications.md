# EP-001 — First actions and emergency communications

**Created:** 4 September 2026  
**Chapters:** 02 First minutes; 03 Emergency communication; 04 Stay or move  
**Status:** Evidence packet only — not field-ready guidance

## Purpose

Check what the guide can reliably say about recognising an emergency, getting help, explaining your location, using a personal locator beacon (PLB) and deciding whether to move in Victoria. These are research notes, not instructions to use in an emergency.

## Evidence strengths

- Victoria Police is the responsible search-and-rescue authority and directly tells a lost person to call 000, stop and think, check navigation, consider only a short retrace, seek shelter, stay in one place, remain visible and keep a group together. [S-021]
- The US National Park Service independently converges on stopping and assessing, retracing only when safe, staying put after help is sought, moving only for an immediate threat to life, conserving energy and maintaining group cohesion. It strengthens source independence but does not settle Victorian operational wording. [S-056]
- ACMA, Triple Zero and Telstra independently converge that 000 is primary, 112 is a mobile alternative with no priority advantage, and some mobile network coverage is required. [S-043, S-044, S-045, S-054]
- ACMA, Triple Zero and Telstra converge that ordinary SMS cannot be sent directly to 000/112. [S-043, S-044, S-045]
- ACMA, Triple Zero/AML, Emergency+ and Parks Victoria support providing location explicitly and using device location tools as aids rather than guarantees. [S-043, S-046, S-047, S-055]
- ANZCOR's seriously-ill-or-injured-person guidance and Ambulance Victoria's current call guidance support routing another serious illness or injury to 000 and the matching condition action without requiring a lay diagnosis first. [S-117, S-481]
- Managing-emergency, unconscious-person, ambulance-call, beacon-state, observation and handover sources support protection, rechecking and careful separation of attempted, connected, sent and received contact states. They do not validate the completed card's combined order or create a rescue-arrival promise. [S-169–S-175]
- AMSA is authoritative for Australian PLB activation, registration and deployment. Bushwalking Victoria provides compatible operational context. [S-048, S-049, S-050, S-053]
- The exact two-way assessment now separates direct 000 from a working handheld satellite phone, provider-routed communicator SOS, phone-native emergency satellite service and the Australian PLB. Garmin Mini 3 and Messenger manuals support phone-independent custom text; current ZOLEO material does not establish that capability on the bare unit. [S-043, S-048, S-266-S-273]

## Important contradictions and ambiguities found

### 112 and “no signal”

One current Parks Victoria hiking page says 112 can be dialled from any available network “even without signal.” This wording is liable to be read as “works when no network exists.” ACMA, Triple Zero and Telstra are explicit: the phone may camp on another available carrier, but if no carrier has coverage, 000 and 112 cannot connect through the mobile network. The book will not repeat the ambiguous formulation. [S-043, S-044, S-045, S-055]

### When being lost becomes a 000 emergency

Victoria Police says a person who is lost or requires help should call 000. National emergency-call guidance says 000 is for serious, urgent, life-threatening or time-critical situations. The field guide needs wording that encourages an early call when a person genuinely requires police rescue without implying that every momentary route uncertainty is a 000 matter. This requires Victoria Police/search-and-rescue review. [S-021, S-043, S-044, S-054]

### Retracing steps or gaining height

Victoria Police and the Bushwalking Manual allow a short retrace and suggest that gaining some height may aid orientation. The US National Park Service independently allows retracing only when safe, then says to stay put after seeking help and move only for an immediate threat to life. Retracing or gaining height can still increase fall/exposure risk, separate a person from their last-known point and expand the search area. The three-source threshold is now met, but no flowchart can be approved until a Victorian search-and-rescue specialist defines conditions, distance/time limits and immediate-hazard exceptions. [S-021, S-052, S-056]

### Beacon activation threshold

AMSA uses “grave and imminent danger” and equates this with facing a life-threatening situation. A lost person may deteriorate through cold, heat, darkness, injury or depleted water before the danger feels obvious. The guide must not invent a simpler threshold. It needs specialist wording that helps an inexperienced person act early enough while respecting official guidance. [S-048, S-053]

### PLB deployment and movement

AMSA and one Australian manufacturer's manual converge on an upright antenna, clear sky and avoiding obstruction. However, model instructions vary, and moving to higher ground after activation may conflict with staying findable or may be unsafe. Final guidance should prioritise the specific manual and avoid directing hazardous movement merely to improve sky view. A third independent technical check and specialist review are pending. [S-049, S-051]

### Two-way does not mean one universal emergency route

A mobile emergency call, handheld-satellite-phone call, private-centre communicator SOS, phone-native satellite emergency session and PLB alert reach help through different first recipients and infrastructure. The book must name the exact route and distinguish GNSS fix, queued, sent, provider-received, replied, operationally dispatched and responder-arrived states. No acknowledgement is an arrival promise. A communicator adds valuable incident detail but also adds plan/account, rechargeable battery, firmware and sometimes phone/app dependencies, so it does not automatically replace the PLB. [S-043, S-048, S-172, S-223, S-266-S-273]

### Pre-release ZOLEO information conflict

ZOLEO's Australian launch page says its next product is coming in September 2026, while a current support page describes ZL1500 USB-C hardware. The general manual located during this check still ends with legacy micro-USB/Bluetooth specifications and an apparent weight-unit error. The guide will not assemble a favourable ZL1500 procedure from incompatible documents. The model remains deferred until exact launched-model documentation and an Australian sample agree. [S-270]

### 30 September 2026 first-actions-card working assessment

IM-204 is a completed working routing aid, not a substitute for the detailed pages and not field-ready. It uses three text-led panels:

1. **Urgent override:** leave immediate danger when that is the only safe option; route obvious life-threatening bleeding, an unresponsive person who is not breathing normally, and another serious illness or injury immediately to 000 and the matching Chapter 6 action. These actions and the call may happen in parallel when another capable person is available. The card sets no lone-rescuer priority between simultaneous life threats. [S-072, S-076, S-117, S-169, S-170, S-481]
2. **Stop, group, call:** stop unnecessary movement, keep the group together when safe and call 000 for an urgent rescue or medical emergency. Do not wait for a perfect location description or depend only on the reader accepting the subjective label “lost”. A failed call or other failed contact routes to Chapter 3. Attempted, connected, sent and received are different states; none alone proves that rescue is coming. [S-021, S-043, S-052, S-056, S-171, S-172]
3. **Write, protect, stay findable, recheck:** record only what is known without delaying urgent care; protect people from exposure; stay near the reported place only while it remains safe; and recheck whenever the person or situation changes. Detailed shelter, location, observation and handover methods remain on their canonical pages. [S-169–S-175]

The card must not set a five-minute timer or rigid serial order. It must not add retracing, route choice, gaining height, moving for reception, signal-seeking, walkout, group splitting, a messenger, a missing-person search, device technique or a rescue-arrival promise. Every movement question routes to Chapter 4. Final audit must specifically test omission of other serious emergencies, unsafe-site stay, subjective “lost” wording, attempted-versus-received contact, competing life threats with one rescuer, and loss of the urgent override when a panel is detached. The core wording is estimated at 95%, the combined ordering at 75% and the new layout at 60%. These are subjective editorial estimates of wording/order/layout only—not probabilities of safety, successful contact, rescue, treatment outcome or survival. Victorian SAR/emergency-communications, emergency-clinician, human-factors, complete-card, detached-panel and stressed-reader review are mandatory; `FIELD_READY_BUILD=NO` remains controlling.

## Specialist questions

1. At what point should route uncertainty be treated as a police/search-and-rescue emergency rather than self-corrected navigation?
2. What limits, if any, can safely define “short retrace” for an inexperienced person?
3. When, if ever, should a lost person gain height for orientation/reception after making contact or activating a beacon?
4. What immediate hazards justify leaving a last-known point, and how should movement be documented?
5. How should the PLB threshold be explained so a novice does not wait for severe hypothermia, dehydration or nightfall?
6. After a successful two-way SOS, which instructions from the operator override generic book guidance?
