# EP-009 - Evacuation, monitoring and handover

**Created:** 4 September 2026  
**Chapter:** 06 First aid and evacuation  
**Dependencies:** 02 First minutes; 03 Emergency communication; 04 Stay or move; 05 Body temperature and shelter; EP-003 to EP-008  
**Status:** Evidence packet only - not field-ready guidance

## Purpose

Check the evidence and unanswered practical questions about:

- treating someone where they are, moving only as needed for immediate safety or lifesaving care, waiting for rescue or trying to get out without rescuers;
- following 000, police/search-and-rescue, beacon and two-way satellite instructions;
- checking response, breathing, bleeding, signs of getting worse and harm from the surroundings while rescue is delayed;
- recording what happened, treatment and changes without pretending to diagnose an illness;
- telling rescuers what they need to know when they take over care;
- what can be done with the listed equipment, and without it.

This packet does not approve technical rescue, vehicle extrication, steep/vertical/water rescue, spinal clearance, an improvised litter, a single-rescuer carry, private-vehicle transport of a time-critical patient or a universal self-evacuation rule. It also does not create a lay termination-of-resuscitation rule beyond current ANZCOR bystander criteria. No sequence below is field-ready until reviewed by Australian remote-emergency and Victorian search-and-rescue specialists and tested in representative Victorian terrain and weather.

## Evidence strengths

### Emergency services and rescue instructions

- Ambulance Victoria says location is the first and most important information in an emergency ambulance call. The caller should explain what happened, number of people, age, consciousness and breathing, stay on the line, follow call-taker instructions and report any change. Continued questions do not delay dispatch after the location and emergency are known. [S-171]
- Australian emergency-call sources confirm that 000 is for serious/urgent medical help, the caller must state the required service and location, and a call-taker can provide current first-aid direction. Device and network limitations remain governed by EP-001. [S-043, S-044, S-171, S-177]
- When two-way contact exists, incident-specific direction from 000, police/search-and-rescue or another responsible rescue service is more current and better informed than a static book. A group should repeat back location, intended movement, hazards and instructions rather than silently interpreting them. [S-171]
- AMSA says a genuine distress beacon should remain on until rescue services advise otherwise. An apparent improvement, a return-link acknowledgement or hearing an aircraft is not permission to turn it off; response time varies with terrain, weather and access. [S-172]

### Move only for a defined reason

- ANZCOR warns that movement can worsen pain, injury, blood loss and shock. It supports movement when needed for safety, extreme-weather protection, difficult-terrain evacuation, airway/breathing care or severe-bleeding control, and prioritises prompt ambulance transport. [S-169]
- ANZCOR, St John Victoria and the IFRC independently converge that the risk of moving must be balanced against the risk of leaving the person where they are. Immediate danger or inability to provide lifesaving care are clear reasons to move; unnecessary movement is discouraged. [S-169, S-175, S-176]
- Airway and breathing take priority over suspected spinal injury. An unresponsive person who is breathing normally is placed on their side with gentle handling; a face-down collapsed person may need to be rolled to assess breathing/perform CPR. [S-097, S-169, S-170]
- “Move for difficult-terrain evacuation” does not establish that an untrained group should carry a casualty over rough ground. St John requires consideration of patient/crew size, available help, injury, ground and distance. Technical extraction and complex carries require a trained team, leader, equipment and route plan. [S-169, S-175]
- Victoria Police tells lost people to seek help, shelter, stay in one place, remain visible and keep a group together. This favours organised rescue after contact/PLB rather than an unsupported walkout, except for a current hazard or explicit responder direction. [S-021]

### Monitoring during a delayed response

- ANZCOR's general emergency guidance requires prevention of further harm, checks of response, airway/breathing and bleeding, weather protection, gentle handling, reassurance and continued observation. [S-169]
- An unconscious person who is breathing normally requires constant rechecking for any change; abnormal breathing triggers the BLS/CPR pathway. Care of the airway still takes priority over possible spinal injury. [S-170]
- The current seriously-ill-person guideline likewise emphasises concern-based escalation and repeated observation rather than requiring a lay diagnosis or a single normal number. A parent, companion or first aider thinking “something is wrong” is sufficient reason to seek emergency assessment. [S-117]
- Observation must be condition-specific. Bleeding control, anaphylaxis, snakebite/PIT, head injury, heat/cold illness, drowning, seizure, poisoning and other branches have different deterioration signs and prohibited actions. This packet creates one recheck spine but does not replace those cards.
- ANZCOR says bystander CPR continues until the person responds/breathes normally, continuation is impossible from exhaustion, a health professional takes over, or a health professional directs cessation. A static wilderness guide cannot add a time-based stopping rule. [S-072, S-073]

### Record and handover

- Ambulance Victoria's handover protocol and the Australian Commission's ambulance standards use structured information: identity, mechanism/medical complaint, injuries/information, signs, treatment and trends, allergies, medicines, background and other relevant issues. [S-173, S-174]
- For a lay field guide, “signs” should mean only accurate observations or measurements the person is trained and equipped to take. The record must never fabricate a pulse, blood pressure, oxygen saturation, Glasgow Coma Scale or diagnosis. A change from that person's earlier state can matter even if no number is available. [S-117, S-173, S-174, S-175]
- An objective written timeline reduces reliance on distressed memory: time found/event, first 000/SOS, symptoms and changes, treatments/devices/medicines with time, response, allergies, regular medicines, relevant history, location and hazards. Responders should receive the original record or a faithful copy. [S-171, S-173, S-174, S-175]
- Structured handover is not a reason to delay lifesaving care or interrupt the call-taker. A short prompt card should collect information as it becomes available and clearly mark unknowns. [S-171, S-174]

## Important contradictions and operational tensions

### Treat-in-place and evacuation are not opposites

First aid begins where the person is found when safe. Emergency services may still organise transport while treatment continues. “Do not move unnecessarily” does not mean never transport; “evacuate” does not mean the group should immediately pick the person up. The final flow must separate: lifesaving reposition, minimum move from a live hazard, supported self-movement, organised rescue and technical carry. [S-169, S-175, S-176]

### Minimum emergency move versus full bush evacuation

Moving a person metres away from fire, traffic, rising water, a falling-tree zone or an unmanageable weather exposure is a different problem from carrying them kilometres over steep terrain. A minimum move may preserve life; a failed carry can injure the patient and rescuers, exhaust the group and make everyone harder to locate. The guide will not let one generic “drag/carry” picture serve both tasks. [S-169, S-175]

### Weather protection can require movement

Victoria Police says lost people should shelter and stay, while ANZCOR recognises movement to protect against extreme weather. The least-movement solution may be to build protection around the person, move only to nearby safe shelter or follow rescue instructions. Exact branches for flood, lightning, fire, alpine exposure and falling trees depend on later hazard packets and cannot be solved by the medical chapter alone. [S-021, S-067, S-069, S-071, S-169]

### “Stay together” versus sending for help

Victoria Police says a lost group should never separate. ANZCOR's general emergency guidance says the most experienced rescuer ideally stays while another person seeks help. These are different contexts, and sending a messenger in unfamiliar bush can create another lost person. With modern communications or a PLB, group cohesion is preferred; a no-contact/no-beacon exception requires direct Victorian SAR review. [S-021, S-169]

### An injured person walking out is not automatically safer

Walking can worsen snake envenomation, bleeding, heat/cold illness, head/spinal/limb injury and serious medical conditions. Conversely, remaining in a deteriorating environment without communication can also be dangerous. No single symptom list safely clears self-evacuation. The final decision needs condition-specific exclusions, safe known route, companion capacity, daylight/weather/terrain and responder advice. [S-003, S-005, S-021, S-081, S-097, S-169]

### Private vehicle versus ambulance is context-dependent

Time-critical conditions benefit from emergency assessment and ambulance transport; driving oneself or transporting an unstable casualty can remove monitoring and create a road crash. Yet a remote call-taker may sometimes direct a particular rendezvous or transport plan. The book cannot impose one blanket “drive” or “never drive” rule: call/communicate early and follow the responsible service. [S-108, S-110, S-114, S-116, S-171]

### Rechecking must not interrupt treatment

Repeated formal “vital signs” can distract from direct pressure, CPR, cooling, airway observation or prescribed emergency treatment. Monitoring means noticing whether the person is more or less responsive, breathing normally, bleeding through treatment, developing new symptoms or failing to improve. Numerical observations are secondary and only for trained users with equipment. [S-072, S-117, S-169, S-170, S-175]

### No universal recheck interval is supported

Selected sources use “continued,” “constant,” “frequent” or “regular” observation and condition-specific times. A printed five- or ten-minute timer could be too slow during rapid deterioration or needless during a stable long wait. The field page should couple continuous airway/breathing awareness with event-driven and condition-card rechecks; an exact generic interval remains withheld. [S-117, S-169, S-170, S-175]

### Normal numbers can falsely reassure

A single pulse, temperature, glucose or oxygen-saturation reading cannot clear stroke, heart attack, poisoning, serious infection, head injury, drowning or other time-critical conditions. Devices can be wrong in cold, motion, poor circulation, contamination or user error. The guide will record trends only within the relevant reviewed condition branch and never make a gadget result the gate to 000. [S-108, S-114, S-117, S-120]

### The patient log must fit a stressed lay user

Full IMIST-AMBO is designed for clinical handover and contains observations a lay user may not know. A simplified prompt risks omitting relevant information; an exhaustive medical form risks delaying care. The personal guide needs a tested one-page casualty record that preserves the structure but accepts “unknown” and prioritises event/treatment times and changes. [S-173, S-174]

### Beacon acknowledgement is not rescue arrival

AMSA's return-link acknowledgement indicates that the alert and location were received, not that rescue has arrived or that a particular asset is coming. Turning the beacon off, moving away or assuming a short arrival time can defeat homing and survival planning. [S-172]

### CPR exhaustion is not a clock rule

ANZCOR permits stopping when continuation is impossible from exhaustion, but does not define a wilderness number of minutes. The book must not convert this into permission to stop because response is delayed. Group rotation, if available, should minimise interruption; any remote termination rule beyond the bystander guideline is excluded. [S-072, S-073]

## Candidate propositions under gate control

These propositions are tracked in `CONTENT_REGISTER.md`; none is approved reader instruction.

### Evacuation and movement

1. Give exact location and emergency first, keep the phone available/on speaker when practical, follow the call-taker and report changes.
2. If responsible responders can communicate, repeat back and follow their incident-specific movement, treatment, rendezvous and signalling instructions.
3. Move a casualty only for a defined reason: immediate safety/weather threat, essential airway/breathing/bleeding care, or a reviewed/authorised evacuation plan; use the least movement that solves the problem.
4. After rescue is activated, stay findable, visible, sheltered and together unless the present site is unsafe or rescuers direct movement; keep a genuine PLB transmitting until told otherwise.
5. Do not teach technical terrain/water/vehicle extraction, improvised litter or generic single-rescuer carries in the initial lay guide.
6. Do not issue a universal self-walkout clearance; make it a specialist-reviewed intersection of condition, route, weather, terrain, group capacity and communication.

### Monitoring and handover

1. Continue observing danger/weather, response, normal breathing, bleeding and condition-specific deterioration; any deterioration reopens the emergency flow.
2. Keep an unresponsive normally breathing person on their side with airway observation and gentle handling; abnormal breathing switches immediately to CPR.
3. Do not let numerical observations or gadgets delay 000/treatment or overrule a concerning trend.
4. Record what happened, time, symptoms/changes, actions/medicines/devices, response, allergies, regular medicines/history, location and hazards, marking unknowns honestly.
5. Give a concise structured handover and explicitly transfer responsibility when responders take over.
6. Continue CPR only under the existing ANZCOR duration criteria; add no wilderness time threshold.

## Required paired treatment paths

### With a first-aid kit

Candidate capability classes, not approved generic inclusions:

- the exact condition-specific supplies already registered for bleeding, PIT, anaphylaxis, temperature, wounds and other medical branches;
- indelible time marking (`KIT-009`) for a treatment/device only when that specific page authorises it;
- a waterproof casualty record card, pencil and protected spare (`KIT-030`) using a tested simplified handover structure;
- reliable timekeeping using a watch/phone/device that does not remove the only communication channel from service;
- weather protection, ground insulation, light and communications from the broader emergency equipment system, not mislabelled as medical treatment;
- numerical devices only where a separate content claim, training standard, exact model and decision role are approved; no pulse oximeter or blood-pressure cuff is currently a baseline inclusion;
- a purpose-designed commercial rescue litter only for a trained group and defined activity after separate load, terrain and clinical review; none is registered for the personal baseline kit.

### Without a first-aid kit

This also applies if the kit is missing the item needed for the condition.

- Call/communication: use the available phone/radio/two-way satellite/PLB path from EP-001. Lack of a medical kit is not a reason to delay the emergency alert.
- Movement: remove or reduce the hazard when possible. If the person must be moved to make lifesaving care possible, use only the minimum movement and current call-taker direction; no belt, rope, tarp or branch is assumed to be a safe stretcher.
- Monitoring: stay close enough to observe response and breathing, repeatedly look for renewed bleeding/new symptoms and protect from weather. Do not substitute a pulse check for assessing response and normal breathing.
- Recording: use a phone note only if it does not compromise emergency contact, or any dry paper/cardboard and pencil. If nothing is available, assign one companion to remember/repeat the event, action and change sequence and state uncertainty to responders.
- Handover: give the essential facts verbally, beginning with what is changing or immediately dangerous. Unknown is safer than an invented time, dose, diagnosis or vital sign.
- Evacuation: communication and signalling become more important because improvised carrying equipment is not equivalent to a trained retrieval team. Continue condition-specific care while rescue approaches.

## Visual requirements and prohibitions

Required candidate visuals:

- one movement-decision panel separating live hazard, lifesaving reposition, stay/await rescue, supported movement and technical rescue;
- a “minimum move is not an evacuation” terrain diagram;
- an integrated delayed-rescue recheck loop linked to response/breathing, bleeding, weather and each condition card;
- a simple “condition changed - tell 000/rescue” prompt;
- a beacon-on/stay-findable panel cross-referenced to the exact communications page;
- a one-page waterproof casualty record/handover card tested against the Ambulance Victoria structure;
- group role allocation that keeps the patient observed and does not casually split a lost group.

Prohibitions:

- no generic carry/drag/litter picture that could be copied over rough, steep, wet or vertical terrain;
- no untrained vehicle extrication, water/rope rescue or spinal clearance;
- no advice to walk off a suspected snakebite, serious head/spinal/limb injury, uncontrolled bleeding or unstable illness;
- no instruction to split a lost group or send a lone messenger without an approved exception;
- no fixed rescue-arrival promise, PLB shutoff after acknowledgement or silent movement away from the reported location;
- no fixed generic recheck interval, lay Glasgow Coma Scale, diagnosis or “normal vital signs means safe” panel;
- no measurement that interrupts CPR, pressure, cooling, airway management or emergency communication;
- no invented medicine/dose/time in the log and no handover form that delays immediate care;
- no time-based wilderness CPR cessation rule.

## Validation and review required

- Victorian Police/Search and Rescue or BSAR-aligned reviewer resolves post-alert movement, group cohesion, no-contact messenger exception, findability and rendezvous wording.
- Ambulance Victoria/remote retrieval or Australian wilderness-emergency clinician resolves ambulance versus supported self-evacuation, private vehicle/rendezvous, casualty movement and prolonged-care priorities.
- Accredited first-aid/resuscitation instructor validates emergency repositioning, recovery position, CPR continuation and the boundary between a refresher and a hands-on skill.
- Occupational/manual-handling and rescue specialists determine whether any carry or litter belongs in the book. Exact improvised litter/carry instructions remain excluded unless equipment, crew, load, terrain and failure tests pass.
- Information-design/usability reviewer converts clinical handover structure into a lay one-page record without loss of critical content or treatment delay.
- Tests cover solo and group cases; no mobile coverage; only PLB; intermittent two-way messaging; cold rain; heat; darkness; unstable ground; vehicle-adjacent incidents; lost versus known location; and a casualty who initially improves then deteriorates.
- Movement tests use weighted dummies, not an injured person, and stop on rescuer instability, equipment damage, uncontrolled movement or excessive delay. No steep/vertical/water test is attempted without qualified rescue supervision.
- Monitoring tests record missed abnormal breathing, renewed bleeding, treatment interruption, false reassurance from devices and handover omissions.
- Final cross-chapter review ensures one authoritative location, call, stay/move, weather, medical and signalling flow; duplicated instructions are replaced by links.

## Decision

Evidence packet complete. Core propositions may advance only to specialist review drafts. The following remain excluded or blocked: a universal walkout rule, automatic private-vehicle transport, untrained technical extraction, improvised litter/carry instructions, sending a lone messenger, a fixed recheck interval, unreviewed clinical scores/devices, turning off a genuine PLB before rescue advice, and any time-based wilderness CPR stopping rule.
