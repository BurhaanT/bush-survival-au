# EP-003 — Critical first aid and evacuation

**Created:** 4 September 2026  
**Chapter:** 06 First aid and evacuation  
**Dependencies:** 02 First minutes; 03 Emergency communication; 04 Stay or move; 05 Temperature and shelter  
**Status:** Evidence packet only — not field-ready guidance

## Purpose

Check the first group of urgent medical topics for a Victorian bush guide that works whatever brought the reader there. This packet covers:

- scene safety, unresponsiveness, abnormal breathing, CPR and AED use;
- severe external bleeding;
- known or suspected Australian snakebite;
- anaphylaxis;
- communication and movement decisions that interact with those conditions.

Other packets now cover burns, fractures, head/spinal injury, drowning, chest pain, stroke, seizure, asthma, diabetes, poisoning, eye injury, wounds and other bites or stings (EP-004 to EP-009). Those subjects are not treatment instructions in this packet.

No treatment sequence in this packet is approved for field use. Every eventual entry must follow `book/MEDICAL_ENTRY_TEMPLATE.md`, provide both kit and no-kit pathways and be signed off by a qualified Australian clinician with remote-care competence.

## Evidence strengths

### Basic life support

- ANZCOR’s current Guideline 8 is the primary source: use DRSABCD; start CPR when a person is unresponsive and not breathing normally; use 30 compressions to two breaths for all ages; perform compressions even if unwilling or unable to give breaths; attach an AED as soon as available and follow it. [S-072]
- healthdirect and St John independently converge on the same core sequence, rate and compression-only fallback, while making clear that adult/child and infant technique differ. [S-073, S-074, S-075]
- Emergency-operator instructions and current ANZCOR guidance take precedence over the printed book. The book can act only as a prompt; it cannot provide the feedback or physical practice needed for competent CPR.

### Severe external bleeding

- ANZCOR supports firm direct pressure with hands or a pad, more focused pressure if initial padding fails, pressure around rather than removal of an embedded object, and urgent ambulance transfer. [S-076]
- ANZCOR supports an arterial tourniquet only for life-threatening limb bleeding not controlled by direct pressure. A manufactured windlass device is preferred; haemostatic dressings are reserved for defined severe-bleeding situations and need training. [S-076]
- healthdirect and St John independently support direct pressure, clean dry material and pressure around embedded objects. St John adds a concise catastrophic-bleeding pathway. [S-077, S-078]
- ILCOR and the systematic review support manufactured tourniquets over improvised devices but grade the evidence very low. [S-079, S-080]

### Australian snakebite

- ANZCOR says any suspected Australian snakebite requires an ambulance, stillness, constant observation, pressure bandaging with immobilisation and CPR if the person becomes unresponsive and is not breathing normally. Guideline 9.4.1 applies to adults, children and infants; its evidence search was May 2020, it was approved in April 2021 and the recommendations are Good Practice Statements. [S-081]
- ANZCOR’s technique guideline and current Ambulance Victoria guidance support a broad elasticised pressure bandage, limb immobilisation and a broad clothing/material fallback if no bandage exists. ANZCOR 9.4.8 was searched/approved in 2011 and remained published in 2026; it permits local pressure followed by a second whole-limb bandage or one fingers/toes-up whole-limb bandage. [S-082, S-083]
- healthdirect and St John use the local-first two-bandage route; Ambulance Victoria's public guidance updated 25 February 2026 uses the single distal-to-proximal route. Safer Care Victoria supports clinical continuity but was overdue for review and does not settle this lay-technique conflict. [S-083, S-084, S-085, S-086]
- ANZCOR explicitly acknowledges no direct field comparison, frequent incorrect application and insufficient evidence to identify the best sequence or direction. Strong current-source agreement that PBI is recommended is not high-certainty clinical-outcome evidence. This strengthens the need for tested wording, equipment and practice, not confidence in a static picture. [S-081, S-082]

### Anaphylaxis

- Current ASCIA first-aid, positioning and July 2026 living clinical guidance are the leading sources. They support acting on any one severe airway/breathing/circulation sign without waiting for skin signs, immediate adrenaline, no standing/walking and explicit breathing, side, pregnancy and young-child position branches. [S-087, S-088, S-482]
- ANZCOR independently treats anaphylaxis as an immediate medical emergency and supports prompt adrenaline, ambulance activation, a further dose after five minutes when severe symptoms do not respond, and CPR when the person is unresponsive and not breathing normally. Its older intramuscular/autoinjector product detail does not control the 2026 multi-route landscape. [S-089]
- healthdirect and St John corroborate the core but are older autoinjector- or EpiPen-specific sources. Current ASCIA and RCH material records four Australian product families—EpiPen, Anapen, Jext and neffy—with injection and nasal routes and separate instructions. Product-neutral wording is mandatory on the rapid card. [S-090, S-091, S-092, S-149, S-482]

### Working anaphylaxis visual addendum — 29 September 2026

D-043’s split-not-shrink rule, D-052 and D-062 permit a three-card set in the personal working edition only: IM-191 recognises anaphylaxis and starts immediate action; IM-192 gives the ordered position decision and specifically implements the need formerly planned as IM-024; IM-193 owns five-minute reassessment, the no-product branch, asthma overlap, continued monitoring and hospital follow-up. All three are visibly unapproved and product neutral. The set may show the any-one-severe-sign/no-rash rule, adrenaline first, 000, the **unresponsive and not breathing normally** CPR gate and the relevant position/follow-up branches without shrinking one card into unreadable text.

IM-191–IM-193 do not show or decide a brand, dose, route, cap/end, activation, hold time, anatomical target, age/weight selection, use of an expired or another person’s product, ampoule-and-syringe technique or number of products to carry. Chapter 6’s broader severe/persistent abdominal-pain or repeated-vomiting wording remains cautious because simplified and detailed 2026 guidance use different thresholds. D-062 makes the five-minute action direct rather than permission-gated, keeps failed emergency contact visible with or without a device and narrows the asthma overlap to known severe allergy and possible contact with that severe allergen. Each card’s 95% source-match and 60% new-layout figures are subjective editorial estimates only, not probabilities of safety, treatment success or survival. Allergy-clinical, respiratory-overlap, pharmacist/device, resuscitation, practical, combined-set post-layout and stressed-reader review remain open; `FIELD_READY_BUILD=NO` controls.

## Important contradictions and operational tensions

### DRSABCD versus immediately obvious catastrophic bleeding

The standard DRSABCD sequence begins with danger, response and sending for help before airway, breathing, CPR and defibrillation. ANZCOR and St John say that immediately obvious life-threatening bleeding may need control before airway and breathing, while ANZCOR also directs BLS when a person is unresponsive and not breathing normally. The book must not create two competing opening flowcharts. IM-190 tells multiple helpers to divide calling/observation and pressure, but it does not invent a lone-rescuer algorithm for simultaneous uncontrolled bleeding and CPR. [S-072, S-074, S-076, S-078]

### CPR details cannot be safely compressed into one undifferentiated picture

ANZCOR uses one 30:2 ratio for all ages, but hand/finger technique, head position and breath delivery differ for an infant. Drowning and hypothermic arrest also have special considerations. A small generic silhouette risks a wrong technique. The guide needs separate adult/child-over-one and infant panels or a direct controlled reference, plus in-person training. [S-057, S-072, S-073, S-075]

### Infection barriers must not delay compressions

ANZCOR says a barrier device for rescue breaths is reasonable if available and also encourages all rescuers to provide chest compressions. The with-kit branch can name a validated barrier, but the no-kit branch must not imply that CPR should be withheld while searching for equipment. Exact bodily-fluid and unwilling/unable-to-breathe wording requires clinical review. [S-072, S-073, S-075]

### More dressing layers can reduce useful pressure

Common first-aid shorthand says to add another pad if bleeding continues. ANZCOR warns that repeatedly layering pads spreads force and may miss the bleeding point; it recommends checking placement and concentrating firm pressure. The final diagram must show reassessment, not an endless stack of dressings. [S-076, S-077, S-078]

### Tourniquets are lifesaving only in a narrow problem and can fail dangerously

ANZCOR and ILCOR support a manufactured tourniquet for life-threatening limb bleeding that direct pressure has not controlled, but the evidence certainty is very low. ANZCOR warns that an ineffective improvised tourniquet can increase bleeding and that a venous blood-draw tourniquet is unsuitable. The personal guide cannot treat “tourniquet” as a generic strap. [S-076, S-079, S-080]

### The no-kit tourniquet branch is not equivalent to the kit branch

ANZCOR permits a broad improvised windlass tourniquet as better than none in otherwise uncontrolled life-threatening limb bleeding, while preferring a manufactured device. Improvisation requires material width, positioning, tightening, securing, time recording, bleeding reassessment and non-removal to be correct under stress. This is a candidate only for clinician-led training and destructive simulation testing; the guide may instead decide that without prior training it can safely teach only direct pressure and emergency escalation. [S-076, S-079, S-080]

### IM-190 is a direct-pressure reading card, not an advanced bleeding-control technique

Under D-051, IM-190 is a working-edition exception for the direct-pressure core only. It makes early 000 prominent, uses firm continuous pressure on or around the bleeding point, separates the embedded-object branch, gives kit/no-kit material options and prevents an endless pad stack. It contains no anatomy-dependent force claim, tourniquet placement/tightening, improvised construction, haemostatic application, wound packing, pressure-point method, internal-bleeding treatment or specialised eye/torso impalement care. Its manufactured-tourniquet text is a trained-product boundary, not an application procedure. [S-076–S-080, S-481]

The 95% core-wording and 72% layout figures are subjective editorial estimates only. They do not estimate correct pressure, safety, treatment success or survival. Australian emergency-clinical/instructor review, bleeding task-trainer and exact-product trials, delayed recall, greyscale/print and stressed-reader testing remain required; `FIELD_READY_BUILD=NO` controls.

### Snakebite bandage sequence is not scientifically settled

ANZCOR states that field application is often inadequate and evidence is insufficient to identify the most effective wrapping sequence or direction. It permits local pressure followed by full-limb wrapping or one bandage from fingers/toes upward. St John and healthdirect show a two-bandage bite-site-first sequence; Ambulance Victoria publicly describes starting at fingers/toes and covering the bite. IM-189 uses the local-first wording only as one named working concept under D-050. It must not imply that this is proven best, encourage a search for an unknown site, silently switch to the other method or copy branded/source artwork. [S-082, S-083, S-084, S-085]

### Snakebite “stay still” has a severe lone-person exception

ANZCOR says to bring transport to the person where possible and remain immobile after self-bandaging. It also says that if a lone person cannot obtain urgent help, they may need local pressure and self-movement to seek it, making immobilisation contraindicated in that circumstance. This is one of the book’s hardest movement decisions and must be reconciled with `MOVE-001`, communications options, terrain, expected contact and time. No simplified “never move” or “walk out” rule is acceptable. [S-082]

### A snakebite arterial tourniquet is not the same as a bleeding tourniquet

ANZCOR prohibits arterial tourniquets for snakebite while permitting them for otherwise uncontrolled life-threatening limb haemorrhage. The guide must visually separate these pages so a reader does not transfer a tool between conditions. [S-076, S-081, S-082]

### Anaphylaxis may occur without skin signs

Simplified allergy advice often foregrounds rash or swelling. Current ASCIA and RCH material treats any one severe airway, breathing or circulation sign as enough and does not require skin symptoms. The field card must not make a rash a prerequisite. Detailed 2026 guidance says severe persistent abdominal pain or repeated vomiting can indicate anaphylaxis from any cause, while simplified first-aid plans highlight abdominal pain/vomiting after insect sting; IM-191 therefore uses cautious “can be” wording pending allergy-clinical review. [S-087, S-088, S-090, S-482]

### Anaphylaxis position is not simply “sit them up” or “recovery position”

ASCIA says flat and never standing/walking; difficult breathing can use ground sitting with legs outstretched, while vomiting or unresponsiveness with normal breathing uses the side/recovery position and pregnancy uses the left side. Young children are flat across a carer’s lap, or legs-out when breathing is difficult, not upright or over a shoulder. healthdirect’s November 2024 page combines vomiting with legs-out sitting, so current ASCIA specialist guidance controls. IM-192 shows ordered outcomes, not how to roll a person; a clinician must review its branch order and overlaps. [S-087, S-090, S-091, S-482]

### Device instructions and doses cannot be merged

EpiPen, Anapen, Jext and neffy are not interchangeable instruction sets; neffy is nasal while the other three families inject. Prescribed product and dose depend on the person and current plan. ANZCOR’s older age-banded autoinjector text and the older healthdirect/St John pages cannot override current labelling or ASCIA practice. The guide must never present a hybrid drawing, imply that every product is an autoinjector or tell an untrained reader to draw adrenaline from an ampoule. Exact availability, prescription, dose, route, label, storage and instructions require a fresh check every edition and before a trip. [S-088, S-089, S-091, S-092, S-149, S-482]

### The no-kit anaphylaxis path has no medication substitute

Positioning, preventing further exposure only when safe and without delay, calling 000 and monitoring breathing matter, but none replaces adrenaline. Antihistamines, asthma relievers, food, drink or an improvised remedy must not be presented as substitutes. CPR starts only when the person is unresponsive **and** not breathing normally; the February 2026 ASCIA poster’s “or” wording conflicts with current ANZCOR basic life support and RCH, so **and** controls. The no-product branch must say explicitly that the missing medication cannot be improvised. [S-072, S-087, S-088, S-089, S-090, S-482]

## Candidate propositions under gate control

These propositions are tracked in `CONTENT_REGISTER.md`; they are not approved reader instructions.

### First response / CPR

1. Use scene safety, response, help, airway, breathing, CPR and defibrillation as the common emergency frame, with one explicit catastrophic-bleeding interrupt reviewed by a clinician.
2. Begin CPR when a person is unresponsive and not breathing normally, including when only occasional gasps are present.
3. Use the current ANZCOR 30:2 approach; compression-only CPR is the fallback for a rescuer unwilling or unable to give breaths.
4. Use an AED as soon as one is available without abandoning the person or creating an avoidable interruption to compressions.
5. Separate adult/child-over-one and infant technique; link the page to current ANZCOR and make recent hands-on training a preparation requirement.

### Severe bleeding

1. Recognise blood loss that is uncontrolled by firm continuous pressure and/or accompanied by shock-like deterioration as life threatening and activate 000 early.
2. Use firm direct pressure on the bleeding point; where no pad exists, hands or appropriate clean dry ordinary material are candidate no-kit measures.
3. Apply pressure around an embedded object and do not remove it.
4. If bleeding continues, keep focused pressure, use no more than one additional accurately placed pad, reassess whether pressure is on the source and avoid a loose stack.
5. Use a manufactured arterial tourniquet only for life-threatening limb bleeding not controlled by direct pressure, following the exact device instructions and training.
6. Record application time, keep the device visible, confirm that bleeding stops and never remove it before specialist care.
7. Treat haemostatic dressings and improvised tourniquets as separate high-training branches; neither is an ordinary bandage substitute. IM-190 teaches neither method.

### Suspected snakebite

1. Treat every known or suspected Australian snakebite as potentially life threatening even when the mark is tiny, painless or symptoms improve after an initial collapse.
2. Call 000, keep the person still/reassured/continuously observed and apply a reviewed pressure-immobilisation technique; CPR takes priority when indicated.
3. With kit: use the selected broad elasticised bandage(s), splint and marking tool only under the final tested sequence. IM-189's whole-limb picture is a concept map, not wrapping or pressure instruction.
4. Without kit: use a reviewed broad clothing/material wrap and splint only if the exact material and method pass pressure/usability testing; stillness is not replaced by wrapping. IM-189 contains no no-kit mechanics.
5. Do not wash, cut or suck the site, use an arterial tourniquet or pursue/catch/kill the snake.
6. Never remove the bandage/splint before appropriate hospital evaluation.
7. Give a separate lone-person/no-contact decision that does not contradict the general stay-still rule.

### Anaphylaxis

1. Recognise any one severe airway, breathing or circulation sign as possible anaphylaxis; skin findings need not be present. Use cautious “can be” wording for severe persistent abdominal pain or repeated vomiting after a likely allergen until a clinician approves the lay threshold.
2. Use the person’s current ASCIA action plan and exact adrenaline product immediately; then call 000. A helper may call while it is given; if alone, adrenaline comes first.
3. Keep the person flat and never standing/walking, with ordered reviewed branches for difficult breathing, vomiting or unresponsiveness with normal breathing, pregnancy and infants/young children.
4. Five minutes after the first device, if severe signs have not improved, are still present or have returned, use the person's next adrenaline device, following their current action plan and that device's own instructions. Follow any directions from 000 and record the device and time.
5. If no adrenaline product exists, state that no medication substitute can be improvised; call 000, position correctly, prevent further exposure only when safe and without delay, monitor breathing and start CPR only when unresponsive **and** not breathing normally.
6. Keep EpiPen, Anapen, Jext and neffy instructions separate. IM-191–IM-193 are product neutral and contain no brand, dose, route, cap/end, activation, hold time, anatomical target, age/weight rule or ampoule-and-syringe method.

## Required paired treatment paths

### With a first-aid kit

Candidate capability classes, not yet approved kit entries:

- non-latex medical gloves and hand-cleaning capability;
- CPR face shield or pocket mask validated for intended use;
- AED where the activity/vehicle/location makes carriage realistic;
- trauma pad and conforming/pressure bandage;
- a genuine manufactured arterial tourniquet with visible instructions and prior training;
- haemostatic dressing only if selected, trained and clinically reviewed;
- at least two broad elasticised pressure-immobilisation bandages and splinting materials;
- marker and time-recording method;
- the at-risk individual’s correctly prescribed, in-date adrenaline product(s), exact current instructions and ASCIA action plan, kept immediately accessible rather than buried in the kit;
- insulation, shelter, communication and lighting needed to support continuing care.

Every selected item must receive a stable `KIT-` ID, exact capability, minimum quantity/size, storage and expiry requirement, counterfeit/provenance control, training prerequisite, scenario test and clinician sign-off. The phrase “comprehensive kit” will never imply that every commercial first-aid kit contains these items.

### Without a first-aid kit

This also applies if the kit is missing the item needed for the condition.

The no-kit branch is a different risk state, not an equivalent improvisation exercise:

- CPR: compressions and, if willing/able, breaths are not delayed while searching for a barrier or AED; emergency-operator guidance controls.
- Severe bleeding: hands and appropriate ordinary material may provide direct pressure; embedded objects stay in place. Improvised tourniquet wording remains blocked because failed constriction can worsen bleeding.
- Snakebite: ANZCOR permits broad clothing/other material when a suitable bandage is absent, but the exact wrap must be pressure-tested and reviewed; stillness and urgent rescue remain central.
- Anaphylaxis: there is no substitute for adrenaline. Correct positioning, 000, safe no-delay exposure prevention and breathing observation are supportive actions only; CPR begins only when the person is unresponsive and not breathing normally.

Every no-kit page must state what cannot safely be replaced and how missing equipment changes the urgency, duration of manual care or feasibility of self-rescue.

### Emergency / remote-medicine clinician

2. What CPR content is useful as a field refresher versus unsafe without practical instruction, especially for infants?
3. Which manufactured tourniquet and haemostatic-dressing skills, if any, belong in this personal guide and kit?
4. Is an improvised arterial tourniquet teachable here; if so, under what exact failure checks and training prerequisite?
5. What reassessment language should distinguish persistent bleeding, recurrence and shock without relying on a lay pulse measurement?
6. Which actions may be performed by the injured person when alone, and which require a rescuer?

### Clinical toxicologist / Australian envenomation specialist

1. Which pressure-immobilisation sequence should this guide teach given ANZCOR’s acknowledged uncertainty?
2. What bandage width, pressure cue and splint method can a novice reproduce reliably without pressure indicators?
3. Which clothing/material fallbacks are safe enough to illustrate, and which stretch/width properties fail?
4. How should the non-limb bite rule be shown without restricting breathing or applying pressure to head/neck?
5. How should a lone, completely out-of-contact person decide whether movement to seek help is unavoidable?
6. Does Safer Care Victoria’s overdue review date expose any material conflict with current practice?

### Allergy / anaphylaxis clinician

1. Confirm the recognition set and approve exact lay wording for severe persistent abdominal pain or repeated vomiting given the difference between simplified insect-sting wording and detailed 2026 any-cause guidance.
2. Confirm the ordered flat, legs-outstretched, side/recovery, pregnancy and infant/young-child branches and correct the vomiting inconsistency found in healthdirect.
3. Confirm that the IM-191–IM-193 set’s product-neutral “give their adrenaline product exactly as their plan/label shows” boundary is safe without a displayed dose, route, cap/end, activation, hold time or anatomical target.
4. Confirm the five-minute “not improved, remains or returns” wording and what, if anything, a lay guide may say about a different, expired or another person’s product.
5. Confirm the asthma overlap and that the card’s CPR transition must use unresponsive **and** not breathing normally despite the February ASCIA poster’s “or” wording.
6. Decide how many products an at-risk individual should carry for remote Victorian travel and what product-specific vehicle/pack storage, inspection and expiry rules are essential.

## Field and usability tests required

- Current accredited CPR/first-aid training for the founder before relying on the personal guide as a refresher.
- Timed, observed CPR/AED scenario using a training manikin and trainer AED; no live device discharge.
- Bleeding-control drills using artificial blood and task trainers; record time to effective pressure, pad errors, device error and loss of control during communication.
- Tourniquet practice only with a trainer/device and qualified instructor; no prolonged constriction on a person.
- Pressure-immobilisation trials on a healthy volunteer under a qualified instructor using pressure-feedback equipment, followed by delayed recall tests.
- Test proposed no-kit snakebite materials for width, sustained pressure, immobilisation and application error; reject rather than generalise poor materials.
- Combined IM-191–IM-193 scenarios covering cross-card navigation, no rash, GI ambiguity, asthma-like wheeze, attempted standing/walking, overlapping pregnancy/vomiting/child/breathing states, no product, failed 000 and no two-way contact both with and without a device, five-minute reassessment, improvement wrongly cancelling help and unresponsive normal versus abnormal breathing.
- Separate product-specific training with inert trainers and the exact current instructions for every product actually carried; no live adrenaline product and no transfer of one product’s steps to another.
- One-person scenarios for calling/PLB activation while maintaining pressure or patient position.
- Final-page tests in low light, rain protection/gloves and high stress; any recurring misread blocks the page.
