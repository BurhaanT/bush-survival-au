# Evidence packet EP-012 — Victorian fire law, use and extinguishment

## Research question

Check when a stranded reader in Victoria must not light a fire and which laws and site rules apply. Check how a permitted fire or stove must be watched and put out. Decide whether the guide can include fire for warmth, treating water, cooking or signalling for help. This packet does not yet cover what to do when caught in a bushfire.

This packet does not authorise a campfire, stove, signal fire, improvised fireplace, friction-fire technique, accelerant, fuel transfer or fire-based medical treatment. Fire status depends on the current district, day, land category, site rules, weather, fuel/appliance and purpose.

## Evidence strengths

### Current rules and location control

- A Total Fire Ban (TFB) is a legal restriction that may apply by district or statewide and can be declared at any time of year. It is distinct from the Fire Danger Rating and Fire Danger Period. Current CFA, VicEmergency, FFMVic, land-manager signs and closures control over a printed guide. [S-215, S-216, S-218, S-220]
- Campfires and fires for warmth or comfort are prohibited on TFB days. Fires cannot be left burning overnight into a TFB day. [S-215, S-217, S-219]
- Rules vary by land. Parks Victoria says national and state parks permit campfires only in purpose-built fireplaces provided by the park, while State forests have a different rule set and forest reserves allow campfires only in land-manager fireplaces. Local signs, closures and park-specific rules can be stricter. [S-216, S-217, S-218]
- Catastrophic fire danger can trigger closures of parks and state forests in the affected district. The current closure for the exact place controls; the guide must not claim every Victorian park is automatically closed. Official guidance says not to enter or remain in an affected Catastrophic-risk area. Campfire legality is never evidence that the broader location is safe. [S-218, S-220, S-299]

### Permission is not a recommendation

- CFA, Victoria Police and Parks Victoria all warn that small/unattended campfires can become bushfires and say not to light when uncertain or when conditions are unsafe. Parks material explicitly describes not lighting a fire as the safest option and says not to rely on fire as the primary heat/cooking system. [S-217, S-220, S-221]
- A survival fire consumes time, water, fuel and attention and can cause burns, smoke/CO exposure, loss of shelter/equipment, environmental damage or a new emergency. If the reader lacks a lawful site, sufficient extinguishing water or continuous competent supervision, the fire branch stops. [S-179, S-215, S-217, S-221]
- Need for warmth does not override TFB/site rules or make enclosed combustion safe. Prevention, dry insulation, ground insulation and non-combustion shelter remain the primary temperature controls. [S-057, S-061, S-179, S-217]

### Size, separation and supervision

- Current State-forest/CFA rules converge on a fire no greater than one metre by one metre, with at least three metres around and above clear of flammable material, a properly constructed fireplace or context-permitted trench, continuous supervision by a capable person and complete extinguishment before leaving. [S-215, S-216, S-219]
- During a Fire Danger Period, CFA also sets a wind condition of no more than 10 km/h for open cooking/comfort fire rules. Visual leaf movement is only an official practical indicator; it does not make a gusty site safe or measure local ember behaviour. [S-215, S-219]
- Parks guidance uses “adult within 50 m and line of sight”, while other official wording says watched at all times by someone able to extinguish quickly. A guide should not turn 50 m into a permitted working distance. The final wording should require uninterrupted direct attendance close enough for immediate control; exact legal phrasing remains in a current-rules box. [S-215, S-216, S-217]
- Tents and other flammable equipment belong outside the clearance. Overhead, root/peat/duff, slope, wind and ember paths also require review; a cleared circle alone does not establish a safe site. [S-215, S-216, S-221]

### Extinguishment

- Parks Victoria and the Conservation Regulator require water, not soil, because buried embers can remain hot and reignite. Parks says at least 10 litres must be available before lighting. [S-216, S-217]
- The fire must be fully out before sleep, even a short absence, relocation or abandonment. “No visible flame” is not extinguished. Official public wording uses cold/cool to touch; the final test must avoid placing a hand into ash/coals and should be physically reviewed. [S-216, S-217]
- The 10-litre rule is a precondition, not a guarantee that 10 litres will extinguish every fire. The fire remains small and the reader keeps adding water/mixing/checking until no heat remains under the final reviewed method. The guide must not promise that one bucket is always sufficient. [S-216, S-217]

### Gas/electric stoves and TFB complexity

- A commercially manufactured gas or electric outdoor cooking appliance may be used on a TFB day only under exact conditions: designed exclusively for meal preparation, stable, three-metre flammable-material clearance, continuous adult supervision/capacity to extinguish, 10 litres of water or ready hose and complete shutdown. Solid-fuel and liquid-fuel cooking are prohibited outdoors. [S-215, S-216, S-219]
- The TFB allowance is purpose-limited to meal preparation. It does not authorise gas flame for warmth, a signal, equipment drying or generic survival use. Whether boiling emergency drinking water counts as meal preparation is not established by the selected public wording; the guide will not assume it does. [S-215, S-216, S-219]
- Appliances remain outdoor-only. A legal TFB cooking appliance is still unsafe inside a tent, vehicle or enclosed/partly enclosed shelter. [S-179, S-180, S-215]
- Portable gas products can leak, overheat, explode or be recalled. Exact approval marking, recall check, manual, pot/vessel limits, connection/leak procedure, fuel storage and environmental testing are required. [S-222]

### Fuel and ignition

- Public-land firewood collection is restricted; standing trees/branches cannot be cut and parks may prohibit collecting wood/kindling. Carrying fuel from a lawful source avoids habitat damage and an unsafe search. [S-216, S-217]
- Petrol, oil or kerosene must not be used to help light a fire; flammable-liquid and gas systems require their exact manufacturer instructions and no refuelling while alight/hot. [S-219, S-222]
- A lighter, match or ferrocerium rod is not approved solely because it creates flame. Grip, wet/cold performance, accidental ignition, hot-vehicle storage, legal conditions and extinguishment capacity belong to the same capability test. Natural/friction methods add exertion and error and are not an initial priority.
- Parks Victoria's current packing list names brought firewood, kindling, a fire starter and waterproof/extra-long matches or a lighter. International government fire/survival guidance independently converges on preparing fuel first, keeping air gaps and moving from match-thin through pencil- and thumb-thick kindling only as each smaller stage burns. This supports a provisional solid-campfire-firelighter sequence but not a claim of Victorian approval or field reliability. The exact sequence remains behind live-fire and stressed-reader review. [S-455]

### Signalling fire

- No current Victorian authority found in this packet provides a general lost-person exemption allowing a signal fire during a TFB or at a prohibited site. CFA/FFMVic language treats open fire restrictions as legal controls, with formal permits for limited activities. [S-215, S-218, S-219]
- The guide therefore will not recommend a signal fire as an emergency override. PLB, phone/satellite communication, whistle, light and high-contrast material are the primary signalling candidates. Any life-threatening exceptional legal doctrine or responder-directed fire use requires formal Victorian legal/fire-agency review before appearing. [S-048, S-172, S-215, S-218]

## Important contradictions and operational tensions

### State forest freedom versus park fireplace-only rules

“You can light a campfire anywhere in a State forest” is dangerous when carried into a national/state park, forest reserve, campground with signs, closed land or a TFB. The reader may not know the tenure while lost. If current permission cannot be positively established, the fire branch stops. [S-216, S-217, S-218]

### Gas cooking may be legal on a TFB while heat/signalling fire is not

A gas stove can be conditionally legal for meal preparation, but that does not make it a warming or signalling device. The water chapter also cannot assume emergency boiling fits the purpose without authoritative clarification. The final matrix needs separate rows for fuel, purpose, land, restrictions and current conditions. [S-215, S-216, S-219]

### Water needed for survival is also required for extinguishment

The same limited clean/untreated-water reserve may be needed for drinking, cooling a burn and extinguishing. If adequate extinguishing water is not immediately available, official campfire conditions are not met. The guide must not advise a person to create an unextinguishable fire in order to boil a small amount of water or feel warmer. [S-094, S-203, S-216, S-217]

### Fire warmth can worsen cold survival

Searching for fuel, building a fireplace, exposing insulation to sparks/rain and staying awake to supervise may cost more heat than the fire supplies. A person with significant hypothermia should rest and be handled gently, not gather wood. Fire remains a conditional environmental tool, not a hypothermia treatment. [S-057, S-061, S-217]

### Clearance numbers can create false safety

One metre fire size and three metres clearance are legal/safety minima in selected contexts, not proof that a site with wind, roots, peat/duff, dry grass, overhead fuel or slope is safe. “Meets the numbers” cannot overrule “if in doubt, do not light.” [S-215, S-216, S-221]

### “Cool to touch” can invite a burn or shallow check

Official pages use this memorable test, but reaching into ash can burn and surface cooling may hide heat below. The final extinguishment visual needs a reviewed sequence using water, mixing/exposing material and cautious heat checking without bare-hand contact with ash or coals. [S-216, S-217]

## Candidate propositions under gate control

These are research propositions, not approved reader instructions.

1. Check current TFB, Fire Danger Rating/Period, land tenure, closure, park/forest page and on-site signs before any ignition; if status or permission is unknown, do not light.
2. Do not light a campfire on a TFB day or carry one into the TFB period; never use an open fire as the baseline warming, water-treatment or signalling plan.
3. Treat permission as only the first gate: stop if weather/wind/site, supervision, extinguishing water, fuel or personal condition is inadequate.
4. Where permitted, use the applicable provided/properly constructed fireplace, keep the fire within current size/clearance rules and under uninterrupted competent control.
5. Extinguish completely with water before sleep, movement or absence; soil, no flame and a surface-only check are insufficient.
6. Keep any stove/appliance outdoors and use it only for its exact lawful purpose and manufacturer-approved configuration.
7. Do not imply that a conditionally legal gas/electric cooker on a TFB can be used for warmth, signalling, drying or emergency water boiling without specific authority.
8. Permit only the provisional carried solid-firelighter plus lighter/waterproof-match sequence after every conservative gate passes. Exclude signal-fire, liquid/gas accelerant, fuel-transfer, friction-fire and improvised-fireplace instructions until their legal, safety and usability gates are met.

## With-equipment and without-equipment paths

### With selected equipment

- Current-status capability: live CFA/VicEmergency/land-manager information with an offline fallback that cannot falsely declare permission.
- A selected outdoor appliance must have its manual, approval/recall record, compatible vessel/fuel, stable base, clearances, shutdown and fire-control capability.
- Campfire capability requires enough immediately available water for the legal minimum and complete extinguishment, not merely drinking water promised for later; a non-combustible turning tool; an authorised fireplace; and dry, untreated fuel that is both lawfully obtained and expressly permitted at the exact site.
- The provisional ignition kit is one labelled solid campfire firelighter, a lighter or waterproof match and prepared match-thin, pencil-thick and thumb-thick kindling. Common-object sizes are technique aids, not legal measurements, and the exact sequence is not field-approved. [S-455]
- Carried lawful fuel, ignition and fire-control equipment do not authorise use; every upstream gate still applies.

### Without those supplies

- No status/permission: do not light.
- No required water, safe site, continuous capable watcher or lawful fireplace: do not light.
- No safe open-air appliance/vessel: use another validated water treatment; do not move combustion into shelter.
- No fire: prioritise wind/rain protection, ground insulation, dry layers, shade as relevant, communications and non-fire signalling. A static guide must not compensate by teaching an unreviewed friction fire or accelerant method.

## Visual requirements and prohibitions

Required candidate visuals:

- a current-status gate separating TFB, Fire Danger Rating/Period, land type, closure/sign and site/weather conditions;
- a purpose/fuel matrix distinguishing campfire, comfort heat, meal-preparation gas/electric, solid fuel, liquid fuel, water boiling and signalling without implying permanent legal permission;
- a permitted-fire site cross-section showing applicable fireplace, size/clearance minima and residual wind/root/duff/overhead/slope hazards;
- a water-only extinguishment sequence with deep heat recheck and no hand in coals;
- an outdoor-appliance panel linked to CO, stability, vessel and recall/manual checks;
- a no-fire survival path linking shelter/insulation, water backup and non-fire signalling.

Prohibitions:

- no static “fire allowed” Victorian map or calendar;
- no fire, stove or lantern inside a tent, vehicle, enclosed awning or partly enclosed shelter;
- no signal fire on a TFB/unknown-status branch and no legal-emergency exemption claim;
- no petrol, oil, kerosene or uncontrolled accelerant ignition;
- no image of a person leaving, sleeping or gathering wood while a fire burns;
- no soil-only extinguishment, bare hand placed in ash/coals, or “smoke stopped = out” cue;
- no one-metre/three-metre/10-litre numbers detached from their land, restriction and complete-extinguishment context;
- no AI-generated factual flame, ember, clearance or extinguishment image.

## Validation and review required

- CFA/FFMVic/Parks-aligned fire-law reviewer checks exact rule branches, land categories, TFB cooker purpose and current-information hierarchy.
- Victorian wildfire/field instructor reviews site, wind, fuel, supervision, extinction and escape implications.
- Product/gas-safety reviewer checks any appliance, connector, fuel and vessel; current recalls are rechecked before each edition.
- Remote-emergency clinician reviews cold/heat/burn and exertion trade-offs; fire is not labelled medical treatment.
- Physical tests occur only where/when authorised, under competent supervision, conservative weather and an extinguishment plan. No test occurs during a TFB, Fire Danger Period unless all rules are independently confirmed, or near a shelter.
- Fire extinction testing records water used, time, hidden heat, wind, substrate and recheck, but cannot relax official minima.
- Final layout testing checks that a reader cannot carry a gas-cooking exception into heat/signalling, a State-forest rule into a park, or one clearance number into unsafe conditions.
