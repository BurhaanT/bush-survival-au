# Evidence packet EP-014 — Victorian weather, warnings and environmental change

**Status:** Initial evidence packet complete; exact field wording and responses remain behind meteorological, emergency-management, fire, clinical and SAR review  
**Chapters:** 02 First minutes; 04 stay/move; 05 temperature/shelter; 08 fire; 09 navigation/signalling; 10 regions/seasons; 13 equipment  
**Dependencies:** EP-001, EP-002, EP-009, EP-010, EP-012 and EP-013  
**Source snapshot:** checked 6 September 2026

## Research question

Define how a stranded person anywhere in Victoria should use forecasts, warnings, incident information and observed environmental change without:

1. mistaking the absence of a warning for safety;
2. assuming a radar image, app or map shows exactly what is happening on the ground now;
3. relying on one battery, network or information channel;
4. converting weather lore into a prediction method;
5. letting “stay put” override a live flood, fire, lightning, tree or exposure threat; or
6. encouraging unplanned movement merely because conditions feel uncomfortable.

This packet does not teach weather forecasting, storm chasing, lightning shelter positions, what to do when trapped by bushfire, flood crossing, smoke-mask selection, alpine travel, marine forecasting or medical treatment. Those subjects need separate safety reviews. This packet checks how to use weather information and when changed conditions mean the plan needs checking again.

## Core finding

Weather information has three complementary layers:

1. **Forecast risk:** what authoritative services say may happen in the exact area and time.
2. **Official event information:** current BOM warnings, VicEmergency warning/action statement, fire rating/TFB, incident update, closure and responder instruction.
3. **Observed change:** what is actually occurring at the person's site—thunder, smoke/ash/embers, sudden wind, falling debris, intense rain, rising/noisy/dirty water, heat/cold, fog/whiteout or deteriorating visibility.

None substitutes for the other two. A person may have dangerous local conditions without an official warning, and an official warning remains actionable even when the phone screen, sky or radar image looks reassuring [S-185, S-291, S-292].

## Evidence strengths

### Forecasts and warnings are location- and time-specific

- BOM provides Victorian warnings, district/local forecasts, Australian Alps information, observations, rainfall/river data, radar and forecast maps through separate products. The relevant issue time, validity period, location and warning detail matter; one city icon or old screenshot is not the forecast for an entire route or delayed rescue period [S-291].
- Warning detail includes the hazard, affected area/time, observed/forecast development and recommended actions. The guide should lead to the full warning, not reduce it to an icon or headline [S-291].
- Forecast uncertainty is not failure. Conditions may change between issues and local terrain can produce different wind, rain, temperature, cloud and visibility from a nearby town. Broad Victorian coverage therefore requires the exact trip area, not fixed stereotypes for “the High Country”, “the Mallee” or “the coast” [S-185, S-188, S-291].

### No warning is not a safe signal

- BOM does not warn for every thunderstorm; all thunderstorms produce lightning, but severe-thunderstorm warnings are for defined severe or very dangerous phenomena. Remote/unpopulated warning services can be more limited where monitoring data are sparse [S-071, S-291].
- VicEmergency explicitly says not to rely on one source or wait for an official warning before leaving. Its incident area is a time-stamped representation that may not show the current extent [S-292].
- A grey/no-warning screen may mean no current warning was issued or retained for the chosen location; it does not mean no hazard exists. App notification settings, watch zones, operating system, battery, network, profile and data refresh can all affect what the user sees [S-292].

The final design must never use green, a tick or the word `SAFE` for “no current warning”. The correct state is `No current warning shown — keep checking conditions and other sources`.

### Radar is evidence, not clearance or a route planner

- Weather radar may miss low precipitation, precipitation far from the radar or weather hidden by terrain; it can show precipitation aloft that evaporates before reaching the ground and can also show non-rain echoes. Its useful range and representation vary [S-185, S-291].
- BOM says radar is one tool to combine with forecasts, warnings, observations and satellite information. If a warning exists but radar appears empty, the warning controls [S-291].
- A coloured cell or animation does not establish where a flash flood, lightning strike, wind damage or safe travel corridor will occur. The book should not teach the user to thread a route between radar colours.

### Warning level does not replace the action statement

VicEmergency uses three Australian Warning System levels [S-292]:

- **Advice:** an incident is occurring or has occurred; obtain information and monitor conditions.
- **Watch and Act / Warning:** an emergency is developing nearby; take action now to protect people.
- **Emergency Warning:** imminent danger; take action immediately.

Evacuation or other action statements sit within those levels. The exact message may tell the reader to leave, prepare, avoid, shelter or monitor. The book must say to read/hear the entire current message and follow its action statement; it must not map each colour to one universal bush action.

The information is still not a guaranteed rescue path. A warning polygon, incident extent and evacuation area do not prove a road, bridge, track or improvised route is passable. Movement remains subject to the exact message, current closures, live terrain and responder direction [S-182-S-185, S-292].

### One channel is a foreseeable common failure

- Emergency Victoria recommends more than one source. VicEmergency, BOM and online pages share phone, power and network dependencies. ABC/local designated emergency radio offers a differently failing broadcast path, but radio reception also varies by location, terrain and weather [S-292, S-295].
- A static frequency list ages and will not suit every Victorian location. The trip plan should identify the current official broadcaster/frequency for the actual area and test reception before departure; exact radio selection belongs in the equipment gate [S-295].
- Social-media reposts, screenshots and group messages can be delayed, cropped or detached from issue time/action text. Use them only to reach the originating official warning, never as the final authority.
- A downloaded forecast or screenshot is historical evidence once connectivity is lost. It helps show the last known forecast and issue time but cannot truthfully show that no new warning exists.

### Fire danger, fire occurrence and fire prohibition are different

- The Fire Danger Rating describes how dangerous a fire could be if one started. It is not evidence that a fire exists and is not the same as a Total Fire Ban [S-215, S-218, S-293].
- On forecast Catastrophic days, CFA's public action is to leave bushfire-risk areas and not be there; on Extreme days, leaving early is the safest option and travel through bushfire-risk areas should be reconsidered [S-293]. These are pre-exposure controls, not instructions for somebody already trapped by fire.
- Wind strongly affects fire speed, direction, intensity and spotting, and a wind change can turn a flank into a broad fire front. CFA describes a common Victorian north/north-west then south-west pattern, but “common” is not an incident forecast or a rule for choosing an escape direction [S-293].
- Smoke, ash, embers or a large smoke column are reasons to suspect/check a fire and avoid continuing towards it. Smoke colour, smell or apparent wind direction does not locate the fire edge, forecast the next wind change or identify a safe route. Exact caught-in-fire/vehicle actions require their own current CFA/SAR review and are deliberately excluded here [S-293, S-294].

### Smoke is both information and exposure—not a navigation method

- Bushfire smoke contains particles and gases. Health effects vary with exposure and the person's health; people with heart/lung conditions, asthma, diabetes, pregnancy, very young or older age may be more vulnerable [S-294].
- Victorian Health says to check fire warnings when smoke haze is seen. EPA air data can inform exposure but changes with weather and monitoring coverage. Neither an air-quality category nor the absence of a nearby monitor proves the fire is distant or travel is safe [S-294].
- An ordinary cloth, paper mask or bandanna is not protection from fine smoke particles. A properly fitted P2/N95 can reduce some particle exposure but does not protect from carbon monoxide, radiant heat, flame or all gases, and may burden breathing. The personal medical plan and exact mask/equipment review control; this packet does not create a generic “put on a mask and move” instruction [S-294].
- Reducing exertion can reduce inhaled dose, but staying in the path of fire is not a smoke-health solution. The live fire warning, radiant heat/embers and responder instructions outrank general smoke advice.

### Observed change reopens the emergency decision

The following are **reassessment triggers**, not amateur prediction rules:

- thunder or lightning;
- smoke, ash, embers, flame, a substantial smoke column or rapidly worsening visibility;
- sudden/gusty wind, falling branches/debris or audible tree failure;
- intense rain locally or upstream information, rising/noisy/dirty water, water entering a dry channel or a route losing clearance;
- hail, rapid temperature fall/rise, worsening cold/wet exposure or heat stress;
- fog, low cloud, snow, dust or smoke obscuring terrain/location; or
- an official warning, action statement, closure or responder instruction.

On any trigger, stop the current task if stopping is safe, account for people, preserve location/communications, read the exact available information and compare the live hazards. The default is to build protection at the known site. Movement occurs only when a defined immediate threat, essential lifesaving care or current responder direction outweighs the loss of shelter/findability, and then uses the least movement that solves that threat [S-021, S-067-S-071, S-169, S-182-S-189, S-292].

This packet does not yet approve the exact response to lightning with no substantial shelter, a person already surrounded by floodwater, fire overrun, vehicle entrapment, whiteout travel or a seriously ill person who cannot move. Those branches remain visibly blocked rather than filled with folklore.

## Contradictions and traps

### “Stay where you are” versus worsening conditions

Victoria Police's lost-person default favours stopping, sheltering and staying findable. Flood, fire, falling trees, lightning and severe exposure can make the present site unsafe. The solution is not a broad licence to self-evacuate: first seek protection at or immediately beside the known position; if a live hazard cannot be controlled there, move together only as far as necessary to a more defensible known place and report/record the change when possible. Exact hazard branches need Victorian SAR and operational review [S-021, S-067-S-071, S-182-S-189].

### “I checked before leaving” versus a delayed incident

A correct morning forecast can be overtaken by a later warning, local storm, wind change, upstream rainfall or multi-day delay. Pre-trip checking is necessary but not continuing awareness. The equipment/plan must support rechecking; when it cannot, uncertainty increases conservative protection and earlier trip modification—it does not make the old forecast current [S-185, S-291, S-292].

### Phone alerts versus app configuration

Having the app icon is not a working warning system. Watch zone, hazard settings, notification permission, device sound/focus state, account/profile, data, battery and exact trip area must be tested. Even a successful test shares phone damage/network/power failures with maps and communications [S-292].

### Current incident map versus actual extent

Map polygons are time-stamped information, not fences. A marker outside the user's pixel does not prove absence of smoke, embers, floodwater or wind damage; a marker inside does not specify the safe direction of movement. Read the update time and action statement and compare with observed conditions [S-292].

### Radar animation versus future conditions

Radar shows recent/observed echoes with limitations, not a guaranteed future track. Mountains can block the beam; distance changes beam height; false/missing echoes occur. Do not infer “the gap will remain open”, “rain is only light” or “the creek cannot rise” from one animation [S-185, S-291].

### Bush smell/animal/cloud lore versus official evidence

The packet found no need for folk signs such as animal behaviour, red sky, cloud rings, aching joints or smoke-colour rules. Some observable changes are genuinely important, but their safe use is to trigger a check and protection—not to predict timing, severity or direction. Any later environmental-interpretation entry must identify a specific evidence-backed decision it improves and pass the same consequence gate.

### Warning colour versus exact instruction

The same warning level can carry different action statements. A simple colour ladder is memorable but can cause a reader to take yesterday's action for today's different hazard. Display the current level, hazard, place, issue/update time and action text together; never teach colour alone.

### Smoke shelter versus heat stress and direct fire

Closing a building/vehicle can reduce smoke entry but may worsen heat, and a tent is not a smoke-cleaner or fire shelter. P2 protection is partial and does not handle carbon monoxide. This is a compound decision requiring fire, health, heat, shelter and vehicle guidance; the book must not turn general community indoor-smoke advice into a stranded-bush guarantee [S-179, S-294].

## Candidate decision architecture

### Before entering bush country

1. Define exact route/area, date/time, elevation/coast/water context and nearby escape/closure information.
2. Check full BOM local/district forecast and warnings, not just one icon; record issue time.
3. Check VicEmergency warning/incident details, CFA FDR/TFB and relevant land-manager closures/signs.
4. Read action statements; modify/cancel where current official guidance calls for leaving/avoiding the risk area. Do not enter a bushfire-risk area on a forecast Catastrophic day [S-293].
5. Set and test VicEmergency watch zone(s) around the trip/approach area, notification permission and device power/data.
6. Identify a current official emergency broadcaster/frequency for that exact region and a battery-independent or separately powered receiver candidate.
7. Tell the responsible contact the route/vehicle/party/return trigger and record the last forecast/warning issue time; this does not replace PLB/satellite/mobile plans.

No step is approved as a field-ready checklist until exact app screens, terminology and cross-agency responsibilities are tested at publication time.

### While delayed, lost or stranded

At the normal recheck loop:

1. observe weather, water, smoke, trees, visibility and each person's condition without interrupting lifesaving care;
2. retrieve the newest available BOM/VicEmergency/land-manager/responder information and read its place, issue/update time and action statement;
3. compare it with at least one differently delivered official source when practical;
4. record material change and the time/source;
5. protect at the known site where possible; and
6. if a live hazard forces relocation, keep the group together, take critical on-person equipment, record/mark/report the old and new positions and stop at the first defensible place rather than beginning an open-ended walkout.

The final guide must not prescribe a universal recheck interval. Warning issue cycles, battery/network constraints, the changing hazard and casualty care differ. Event-triggered checks plus a human-factors-tested reminder are safer than an invented “every 30 minutes” promise.

### Information confidence labels

Every weather/warning display in the guide should distinguish:

- `LIVE / CURRENT`: successfully retrieved now, with issue/update time shown;
- `LAST RETRIEVED`: cached or screenshotted, with retrieval and issue time;
- `OBSERVED HERE`: direct site observation, time and location;
- `REPORTED BY RESPONDER`: direct instruction/message, exact wording/time; and
- `UNKNOWN / UNAVAILABLE`: no current source—never rendered as no hazard.

The book itself is always background information, never a live warning source.

## With communications equipment / without it

This is not medical treatment, but the same paired-access principle is valuable.

### With a working connected phone/radio

- use official BOM and VicEmergency details, current land/fire pages and exact responder messages;
- confirm location/watch zone and issue time rather than assuming device location is correct;
- preserve power and keep PLB/two-way distress roles separate from passive warning monitoring;
- listen for the full action statement and updates; and
- record an enforced relocation in the rescue message/location log.

### Without current connectivity or a working receiver

- treat the last retrieved forecast/warning as stale, not reassurance;
- increase attention to direct environmental triggers and use the existing live-hazard/stay-move gate;
- do not attempt cloud, animal, smoke or radar-memory forecasting;
- do not move solely to chase reception or a view unless the separate stay/move and terrain gates approve the smallest safe attempt;
- use PLB or other exact distress system when its emergency threshold is met; and
- prioritise non-combustion shelter, temperature control, flood/fire/tree/lightning site reassessment and findability.

The no-connectivity path cannot recreate warnings. It must openly state that the capability is absent.

## Visual requirements and prohibitions

Required candidate visuals:

- a three-layer `forecast risk → official event information → observed change` model with arrows back to reassessment;
- a warning card anatomy showing hazard, place, level, action statement, issue/update time and source;
- a `NO CURRENT WARNING ≠ SAFE` state and a `LAST RETRIEVED` stale state;
- a multi-channel dependency diagram: phone/app/web share network/power; separately powered local emergency radio differs but has reception/current-frequency limits;
- a radar-limit panel showing mountain blockage, beam height/distance, virga and false echoes without suggesting the reader interpret storm severity;
- an observed-trigger matrix linking to the canonical fire, flood, lightning, falling-tree, cold/heat, visibility/navigation and medical pages;
- a stay/protect/minimum-relocate flow with known/reported positions; and
- regional scenario panels driven by exact forecast and landform, not caricatured seasonal calendars.

Prohibitions:

- no green tick, `all clear` or `safe` attached to no warning/no incident/no radar echo;
- no screenshot without visible issue/retrieval time and clear stale-state treatment;
- no copied BOM/VicEmergency map/icon/forecast asset until licensing/attribution is resolved;
- no static warning polygon, statewide radio-frequency table or “always” wind-change arrow;
- no route drawn between radar cells, across a warning polygon or toward apparent clearer sky;
- no animal/cloud/red-sky/smoke-colour prediction chart;
- no lightning crouch, tree-distance, flood crossing, fire escape, mask-fit or vehicle-overrun diagram from this packet;
- no hard-top vehicle icon that implies universal protection from fire, flood, falling trees, heat, exhaust or road hazards; and
- no AI-generated weather, smoke, terrain or warning screenshot used as factual evidence.

## Claims that may advance to specialist review

1. Use forecast risk, current official event information and observed change as complementary layers.
2. Read the full warning/action statement, location and issue/update time; colour/icon alone is insufficient.
3. No warning, no notification and no radar echo are not safety clearance.
4. Use more than one official channel; phone/app/web share failure and a radio path has separate reception/current-frequency limits.
5. Radar is an observation tool with coverage/beam/topography/echo limits and never overrules a warning.
6. Treat thunder, smoke/ash/embers, sudden wind/tree failure, intense rain/rising water, temperature/visibility deterioration and official messages as reassessment triggers, not prediction methods.
7. Preserve the known site and findability unless a defined live hazard, lifesaving need or responder instruction requires the least necessary relocation.
8. Distinguish FDR, TFB and an active incident; do not enter bushfire-risk areas on Catastrophic days and reconsider travel/leave early under current Extreme guidance.
9. Do not use typical wind changes, smoke direction/colour, air-quality data or P2 masks to locate a fire or clear movement.
10. Mark cached information `LAST RETRIEVED`, and unknown current status `UNKNOWN`, never `safe`.

## Required review and testing

- BOM-qualified meteorological reviewer: forecast/warning/radar limits, observed-trigger wording and regional transfer.
- VicEmergency/VICSES-aligned emergency-information reviewer: warning levels/action statements, app/watch-zone/currentness and multi-channel flow.
- CFA/FFMVic/Parks-aligned fire/SAR reviewers: FDR/TFB/incident distinctions, smoke/wind-change boundary and live-hazard relocation.
- Victorian public-health/respiratory clinician: smoke, exertion, sensitive-person and mask wording; link to person-specific asthma/cardiac plans.
- Victorian land/aviation SAR and qualified navigation reviewers: visibility loss, reception-seeking and reported-location movement.
- Human-factors testing on exact current BOM/VicEmergency/app/radio interfaces with wrong location, stale screenshot, disabled notification, no data, low power, backgrounded app, map-boundary, warning/radar conflict and multiple concurrent hazards.
- Scenario testing across alpine/forest, western/northern heat/fire, coast, floodplain/gorge and vehicle-separated settings without exposing anyone to storms, fire, smoke, flood, extreme temperatures or dangerous terrain.
- Final source/currentness check immediately before edition release and a prominent statement that the book is not a warning service.

## Decision

The packet supports a weather-and-warning reassessment spine, not a field weather-prediction chapter. The guide should show three complementary layers, require full current action statements and issue times, expose app/radar/network failures, preserve multiple official channels and turn direct environmental change into a prompt to re-run existing hazard decisions. It should not teach lore, radar routing, smoke-direction escape, static warning areas/frequencies, or an unreviewed no-shelter lightning/fire/flood response. All reader-facing wording remains provisional until the named reviewers and interface tests approve it.
