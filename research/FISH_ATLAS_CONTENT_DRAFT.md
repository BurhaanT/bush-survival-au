### What this small atlas can do

> [!WARNING]
> **Atlas-limit wording confidence: 88%.** This is confidence in the boundary stated here, not the probability that a reader can identify a fish. A photograph, colour patch or common name is never a guarantee.

When its plates and reviews are complete, this atlas may help a reader:

- compare a fish with several positive features of European carp, redfin or black bream;
- notice selected morphs and lookalike traps;
- find the current rule that must be checked for a positive identification;
- measure a scale fish from the tip of its **closed-mouth snout** to the end of its tail; and
- stop when the evidence does not support a positive identification.

It cannot:

- identify every Victorian freshwater, estuarine or marine fish;
- prove identity from one photograph, colour, fin or common name;
- identify a hybrid with certainty;
- replace the current Victorian rules, notices, signs or health advice;
- prove that a legal fish is safe to eat;
- show how to restrain, dispatch, bleed, clean or fillet a fish; or
- make an unknown fish safe to keep or eat.

The measurement rule comes from [S-459: VFA fishing definitions](https://vfa.vic.gov.au/recreational-fishing/recreational-fishing-guide/fishing-definitions). Identity, legal take and food safety are separate gates.

## Strict entry gate — complete this before casting

> [!DANGER]
> **Pre-cast gate wording confidence: 91%.** This is a conservative project synthesis of the checked sources, not a rule published in this exact sequence by VFA. The percentage is **not** the probability that fishing, identification or later handling will be safe.

Do not cast until every answer below is **YES**.

1. **Are rescue, immediate danger, first aid, exposure, shelter and water already under control?** Food is usually a later priority.
2. **Can you remain in a safe place?** Do not trade a stable rescue position for a flooded bank, surf rock, current, steep drop or cold-water entry.
3. **Have you checked the current rule for this exact water and date?** Check licence or exemption, open water, method, equipment, bait, season, protected species, size, bag and possession limits, notices, signs and health warnings.
4. **Do you know which fish are reasonably likely here?** This three-species atlas is not enough where other catches are likely and unfamiliar.
5. **Can you identify each likely catch and apply its lawful disposition?** There is no safe universal action for an unknown fish. A protected fish, a fish outside the legal size range or other applicable limit, or an excess fish normally needs prompt release, while a positively identified European carp must not be returned alive.
6. **Can you measure and handle the likely catch without entering the water or exposing anyone to hooks, line, teeth or spines?**
7. **If keeping food is possible, do you already have the species-specific humane dispatch and cleaning competence that this guide withholds?** The law that requires a confirmed carp to be killed does not supply the missing humane method.

If any answer is **NO**, do not fish. Use carried food and keep working on rescue and exposure control.

This gate is a project synthesis based on [S-459: VFA rules and definitions](https://vfa.vic.gov.au/recreational-fishing/recreational-fishing-guide), [S-460: VFA European carp rule](https://vfa.vic.gov.au/recreational-fishing/recreational-fishing-guide/catch-limits-and-closed-seasons/types-of-fish/freshwater-scale-fish/european-carp), [S-461: VFA redfin rule](https://vfa.vic.gov.au/recreational-fishing/recreational-fishing-guide/catch-limits-and-closed-seasons/types-of-fish/freshwater-scale-fish/redfin), [S-462: VFA bream rule](https://vfa.vic.gov.au/recreational-fishing/recreational-fishing-guide/catch-limits-and-closed-seasons/types-of-fish/marine-and-estuarine-scale-fish/bream-all-species) and [S-465: VFA responsible-fishing guidance](https://vfa.vic.gov.au/recreational-fishing/recreational-fishing-guide/responsible-fishing-behaviours).

## If the fish is unknown

> [!DANGER]
> **Unknown-fish wording confidence: 91%.** This confidence applies to the reason for stopping. It is **not** a probability that any single kill-or-release action is lawful. The checked sources do not support one universal action for every unidentified fish.

1. Keep the fish in the water where possible while making a prompt identification check.
2. Minimise handling. Do not assume an unfamiliar fish is a pest.
3. Do not treat it as food or intentionally retain it for food.
4. Do not apply the carp rule unless the fish is positively identified as European carp.
5. Check a current official source immediately. Contact VFA on **136 186** when communication is available.

This atlas deliberately does not tell you to kill or release every unknown fish. Either action can be wrong. If identity and the required action cannot be resolved promptly, especially when communication is unavailable, **this draft has no safe terminal instruction**. That unresolved live-fish branch blocks reader-facing integration. It is also why the pre-cast gate requires the likely catches and their rules to be known first. See [S-459: VFA Recreational Fishing Guide](https://vfa.vic.gov.au/recreational-fishing/recreational-fishing-guide), [S-460: European carp](https://vfa.vic.gov.au/recreational-fishing/recreational-fishing-guide/catch-limits-and-closed-seasons/types-of-fish/freshwater-scale-fish/european-carp) and [S-465: VFA responsible fishing](https://vfa.vic.gov.au/recreational-fishing/recreational-fishing-guide/responsible-fishing-behaviours).

## European carp

**Scientific name:** *Cyprinus carpio*  
**Working atlas status:** candidate only; not approved for first-time field identification

> [!DANGER]
> **Carp-entry wording confidence: 90%.** This estimate is confidence in the written summary of the checked sources. It is **not** the probability that a fish is carp. A false positive could cause a person to kill a fish that should have been released. Apply the must-kill rule only after a positive identification.

### Where it occurs in Victoria

VFA records carp as widespread and common through much of south-eastern Victoria and the Murray–Darling system, with populations in other western and south-western waters. VFA records it as absent east of the Snowy River. Do not shorten this to “statewide”. See [S-460: VFA carp species account](https://vfa.vic.gov.au/education/fish-species/carp).

### Positive features to check together

Do not identify carp from colour or body depth alone. In the ordinary scaled form, check for this combination:

- stocky, moderately deep body;
- small eye;
- blunt snout;
- thick-lipped mouth that can extend forward;
- ordinarily four whisker-like barbels: one longer barbel at each corner of the mouth and one shorter barbel at each end of the upper lip;
- one long-based dorsal fin; and
- normally large, thick scales.

The working plate must require several features to agree. If the mouth, barbels or fin arrangement cannot be seen, stop. Sources: [S-460: VFA carp species account](https://vfa.vic.gov.au/education/fish-species/carp) and [S-463: Fishes of Australia — European carp](https://fishesofaustralia.net.au/home/species/3964).

### Morphs and lookalike traps

- **Mirror carp** may have only a few very large, scattered scales.
- **Leather carp** may have few or no scales.
- **Carp–goldfish hybrids** may have weak or no barbels.
- **Goldfish** normally lack barbels, but “no barbels” does not by itself prove that a fish is goldfish because hybrids complicate that comparison.

Do not stretch the ordinary-form checklist to fit a scaleless or no-barbel fish. Until the morph and hybrid plate passes specialist review, treat that fish as unresolved. Compare [S-460: VFA carp](https://vfa.vic.gov.au/education/fish-species/carp) with [S-463: Fishes of Australia — carp](https://fishesofaustralia.net.au/home/species/3964) and [goldfish](https://fishesofaustralia.net.au/home/species/1855).

### Handling hazards and limits

The atlas does not yet provide a reviewed carp-specific grip or restraint. A hooked fish can move suddenly while the hook and line remain under tension. Keep people clear of the hook and line, keep fingers away from the mouth and gill openings, and do not enter the water to land or recover it. These are general controls, not a carp-dispatch method. See [S-465: VFA responsible fishing](https://vfa.vic.gov.au/recreational-fishing/recreational-fishing-guide/responsible-fishing-behaviours).

### Current Victorian legal disposition — checked 28 September 2026

- No minimum legal size.
- No bag limit.
- A **positively identified** European carp must be killed immediately.
- It must not be returned alive.
- Do not leave it on the bank.
- Possessing, transporting or releasing live carp, or using live carp as bait, is prohibited.
- VFA says a dead carp may be taken away or its carcass may be cut so that it sinks before return to the water.

The dead-carp disposal option is a narrow species-specific exception. Do not apply it to an unknown fish, another introduced species or ordinary fish waste. This page states the rule but gives **no killing, cutting or disposal technique**. If the reader does not already have a humane, species-specific way to comply, the pre-cast gate has failed. Source: [S-460: VFA European carp limits](https://vfa.vic.gov.au/recreational-fishing/recreational-fishing-guide/catch-limits-and-closed-seasons/types-of-fish/freshwater-scale-fish/european-carp); ordinary fish-waste comparison in [S-465: VFA catch food safety](https://vfa.vic.gov.au/recreational-fishing/recreational-fishing-guide/food-safety).

### Required views before this entry can be used

1. Fresh whole fish in a true side view, with the entire dorsal-fin base visible.
2. Front-oblique and side close-ups of the thick-lipped mouth extending forward and all four ordinary barbels.
3. Close-up of normal large, thick scales.
4. Separate verified examples of ordinary, mirror and leather forms.
5. Goldfish beside carp, with the mouth and barbel region equally clear.
6. A verified carp–goldfish hybrid example showing why weak or absent barbels do not resolve identity.
7. A map that shows VFA's reported distribution limit — absent east of the Snowy River — without presenting it as a hard biological or legal boundary or implying presence in every other water.

Every image needs specimen identity, locality where available, creator, original file page, licence and image-specific diagnostic limits. Candidate rights leads are recorded under S-463; none is approved merely because it appears online.

## Redfin / English perch

**Scientific name:** *Perca fluviatilis*  
**Working atlas status:** candidate only; not approved for first-time field identification

> [!WARNING]
> **Redfin-entry wording confidence: 92%.** This estimate is confidence in the written summary, not the probability of a correct identification, lawful retention or safe outcome. Do not identify the fish from its red fins or dark bands alone.

### Where it occurs in Victoria

VFA records redfin as widespread and common in many Victorian lakes and slower waters. It records only a few Gippsland localities and notes that redfin are absent from fast water and are usually absent from high-altitude areas. This is not a water-by-water presence map. See [S-461: VFA English-perch species account](https://vfa.vic.gov.au/education/fish-species/english-perch).

### Positive features to check together

Check for the combination below:

- deep body that is fairly narrow from side to side;
- large head;
- large mouth reaching to about the eye;
- two clearly separate dorsal fins, with a spiny first dorsal;
- five or more broad, dark vertical bands;
- a distinct dark blotch near the rear of the first dorsal fin; and
- red or orange lower fins—the paired pelvic fins under the front half and the single anal fin underneath nearer the tail—and colour on the outer or lower tail area.

The two separate dorsal fins, first-dorsal blotch and body-band pattern must be shown clearly. Colour alone is not enough. Sources: [S-461: VFA redfin limits and species account](https://vfa.vic.gov.au/recreational-fishing/recreational-fishing-guide/catch-limits-and-closed-seasons/types-of-fish/freshwater-scale-fish/redfin) and [S-463: Fishes of Australia — redfin](https://fishesofaustralia.net.au/home/species/3690).

### Morph and lookalike traps

This small atlas does not yet contain a full redfin lookalike key. A fish with only orange-red fins, only vertical bands, or only a deep body has not passed the entry. If the two-dorsal arrangement, first-dorsal blotch and the rest of the feature set cannot all be checked, stop and treat identity as unresolved.

The final test set must include juveniles, dull specimens, partly folded fins and non-redfin fish with bars or warm-coloured fins. This is a proposed test requirement, not a claim that all redfin change in one predictable way.

### Handling hazards and limits

The first dorsal fin is spiny. The gill cover also has a strong spine, called an opercular spine. Both need explicit close-ups and hazard labels. Keep hands clear of those structures and the hook. The atlas still needs an experienced Victorian fishing educator to approve any exact hand placement; none is supplied here. Sources: [S-461: VFA redfin account](https://vfa.vic.gov.au/education/fish-species/english-perch) and [S-463: Fishes of Australia — redfin](https://fishesofaustralia.net.au/home/species/3690).

### Current Victorian legal disposition — checked 28 September 2026

- No minimum legal size.
- No bag limit.
- VFA encourages anglers **not** to return caught redfin because of their effects on native fish.
- That encouragement is not the same as the carp rule. Do not rewrite it as “illegal to release” or “must kill”.
- Transporting live fish without VFA approval is illegal.

A retained redfin still needs positive identity, current exact-water clearance, humane dispatch competence, safe handling, cold storage and safe cooking. This atlas supplies none of the missing dispatch or cleaning method. Source: [S-461: VFA redfin limits](https://vfa.vic.gov.au/recreational-fishing/recreational-fishing-guide/catch-limits-and-closed-seasons/types-of-fish/freshwater-scale-fish/redfin).

### Required views before this entry can be used

1. Fresh whole fish in a true side view with both dorsal fins raised and separated.
2. Close-up of the first dorsal spines and the dark rear blotch.
3. Close-up of the spine on the gill cover (opercular spine), shown as a hand hazard rather than a gripping point.
4. Clear view of five or more broad vertical bands.
5. Clear views of pelvic, anal and tail colour, labelled as supporting rather than decisive features.
6. Juvenile, dull-coloured and folded-fin examples.
7. Verified non-redfin comparison images that share one tempting feature, such as bars or orange fins.

Candidate rights leads appear under S-463. File-level rights, provenance and diagnostic review are still required.

## Black bream, with yellowfin-bream comparison

**Black bream:** *Acanthopagrus butcheri*  
**Yellowfin bream:** *Acanthopagrus australis*  
**Working atlas status:** candidate comparison only; not approved for first-time field identification

> [!DANGER]
> **Bream-entry wording confidence: 82%.** This lower estimate reflects difficult separation, juvenile colour overlap, hybridisation and a current wording conflict about filleting. It is confidence in this cautious summary, **not** the probability of a correct species identification, legal retention or safe meal. Fin colour by itself must never decide the species.

### Where black bream occurs in Victoria

Black bream occur in Victorian coastal drainages, especially estuaries, coastal lakes and lower river reaches. This habitat statement does not prove that a particular fish is black bream. See [S-462: VFA black-bream species account](https://vfa.vic.gov.au/education/fish-species/black-bream).

The yellowfin-bream panel is a comparison aid where the fishes may be confused. It is not a complete Victorian yellowfin distribution map and must not use place alone to force an identification. See [S-464: Fishes of Australia — yellowfin bream](https://fishesofaustralia.net.au/home/species/672).

### Positive black-bream features to check together

Check for the combination below:

- robust, deep body;
- sharply rounded snout;
- one continuous dorsal fin;
- large scales;
- visible lateral line running along the side;
- gold-brown, bronze or olive upper body with a pale belly and possible greenish reflections when fresh; and
- usually dusky-brown pelvic, anal and tail fins.

These are comparison features, not a stand-alone legal key. **The present evidence packet supplies no verified non-colour feature that lets a novice reliably separate black bream from yellowfin bream.** Until a fish-identification specialist supplies and tests that separation, this entry cannot support a species-specific Gippsland black-bream decision. Sources: [S-462: VFA black-bream account](https://vfa.vic.gov.au/education/fish-species/black-bream) and [S-464: Fishes of Australia — black bream](https://fishesofaustralia.net.au/home/species/674).

### Yellowfin, juvenile and hybrid traps

- Yellowfin bream usually have yellow pelvic and anal fins.
- Juvenile black bream may also have yellowish fins.
- Black and yellowfin bream can be difficult to distinguish.
- The two species hybridise in parts of their range.
- “Black bream” is also used for unrelated fish. Every plate and search must show the scientific name *Acanthopagrus butcheri*.

Therefore:

- do not use yellow versus dusky fins as the sole decision;
- do not let a common-name web search choose an anatomy image;
- do not treat a likely hybrid as positively identified; and
- do not keep or eat a bream whose food identity remains unresolved.

Sources: [S-464: Fishes of Australia — black bream](https://fishesofaustralia.net.au/home/species/674), [yellowfin bream](https://fishesofaustralia.net.au/home/species/672), and the common-name anatomy trap recorded under [S-467: unrelated “black seabream” result](https://www.ikijime.com/fish/bream-black/).

### Handling hazards and limits

This draft has no reviewed black- or yellowfin-bream grip. The hook, line and fins remain puncture risks during identification and release. Use wet hands, support the fish horizontally and keep handling brief where release is required, but do not infer an exact hand placement from this text. A fish-identification educator must mark any hazardous structure on the final verified photographs. General handling source: [S-465: VFA responsible fishing](https://vfa.vic.gov.au/recreational-fishing/recreational-fishing-guide/responsible-fishing-behaviours).

### Current general Victorian bream rule — checked 28 September 2026

- Minimum legal size for all bream species, including tarwhine: **28 cm**.
- Combined general bag limit: **10**.
- A fish below the minimum, above any applicable maximum, excess to the applicable limit, protected or otherwise unlawful must be released immediately with the least possible harm.

Source: [S-462: VFA bream limits](https://vfa.vic.gov.au/recreational-fishing/recreational-fishing-guide/catch-limits-and-closed-seasons/types-of-fish/marine-and-estuarine-scale-fish/bream-all-species).

### Gippsland Lakes black-bream rule — recheck before every use

The [S-462: Fisheries (Gippsland Lakes Black Bream) Notice 2026](https://vfa.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0003/1235640/Fisheries-Gippsland-Lakes-Black-Bream-Notice-2026-Gazette-copy.pdf) applies to black bream in the Gippsland Lakes and tributaries, excluding Lake Tyers. As checked on 28 September 2026, it sets:

- minimum size: **28 cm**;
- maximum size: **38 cm**; and
- bag limit: **7**.

The notice commenced on 27 August 2026 and is drafted to expire 12 months later unless revoked sooner. Do not print or use these figures without a fresh notice check.

### Filleting wording conflict

The [S-462: Fisheries Regulations 2019, regulation 130](https://content.legislation.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-07/19-163sra006-authorised.PDF) and current VFA web page specifically name **black bream**: it must not be filleted in or on Victorian waters, with an exception for filleting at a cleaning table at a boat ramp. The [S-462: downloadable 2026 Recreational Fishing Guide](https://vfa.vic.gov.au/recreational-fishing/recreational-fishing-guide/7786-VFA-2025-Rec-Fishing-Guide-2026-FA-3.0-Digital-1.pdf) says more broadly that “Bream can't be filleted”.

For design and legal review, the proposed fail-closed constraints for a bream that cannot confidently be separated from black bream in the Gippsland Lakes system are:

- use the stricter **28–38 cm** size window;
- use the **maximum-seven** bag limit; and
- show no processing option beyond whole fish or the legal scale-fish carcass form while in or on Victorian waters.

That is a **conservative project synthesis**, not a reader instruction and not a claim that every bream everywhere is named by the black-bream notice or regulation. It does **not** authorise retention or eating when species identity is unresolved. Any retention must first be independently confirmed lawful using current authoritative identification and rules. Under [S-459: VFA definitions](https://vfa.vic.gov.au/recreational-fishing/recreational-fishing-guide/fishing-definitions), a scale-fish carcass may have the gut, gills or scales removed but must not otherwise be cut or mutilated. This packet gives **no cutting or cleaning instructions**.

### Required views before this entry can be used

1. Verified adult black bream in a true side view.
2. Close-ups of the rounded snout, continuous dorsal fin, large scales and lateral line.
3. Pelvic, anal and tail fins under neutral light, with colour labelled as supporting evidence only.
4. Verified juvenile black bream showing possible yellowish fins.
5. Verified yellowfin bream in matched side and fin views.

7. A verified hybrid example if a qualified reviewer confirms that the image and wording are useful; otherwise a clear “hybrids may not be resolved here” stop panel.

9. A simple map distinguishing the Gippsland Lakes and tributaries from excluded Lake Tyers, checked against the current notice.

Candidate image leads appear under S-464, but the displayed licence labels need the original file page, full licence version, provenance and locality resolved before use.

- The reviewer approves the black/yellowfin comparison, juvenile warning and any hybrid image.
- No plate depends on colour alone.

### Gate 3 — rights and provenance

- Every image has a file-level licence or written permission.
- Creator, attribution, provenance, permitted edits and integrity hash are recorded.
- A rights reviewer confirms that the intended personal PDF and local webpage uses are allowed.
- No third-party anatomy or clinical diagram is copied under another publisher's permission.

### Gate 4 — novice false-positive testing

Use verified photographs that include target fish, morphs, lookalikes, hybrids or unresolved specimens, folded fins, partial views and poor-but-realistic light.

The atlas fails if a novice:

- applies the carp must-kill rule to a goldfish, hybrid, lookalike or unknown fish;
- treats redfin encouragement as a statutory must-kill rule;
- identifies bream from fin colour alone;
- misses the Gippsland Lakes 38 cm maximum or seven-fish limit;
- treats the conservative uncertain-bream rule as permission to eat an unresolved fish;
- uses the page as a dispatch, bleeding or cleaning method; or
- cannot find the stop branch quickly.

Any safety-critical false positive blocks release of the atlas. Revise the words or images and repeat the test.

### Gate 5 — measurement and navigation test

- A novice can find the closed-mouth snout-to-tail measurement rule and use it correctly on models around the 28 cm and 38 cm boundaries.
- A novice can reach the current VFA guide, exact-water restriction and notice route from the page.
- A novice can explain, in plain language, why “unknown fish”, “legal fish” and “safe food” are different decisions.
- The page remains readable in the local reader and in the intended print size.

### Gate 6 — welfare boundary

- An aquatic-animal-welfare specialist and experienced Victorian fishing educator confirm that the pre-cast gate does not leave a novice with an unavoidable live-fish problem.
- Dispatch, bleeding and cleaning remain absent unless they pass their own separate evidence, illustration, practical and specialist-review gates.
- No live-animal trial is authorised by this packet.

### Gate 7 — release decision

- All preceding gates have documentary evidence.
- The atlas still carries its limits, access date and uncertainty branch.
- `FIELD_READY_BUILD` remains `NO` until the project's separate full safety release gate is cleared.
