# Portable drinking-water treatment system assessment

**Status:** Conditional research shortlist - no treatment product, dose, source or drinking sequence is approved  
**Scope:** Personal Victorian guide; activity-agnostic; selected suspect fresh water only  
**Evidence basis:** EP-011, S-203-S-214 and S-274-S-281  
**Checked:** 6 September 2026

## Short conclusion

Keep two proposed systems for testing once their documents are checked. One combines the exact Platypus QuickDraw 1 L `13903`, an Aquatabs product whose identity and dose still need settling, and a separate clean-water container. The other is the LifeSaver Wayfarer `WPA0101`, for comparison as a single-pass system. Neither is approved. Missing performance reports, Australian tablet details, storage limits and tests for dangerous user errors must be resolved first.

Start the wider water plan with safe drinking water carried from a known supply. Lawful outdoor boiling may provide a backup for some germ-related risks, but its own failures must be checked. It is not a remedy for chemical, algal, floodwater or salt hazards.

This is deliberately not a recommendation to drink field water. The safest source is potable water carried from a known supply. Every portable candidate in this assessment can be defeated by a wrong source, damage, misuse, storage, cross-contamination or an unsupported marketing claim.

## The source decision comes before the product

Ordinary backcountry treatment addresses microorganisms only within the exact device or chemical's verified scope. It must never convert these sources into an ordinary treatment branch:

- floodwater;
- seawater or brackish water;
- water affected by or officially warned for harmful blue-green algae;
- water with a plausible current or historical mining, industrial, agricultural or sewage contamination pathway;
- water materially affected by bushfire ash, retardant, damaged tanks, debris or dead animals; or
- groundwater whose chemical suitability has not been established.

Clear, cold, flowing or pleasant-tasting water can still contain microorganisms or chemicals. Those cues can help select the lower-risk **suspect** source when no known-safe source is available; they do not prove potability. [S-203-S-214]

## Capability required

The fielded water system must do all of the following before it can be named in the guide:

1. start with a screened source that is inside the system's verified scope;
2. provide verified coverage for protozoa, bacteria and viruses, either in one product or a validated sequence;
3. make exact product identity, dose/volume, contact time or flow, water condition and expiry unmistakable;
4. let the user detect or conservatively respond to filter damage, freezing, clogging, leakage and exhausted consumables;
5. keep untreated water, wet dirty components, clean outlet and treated vessel physically distinct;
6. work with the founder's exact vessels, hands, strength, lighting and likely cold-but-nonfreezing Victorian conditions;
7. survive the intended carriage and storage environment without relying on a hot parked vehicle;
8. retain a differently failing backup rather than a duplicate that shares the same product, heat, vessel or handling failure; and
9. preserve the hard source-rejection boundary for chemicals, algal toxins and dissolved salt.

The words `filter`, `purifier`, `tested to`, `meets`, `certified`, `micron`, `carbon` and a percentage do not establish those capabilities by themselves.

## Why the compact system leads research, not approval

The QuickDraw-plus-disinfectant architecture is attractive because it separates protozoa/bacteria removal from bacteria/virus disinfection and exposes filter damage through a field integrity check. It is substantially lighter and cheaper than the single-pass purifier candidates. The architecture also matches independent public-health guidance that a filter may need a separate disinfection step. [S-209, S-210, S-276]

Its apparent simplicity is deceptive. The exact tablet matters. The common Australian retail pack identified during this assessment contains 3.5 mg troclosene sodium and instructs one tablet per litre of clear water, with two after sediment removal for dirty water and storage below 25 C. The manufacturer's 2025 emergency example identifies an 8.5 mg tablet for one litre, while its general webpage lists many tablet strengths and tells the user to consult the dose chart/pack. WHO's product report says Aquatabs provides targeted bacteria/virus protection, achieved only 0.2-log mean reduction of *Cryptosporidium*, should be used in non-turbid or prefiltered water, and calls for regular checking of chlorine demand and free residual. [S-276, S-277]

These sources cannot be combined into a guessed field recipe. A current Australian product data sheet, exact label/lot/strength, intended emergency-water use, cold/turbid instructions and water-health decision on residual monitoring must agree before the combination is even bench tested. A spare strip of the same tablets is useful consumable redundancy but does not count as the independent backup to a primary system that already depends on them.

QuickDraw's manufacturer states that each filter is individually tested and provides a field integrity check. The current support material also warns that a new or dried filter must first be completely wetted and purged: diffuse foamy bubbles may be trapped air, while a steady local stream may indicate broken fibres. A novice can therefore reject a sound dry filter or accept a damaged one if the workflow is copied incompletely. That distinction must be taught only from the exact current manual and demonstrated on the exact sample. [S-274]

The 1 L reservoir cannot be the only dirty-water container without testing. The maker's own review page currently shows 3.3/5 from 22 reviews and includes reports of pinholes, burst seams, low flow and failed integrity checks. These reports are uncontrolled and do not establish a rate or cause; one out-of-box `failure` may reflect incomplete wetting. They create test cases: a pre-trip leak/dye inspection, alternate compatible robust dirty vessel and a rule never to squeeze a visibly leaking or damaged bag over the clean system. [S-275]

## Why Wayfarer remains a serious comparator

The Wayfarer removes the chemical-dose decision and claims physical control of viruses as well as bacteria and cysts. Its manual provides a post-shock membrane-integrity test: after air is introduced on the dirty side, air spray or bubbles at the outlet means the cartridge must be replaced. It also separates dirty and clean hoses and can draw below surface debris. [S-278]

The evidence wording is not yet clean enough for a high-consequence recommendation. The one-page study summary is useful but says `initial study`, covers two serial-numbered units, uses general test water and an adaptation of NSF P231-2003. The full underlying report, challenge-water stages, end-of-life results and exact current cartridge/model mapping were not public in this check. This is weaker than the product page's `independently tested to exceed full NSF/ANSI P231` statement. It is also not the same as certification in NSF's public listings. [S-278, S-281]

The published pore-size line is internally impossible: 15 micrometres is not 0.015 nanometres. A comparison table elsewhere on the manufacturer's Australian site says 0.015 micrometre, but favourable fragments cannot repair the formal product page. The manufacturer must correct or explain the unit and map it to the tested media. The activated-carbon disc may improve some compounds in controlled testing, but its 100 L claim and broad `chemicals and heavy metals` wording cannot be converted into a drinking instruction for chemically suspect Victorian water.

Operationally, Wayfarer has a different burden from QuickDraw. First priming requires about 8 L of clean water and six minutes of pumping; after use the membrane must not dry, stored water must be refreshed, deep cleaning uses an exact sterilising product, the unit must remain above freezing and ideal storage is 5-20 C. It is therefore a poor `forget it in the vehicle` device until storage and maintenance are demonstrated. `FailSafe` stops flow as pores block; impact damage is a separate path requiring the integrity test. [S-278]

## Treatment and backup architecture

### Barrier 0 - carried potable reserve

Known potable water in robust, accessible containers delays or avoids every natural-water treatment decision. It fails through inadequate quantity, leakage, heat/freezing, contamination, inaccessibility or separation from the person. Capacity cannot be one universal litres-per-day number; it must be planned from person, weather, exertion, duration, vehicle/foot carriage and remoteness. Exact containers and reserve planning are the next assessment.

### Primary microbiological treatment

The primary will be either:

- a validated combination whose components together cover protozoa, bacteria and viruses under the same selected source conditions; or
- a single-pass purifier with equivalent documented coverage and a usable damage/end-of-life response.

The first candidate does not become safer merely because it has two components; more steps create more errors. The second does not become independent merely because it covers three organism classes; one impact, storage or hose failure can remove the whole capability.

### Differently failing backup

Outdoor boiling in a stable heat-safe vessel is the leading microbiological backup only when the exact heat system is lawful, safe, fuelled, stable and usable. It fails differently from membrane damage or expired chemicals, but it can share the clean vessel, source and handling failures. It also introduces fire, carbon-monoxide, burn, fuel and cooling risks. It never fixes salt, algal toxin, heavy-metal or unknown chemical contamination. [S-179, S-203-S-210, S-215-S-222]

No current evidence establishes that the Total Fire Ban gas/electric `meal preparation` exception extends to emergency drinking-water treatment. The guide cannot rely on that exception until CFA/public-land reviewers resolve the purpose. Solid/liquid-fuel and campfire restrictions also vary with date, district, land and closure. Boiling is therefore conditional redundancy, not a universally available backup.

If lawful/safe heat is unavailable, this shortlist does not yet provide a complete independent backup. Extra tablets do not independently back up a tablet-dependent primary, and a second hollow-fibre device may share freeze/damage/source errors. The remaining defensible options are to carry enough known potable reserve for the plan, validate a genuinely independent second purifier, or state the unresolved gap rather than claim redundancy.

## Controlled test programme

No person drinks natural water or water produced during testing. Do not seed water with pathogens, chemicals, algae, faeces or hazardous contaminants. Home microbiological test kits cannot validate safety or reproduce certification.

### Test A - identity and evidence

1. Photograph the exact box, SKU, filter/cartridge, reservoir/hoses, tablet blister, strength, lot, expiry, label and manual revision.
2. Record the exact performance report/certification entry and map it to that model and current consumable.
3. Record Australian seller, price, warranty, replacement parts and support.
4. Reject mixed manuals, old formulations, loose/unlabelled tablets and favourable results from a related model.

### Test B - clean/dirty boundary

Using potable tap water tinted on the simulated dirty side with food-safe dye:

- complete the collection, connection, treatment and clean-vessel sequence without drinking;
- inspect hands, caps, bottle necks, outlet, hose, treatment card, first-aid/food storage and clean vessel for dye transfer;
- test operation and packing with gloves, cold wet hands, low light and one hand where safe;
- have a second novice identify dirty and clean parts by touch and sight; and
- stop the system if a dirty surface contacts the clean outlet or vessel without an exact approved recovery method.

### Test C - filter preparation and integrity

For QuickDraw, follow the current exact manual to wet, purge and perform the integrity test; record diffuse air purge versus local steady bubbles with a safe training sample. For Wayfarer, prime with measured potable water, perform only the maker's non-destructive integrity procedure and distinguish air leakage from clog/end-of-life flow. Never freeze, drop, shock or deliberately damage a relied-upon unit. Use retired/dedicated samples if a reviewer later approves destructive demonstration.

### Test D - usable output and error

With ordinary potable water adjusted only by safe inert suspended material if a water specialist approves:

- measure setup time, effort, spills and collected volume rather than accepting headline flow;
- perform the exact sequence under benign cold-but-above-freezing conditions without exposing a person to cold stress;
- time tablet identification, volume measurement, mixing and contact interval using a training-only labelled product; no drinking;
- introduce safe scripted errors: wrong vessel, unreadable label, missing timer, loose cap, failed integrity result, clogged flow, dropped clean cap and empty consumable; and
- require the user to stop rather than guess whenever identity, dose, time, integrity or source is uncertain.

### Test E - carriage, storage and maintenance

1. Leak-test the exact dirty reservoir and clean container before each trip; compare an alternate compatible robust dirty vessel.
2. Pack wet dirty components so they cannot leak onto the clean vessel, food, medicines, first-aid supplies or insulation.
3. Establish cleaning, drying/wet-storage, sanitising, expiry and inspection records from the exact manuals.
4. Assess normal carriage in the intended pack/vehicle while avoiding deliberate heat, freezing or impact. A product whose allowed storage cannot be maintained is rejected for that carriage role.
5. Re-run integrity and output checks after realistic non-destructive carriage and at each manufacturer-defined event/interval.

### Critical failures

Any of the following blocks approval:

- source-reject conditions are ignored because a product uses `purifier` or carbon language;
- exact model, formulation, strength, lot, expiry, manual or test report cannot be reconciled;
- a `tested to` claim is rendered as independent certification without a current listing;
- the selected sequence does not cover one of protozoa, bacteria or viruses within its verified conditions;
- the user guesses a dose, volume, time, integrity result or recovery step;
- dirty dye reaches the clean outlet/vessel or unrelated critical supplies;
- storage may exceed the product limit in the actual carriage plan;
- one failure removes both primary and claimed backup; or
- any natural/test water is consumed to demonstrate that a system `works`.

## Purchase gate

Do not purchase a relied-upon system from this assessment alone. Before spending:

- obtain and reconcile the exact documents above;
- have a Victorian water-health/water-treatment specialist review organism coverage, source boundary, tablet dose/residual question and backup architecture;
- choose returnable or dedicated test samples only after identity and storage are confirmed;
- test QuickDraw `13903` plus two compatible dirty-vessel options and a separate clean bottle;
- test Wayfarer `WPA0101` only if the full report and contradictory specifications are resolved;
- do not buy the Guardian unless the lighter candidates fail a defined task and exact P248 evidence/service are obtained;
- keep GRAYL deferred until public reports and a damage-detection decision are available; and
- select the carried reserve, clean vessel and lawful heat/boil equipment as part of the system rather than after the treatment device.

## Rejected shortcuts

- **Clear/flowing/cold water is safe:** rejected. These are source-selection cues, not clearance.
- **A rolling boil fixes any water:** rejected. It is a microbial treatment, not desalination or chemical/algal-toxin removal.
- **0.2 micron covers everything:** rejected. The QuickDraw maker itself says no viruses or chemicals/toxins.
- **`Purifier` means independently certified:** rejected. Test protocol, exact model, report and listing must be distinguished.
- **Aquatabs alone are comprehensive:** rejected. WHO classifies the evaluated product for bacteria/virus only and reports poor *Cryptosporidium* reduction.
- **One tablet per litre is generic:** rejected. Strength, formulation, label, source water, temperature, demand, volume and product data sheet control.
- **Double the tablet whenever water is dirty:** rejected as guide wording. A retail label cannot resolve the broader organism, turbidity, cold-water and residual questions.
- **Carbon makes chemical/flood/algae water acceptable:** rejected. No candidate establishes that broad field capability.
- **FailSafe detects every failure:** rejected. It describes blocked pores; membrane damage still needs an integrity check.
- **Flow/cycles/litres prove cartridge safety:** rejected. Those measures do not diagnose every bypass, damage, storage or contamination failure.
- **A spare tablet strip is an independent backup:** rejected when tablets are already part of the primary system.
- **Boiling is always available in Victoria:** rejected. Exact fire law, land, weather, fuel, appliance and TFB purpose control.
- **Improvised cloth/sand/charcoal purifies water:** rejected. At most a reviewed prefilter precedes an independently effective treatment.
- **Drink test water to prove success:** prohibited. Use controlled non-consumption usability tests and qualified laboratory evidence.

No product workflow enters the usable guide from this assessment. It creates the basis for one original visual system with:

- a hard `reject this source` branch before treatment;
- a matrix separating protozoa, bacteria, viruses, particles, chemicals/algal toxins and salt;
- exact product/consumable identity rather than generic icons;
- visibly separated dirty source/vessel/filter inlet and clean outlet/vessel;
- integrity, expiry, storage and stop-on-uncertainty checks;
- primary, carried-reserve and conditional boiling-backup failures; and
- an explicit statement that successful microbial treatment does not certify chemical safety.

Use original photography of exact purchased test samples after approval. Manufacturer diagrams, labels and logos require rights review; performance graphics must cite the underlying report rather than reproduce marketing. AI-generated equipment imagery is prohibited for the final safety-critical sequence because invented ports, labels, doses or water paths could be dangerous.

## Decision

**Proceed with document acquisition and controlled comparison; do not purchase, drink from or publish a candidate yet.**

The compact QuickDraw-plus-exact-disinfectant architecture remains the first research candidate because its layers cover complementary organism classes and the filter has an integrity check. The Wayfarer remains the serious single-pass comparator because it avoids tablet errors and also has an integrity test. Both fail the current evidence gate: QuickDraw lacks a located public test report and the exact Australian Aquatabs dose architecture conflicts; Wayfarer's public initial-study summary is narrower than its product claims and its published specifications contradict themselves. Carried potable water and a differently failing lawful outdoor boil path are part of the complete system, not optional extras.
