# Navigation and non-fire signalling equipment assessment

**Status:** Conditional research shortlist - no product, movement method or rescue-performance claim is approved  
**Scope:** Personal Victorian guide; activity-agnostic; land, inland-water, coastal and vehicle separation scenarios  
**Evidence basis:** EP-001, EP-013, S-021, S-043-S-056, S-169-S-176 and S-240-S-265  
**Checked:** 6 September 2026

## Short conclusion

Plan equipment for two jobs: working out where you are and helping rescuers find you. The proposed set includes current paper and downloaded maps for the trip area, a compass the user has practised with, and a record of location and movement. Signalling options include a whistle, a contrasting panel that stays visible, an aiming mirror and two lights with separate power supplies. None is approved yet.

Nothing in this shortlist tells the reader to walk out, climb for reception, follow water, enter exposed ground or leave a reported position. The stay-or-move checks in EP-001 and EP-013 come before using any item to decide on movement.

## What this assessment does and does not approve

The candidates below are exact items worth obtaining or preparing for non-hazardous comparison. None is approved for the field edition from specifications, price, popularity or apparent simplicity. Maps and electronics change. Compasses can be misused or magnetically disturbed. Audible and visual signals can fail through terrain, canopy, weather, user condition or search-asset technology.

The final guide must preserve six different tasks:

1. **Know and preserve the reference position:** establish the current position and last location known with confidence before considering movement.
2. **Describe the position:** report the displayed coordinate format, datum/grid/zone when known, device/app, accuracy indication, route and landmarks without improvised conversion.
3. **Retain terrain context without power:** protect current paper coverage for the actual area and adjacent exits.
4. **Retain tested offline context:** download the same and adjoining coverage before the trip; verify it with mobile data and Wi-Fi disabled.
5. **Remain conspicuous at the safe site:** use continuous contrast plus repeated audible and controlled visual signals.
6. **Fail independently:** a flat phone, damaged map, lost pack, obstructed sun, weak breath or failed light must not remove every location and signalling layer.

## Why the candidate order is conditional

### Maps: authoritative does not mean sufficient

Vicmap is the authoritative base, but a useful kit is prepared for the place, not for the activity label. A 1:25,000 sheet may provide necessary contour/feature detail yet omit a plausible exit or adjacent search area; a broader 1:50,000 or 1:100,000 overview may lack local detail. The test package therefore needs:

- the detailed area of intended travel;
- adjoining and plausible diversion/exit coverage;
- a broader orientation/index view;
- map title, edition/date, scale, datum, grid and MGA zone visible on both paper and digital copies;
- a measured print-scale check rather than “fit to page”;
- offline access after restart with mobile data and Wi-Fi disabled; and
- a separately protected paper copy that can still be used if the phone fails.

No downloaded map, GPS dot or compass instruction turns an unsafe route into a safe movement choice.

### Compass: fewer settings may beat more capability

The M-3 G leads because it avoids hemisphere ordering and supports a deliberate adjustable-correction workflow. The A-30 SH may win if testing shows that its simpler fixed-scale method produces fewer critical errors. Price, nominal accuracy and a global needle are secondary to whether the user can repeatedly:

- identify the exact map datum/grid/zone and the relevant north references;
- inspect and state the compass correction before use;
- detect a deliberately introduced wrong adjustment;
- orient/read the map away from vehicles, magnets, phones, powerlines and suspect geology;
- obtain consistent readings while cold, wet, gloved and under time pressure; and
- stop rather than walk when map, compass, GNSS and terrain do not agree.

No field-ready bearing or walkout procedure is approved by this assessment.

### Whistle: carrying and human output matter more than marketing loudness

The whistle must remain on the person even when separated from a pack or vehicle, but a neck cord can snag or constrict. Test a short, deliberate attachment to upper clothing/PFD/person-carried equipment that is reachable with either hand and releases before dangerous loading. Do not create a loose loop around the neck.

The three-distinct-short-blast convention comes from EP-013. Exact blast duration and reply wording remain blocked because current Australian/Victorian sources conflict. The product test should measure user success, not ask an unprotected person to stand close to a maximum-effort whistle. Hearing-safety advice is required before any sound-pressure measurement.

### Continuous contrast: colour alone is not findability

The VS-17 CIV is only a material candidate. Before it can outrank a larger V sheet, bright tarp or clothing marker, measure and record:

- true unfolded area, material, colour on each side, mass and packed volume;
- attachment strength and behaviour in controlled wind;
- wet, dirty, shaded and low-angle appearance;
- photographs from controlled ground distance and elevated viewpoints against green forest, dry grass, dark rock, red/brown soil, snow and coastal backgrounds where safely/legally accessible;
- visibility when beside, not on top of, the protected waiting position; and
- whether the item can stay displayed without consuming shelter, insulation or medical capability.

The black `V` on the larger sheet has recognised meaning, but detection still depends on area, angle, contrast and search geometry. The guide must never call the small panel an aircraft-visible guarantee or a shelter.

### Mirror: aim safely and reject range numbers

Test the SOL mirror only against consenting fixed ground targets on a closed, safe line of sight. Do not practise on operating aircraft, road traffic, vessels or people. Record whether a novice can acquire and repeatedly hit the target using one hand, either hand, gloves and different sun angles; inspect for warping/scratching after carry and wetting. Range marketing does not enter the guide.

In genuine grave danger, the eventual reviewed page may direct a mirror toward a search asset. Exact aiming wording and any aircraft interaction still require aviation/land SAR review.

### Light: one primary plus a genuinely independent backup

The Fenix/Petzl pairing fails differently: AA versus CR2032, metal tube versus cased compact body, separate switches and separate storage. The current preferred comparison is:

- **Primary:** HM23 V2.0 with a labelled approved AA chemistry and protected spare set.
- **Backup:** e+LITE in its case, attached on the person separately from the main light/batteries.

Test task light, lowest useful output, direct red/white access, lock/unlock, battery replacement, reverse insertion, wet/glove/cold handling, accidental activation after a realistic carry interval and post-storage output. Run duration tests must be unattended only in a fire-safe, temperature-monitored setup; do not create a deliberate battery fault, short, heat or immersion-with-open-compartment test.

White/red strobe is not an aviation-recognition approval. NATSAR warns that some visible LED signals may be invisible to searchers using night-vision systems. Preserve battery for care, communication and deliberate signalling; do not imply that a repeating strobe proves detection. [S-240]

## Controlled test programme

No test requires deliberate exposure to getting lost, cold stress, dangerous terrain, live search aircraft, road traffic, open water, high sound levels, electrical faults or severe weather.

### Test A - identity and source control

1. Photograph product, box, model/SKU/EAN, manual revision, serial/batch and battery/date markings.
2. Confirm the Australian seller/manufacturer route and current recall notices.
3. Archive the exact manual and product page used; record contradictions rather than choosing the favourable value.
4. Weigh and measure the sample; compare every safety-relevant specification.

### Test B - map package and offline failure

1. Select one familiar, legally accessible Victorian test area near the Zone 54/55 issue only if a geospatial reviewer approves the scenario; otherwise use separate safe areas.
2. Prepare detailed, adjoining and overview maps with visible scale/date/datum/grid/zone.
3. Verify paper scale with the printed scale bar and protect the map.
4. Disable Wi-Fi/mobile data, restart the phone/browser/PWA and retrieve every required map and location field.
5. Simulate a flat phone using power-off, not battery exhaustion; complete the paper-only task.
6. Have a qualified reviewer compare recorded coordinates/landmarks with the source data.

### Test C - delayed cold/wet/glove usability

After a safe indoor delay and unrelated task, ask a participant who has received only the intended field-page training to:

- retrieve each item from its exact carriage location;
- state the stay/move constraint;
- identify the correct map/compass and detect a wrong compass setting or interference cue;
- give a formatted location readback from a prepared scenario;
- produce the reviewed whistle pattern without maximum-effort exposure;
- deploy and secure the contrast panel;
- acquire a safe ground target with the mirror; and
- turn on, select low task light, select the intended signal mode, lock/unlock and replace batteries.

Any unsafe movement, wrong coordinate/grid/zone, unrecognised compass mismatch, neck-loop hazard, inability to find the item, uncontrolled dazzle, wrong-cell selection or assumption that strobe/RLS/search contact means rescue is a critical failure.

### Test D - controlled environment and carriage

- Water-spray and wet-hand tests only within manuals; no deliberate unsealed immersion.
- Drop/abrasion tests from realistic carry height only if the maker permits; no destructive stress intended to create hidden battery damage.
- Recheck map legibility, mirror surface, whistle hygiene/obstruction, panel colour/ties, compass bubble/needle and light contacts after carriage.
- Store primary and backup signals in different failure locations while both remain on the person or immediately reachable.

### Test E - visual detection study

Use fixed cameras/observers at safe, permissioned ground and elevated locations. Predefine distance, angle, lens/eye condition, sun/weather, background and pass criteria. Compare the small orange panel, large V sheet and existing bright gear. The study may support relative usability; it cannot establish aircraft detection range or operational endorsement.

## Purchase gate

Do not buy a full kit until these checks are completed:

- **Vicmap:** one test-area paper/offline package can be produced with correct scale, date, datum, zone and adjoining coverage.
- **Compass:** exact SKU/hemisphere/needle/box/manual identity is confirmed; the first device has no capsule bubble, sluggish/sticking needle or obvious disagreement away from interference.
- **Whistle:** authentic product identity, colour and attachment method are confirmed; no fixed neck loop.
- **Panel:** seller supplies unfolded dimensions/material for the VS-17 CIV or accepts return after inspection; vehicle V sheet mass/packed size is obtained.
- **Mirror:** exact 0140-1004 identity, flat unscratched surface and aimer are inspected; range marketing is ignored.
- **Lights:** exact current manual/model/battery limits are matched; current recalls are checked; ordinary 3.6 V 14500 cells are excluded from the HM23 system; button-cell storage is inaccessible to children.

## Rejected shortcuts

- **One statewide pocket map at useful local detail:** physically incompatible requirements. Use prepared regional/detail plus adjoining/overview coverage.
- **Phone map alone:** power, screen, app/cache and device failures are shared with communication.
- **Compass purchase as navigation competence:** rejected. A compass can add confident error without exact-map practice and cross-checks.
- **Mini button compass as the map compass:** rejected for this shortlist. It may indicate rough cardinal direction but does not replace a baseplate/grid method.
- **“Follow the compass/GPS arrow out”:** rejected. Direction does not establish a safe route or justify movement.
- **Natural-direction folklore:** rejected by EP-013.
- **Three of anything with one universal reply code:** rejected. The stable candidate is three short whistle blasts; exact durations/replies await Victorian SAR review.
- **115 dB or one-mile whistle promise:** rejected; no transferable rescue range.
- **Orange panel as an emergency shelter:** rejected. It has no validated shelter/thermal/ventilation capability and may be required for visibility.
- **Mirror kilometre claim:** rejected. Aim, sun, line of sight, terrain and search geometry control.
- **Visible LED equals aviation-visible:** rejected because NVIS compatibility can differ.
- **SOS/strobe/searcher sighting equals confirmed rescue:** rejected. Continue the PLB/location/findability plan until direct instructions.
- **Signal fire, flare, smoke or laser as baseline:** rejected under EP-012/EP-013.
