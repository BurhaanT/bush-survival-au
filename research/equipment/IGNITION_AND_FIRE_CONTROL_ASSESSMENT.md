# Lighting a stove and controlling fire: equipment assessment

**Checked:** 7 September 2026  
**Status:** Research and test proposal only. No purchase, flame test or field instruction is approved.  
**Scope:** An optional outdoor heat source for the personal Victorian guide, not a general permission to make fire.

## Short conclusion

There are two identifiable, locally listed ignition candidates: a BIC EZ Reach lighter and Redheads Extra Long Safety Matches. They are a pair to investigate, not a proven emergency system. Fire control remains a separate requirement; neither a lighter nor a bucket of water makes a place safe for stove use.

Keep the [boiling-system assessment](POTABLE_WATER_CARRIAGE_AND_BOILING_ASSESSMENT.md) conditional. The exact current stove instructions, Australian unit approval, storage, protective cases and supervised test arrangement still need confirmation. Do not add a lighting sequence to the book yet.

The match maker says both gentle and firm striking in different parts of its page. Do not convert that into an invented force instruction. The exact label and a competent demonstration must settle the novice wording. No stormproof, waterproof or sub-zero reliability claim was established [S-308].

## Source checks are not finished

Maker information copied by a retailer is one product-evidence family. The retailer is useful for a listing and price snapshot, not an independent performance trial. CFA supplies the Victorian fire-rule context, not a product endorsement. Neither candidate has the three substantive checks, specialist review and safe performance evidence required for Level 3 approval.

The PocketRocket 2 manual recorded under S-289 on 6 September could not be reopened on 7 September. Its current product page was accessible, but the FAQ includes instructions for Switch, WindBurner and liquid-fuel stoves. An explicitly AI-generated review summary also describes spark lighting. Those sections do not establish this model's controls or sequence. Older mirrored manuals appeared in search; none was accepted as the current Australian supplied manual. This is a document-version blocker, not proof that the product itself is faulty [S-289].

## Separate fire-control arrangement

This is a **proposed test-site requirement**, not field firefighting instruction:

1. Use an authorised outdoor venue and a competent supervisor. Verify the day's weather, fire declaration, land-manager permission and exact activity before any future flame test. This project will not conduct flame tests on a Total Fire Ban day, even if a particular exception could otherwise apply.
2. Ask the supervisor to approve the site, clearances, water supply, suitable fire equipment, access and abandonment plan before equipment is connected. A working site hose is a possible arrangement; its availability must be confirmed, not assumed. No equipment purchase is yet specified.
3. Keep fire-control water separate from the drinking-water reserve. The CFA public exception contains a ten-litre-or-connected-hose requirement, but is framed around meal preparation. That is not blanket permission for water-treatment-only boiling, and ten litres is not a guarantee of containing an escaped fire [S-290].
4. Have the reviewer identify which incident each control can address. Do not turn a vegetation-fire water requirement into generic instructions for burning gas, pressurised canisters, cooking fat or energised electrical equipment.
5. Keep the response within the operator's training. A small portable extinguisher is not authority to fight a spreading bushfire. Where lawful use, suitable conditions or the approved controls are absent, the proposed stove test does not run.

CFA's June 2023 fire-blanket sheet concerns building occupants, small cooking-fat fires and burning clothing. It emphasises ability, training, accessible storage and replacement after use. It is not evidence that a kitchen fire blanket makes a protective bushfire wrap, a foil survival blanket or a pressurised-stove fire-control system [S-309]. No blanket or extinguisher model is approved here; the relevant standards and exact equipment require a qualified check.

## Checks to commission, not tests already performed

| Stage | Proposed check | Stop or fail condition |
|---|---|---|
| Documents | Match all labels, stove/fuel revisions, approval marks, instructions and recalls; resolve the current-manual gap | Missing or contradictory instructions; unidentified variant; unresolved recall or approval |
| Harmless handling | Use inert props or an approved trainer to assess reaching, opening, grip and separation of supplies | Accidental operation, dropped parts, an unreadable label, or a control the user cannot safely operate |
| Storage | Supervisor-approved case and packing check, using inert substitutes for heat/water tests where necessary | Accidental activation, shared wet-storage failure, crushed supplies or a storage temperature outside the exact warning |
| Supervised ordinary use | Only after separate authorisation, test the complete exact system under its instructions at an approved venue | Leak, abnormal flame, unstable load, unsafe conditions or inability to perform the approved shutdown; no repeated improvisation |
| Fault decisions | Tabletop cards for no ignition, wet matches, lost lighter, wind, missing water and an inaccessible kit | User tries to modify equipment, bypass a safety feature, warm fuel by flame, move into shelter or continue when the approved procedure says stop |

Do not create deliberate leaks, heat canisters, immerse fuelled lighters, disable child resistance, apply chemical match coatings, practise with wildfire or use live emergency calls to test a response. Two methods reduce some shared failures; both still depend on lawful conditions, safe handling and usable hands. Having neither does not justify friction fire or another unreviewed substitute.

- Record actual results, then review the exact prose and diagrams together. No artwork or product recommendation is released on the strength of these candidate names.
