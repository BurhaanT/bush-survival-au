# Carrying drinking water, keeping containers separate and boiling: assessment

**Status:** Research candidate system only — nothing in this file is approved field instruction or a purchase recommendation  
**Scope:** Personal, activity-agnostic Victorian emergency guide  
**Checked:** 6 September 2026  
**Related controls:** `WATER-002`, `WATER-005`, `WATER-012`–`WATER-015`, `FIRE-001`–`FIRE-006`, `EQ-013`–`EQ-018`, `SR-044`, `SR-047`, `SR-054`, `SR-055`

> **Superseded workflow notice — 29 September 2026:** D-060 rejects the dry-rim, thermometer, transfer-ladle, temperature-gated and full-batch-storage workflow assessed below. It is retained only as a record of a candidate system that became more complicated than the public-health instruction it was meant to implement. It is not the current Chapter 7 route and must not be used as a procurement or field checklist. R-482 and S-487 record the current simpler rolling-boil, protected-cooling and clean-use/storage route.

## Short conclusion

Research and test a set of separate water supplies. The proposal is at least two drinking-water containers with separate closures kept with the person, plus an optional flexible container for extra clean water according to the trip. Keep any bag for untreated water clearly different from the clean containers. Divide and secure vehicle water separately; do not count it as water kept with the person. Boiling is only a possible outdoor option: its use must be lawful at that place and time.

This is a proposal to test, not an instruction to carry a fixed amount or use a named product. The person, trip and exact equipment still need the checks below. “Potable” in the technical notes means safe to drink.

## What the evidence does and does not support

Victorian public guidance gives useful planning examples, not one universal survival number:

| Context | Published example | What it can support | What it cannot support |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Parks Victoria general full-day walk | At least 3–4 L of drinkable water per person | A starting comparison for an ordinary full day | A statewide daily requirement for every body, season, route or emergency [S-282] |
| Parks Victoria beginner camping checklist | About 4 L of drinking water per person per day | A simple front-country camping planning example | A hot-route, medical, delayed-rescue or cooking/washing allowance [S-282] |
| Grampians Peaks Trail | 4–6 L per walker per day, especially for longer sections; two 1 L bottles plus a 2–4 L bladder is suggested | Evidence that a Victorian route can require materially more water and a modular carriage plan | Transfer to wet forest, alpine, coastal or vehicle travel throughout Victoria [S-282] |
| Medical/exercise evidence | Intake should respond to conditions, workload, acclimatisation, individual sweat loss and thirst; excessive intake can also harm | A reason to avoid both under-planning and forced fixed-rate drinking | A self-treatment protocol for dehydration, heat illness, hyponatraemia or a medical condition [S-283] |

The book therefore should not print “carry _x_ litres per day” as a universal rule. A reviewed planning box should require the reader to start with current route/land-manager guidance, then adjust for their own measured experience in comparable conditions, forecast heat and exposure, exertion, duration and rescue delay, person-specific medical needs, group composition, source reliability and the consequences of losing one container. Specialist review is still required before the guide gives hydration behaviour or symptoms.

The system begins with known potable water. Natural water is a contingency and remains subject to the source-rejection and treatment gates in `EP-011`; more treatment equipment does not justify carrying less known potable water.

## Provisional carriage architecture

### 1. Water that remains with the person

- **Two independently closed hard bottles:** provisionally two 1 L wide-mouth Nalgene Sustain bottles. Both begin and remain `DRINKING — CLEAN ONLY`. Splitting the reserve limits the consequence of one cap, bottle or handling failure. Using two identical bottles is convenient, but it is still a shared design failure; exact samples need leak, cap, impact and access testing [S-285].
- **Scalable clean reserve:** provisionally one 4 L Sea to Summit Watercell X, carried partly or fully filled only when the trip-specific plan justifies it. It remains `DRINKING — CLEAN ONLY`, is not the sole container, and is never filled with very hot or boiling water. Its light flexible construction introduces puncture, seam, cap and handling risks that the hard bottles partly diversify [S-286].
- **Untreated collection:** the exact treatment system's supplied 1 L QuickDraw reservoir remains `UNTREATED — DIRTY ONLY`. It never becomes a drinking bottle or clean reserve. The filter outlet pours into a clean wide-mouth bottle; the Nalgene mouth is not assumed to screw directly onto the 28 mm filter [S-274, S-285].

This provisional maximum is six litres on the person, but that is **capacity**, not a direction to fill or carry six litres. The trip plan may require less, more or a different distribution. A person who cannot carry or reach this arrangement needs a different system, not a silent assumption that somebody else will retain it.

### 2. Vehicle water

The provisional vehicle comparator is **two Pro Quip 11 L wide-mouth heavy-duty water cans (`1010`)**, each independently closable, secured upright and labelled for drinking. Two smaller containers reduce lifting difficulty and the consequence of a single leak or contaminated tap compared with one 22 L container. The exact 11 L product's Australian availability and price remain to be confirmed [S-287].

Vehicle water is supplemental. It is omitted from every on-person survival calculation because separation by rollover, fire, flood, bogging, a short walk, evacuation or an inaccessible cargo area is foreseeable. Its condition also depends on a clean potable fill, food-grade clean containers, shaded/temperature-aware storage, rotation and inspection—not merely on the word “water” moulded into a can [S-284, S-287].

A stove's legally required extinguishing water is a separate operational reserve. The same ten litres must not be simultaneously promised as drinking reserve and immediately available for extinguishment. No fixed volume guarantees extinguishment [S-290].

### 3. Unmistakable dirty/clean roles

Colour alone is insufficient in darkness, stress or colour-vision deficiency. The test system should use:

- large durable words: `DRINKING — CLEAN ONLY` and `UNTREATED — DIRTY ONLY`;
- conspicuously different shape/material and tactile markers;
- a dirty reservoir and dirty filter inlet stored separately from clean bottle mouths and first-aid supplies;
- a workflow that never places a dirty cap, hose, hand or thread over the clean opening;
- drying/cleaning and inspection that follow the exact manufacturer instructions;
- no field role-switching after a clean reserve is emptied.

The separation is a risk-reduction barrier, not sterility. The exact arrangement must pass an observed food-safe-dye test with every likely user [S-211, S-284].

## Boiling is conditional, not an always-available backup

Victorian guidance allows a tightly conditioned gas or electric cooker on a Total Fire Ban day only where it is a stable appliance designed exclusively for meal preparation, used to cook a meal, with the prescribed clearance, continuous competent adult attendance and water/hose ready. Solid-fuel and liquid-fuel outdoor appliances are prohibited. The legislation describes a meal-preparation exception; it does not clearly authorise using a flame solely to treat emergency drinking water [S-290].

Accordingly:

- do not tell the reader that an emergency makes water boiling lawful;
- do not rely on the TFB cooker exception for water-treatment-only use;
- do not freeze a permission table into the book—current declaration, closure, land tenure, signs and appliance conditions control;
- use carried potable water or a separately validated no-flame treatment path when heat is not positively lawful and safe;
- never use the appliance in a tent, vehicle, cave or enclosed/partly enclosed shelter;
- do not add an improvised windscreen where the exact stove manual prohibits one.

Even when lawful, boiling addresses microbial hazards only. It does not rescue seawater, brackish water, algal toxins or plausible chemical, mining, industrial, agricultural, flood or fire contamination.

The candidate system is incomplete until two exact dry ignition methods and a lawful extinguishing-water arrangement are selected and tested. The stove route requires at least 10 L of separate deliverable extinguishing water in usable vessels or an immediately usable connected hose, without consuming drinking water still needed. A lighter or match is not assumed reliable because it appears on a packing list. This assessment does not substitute a campfire: no exact grate, pot support or sustained-boil campfire cycle has been selected or validated.

The complete workflow also requires a separate suitable untreated-water collector; a dry exact pot; one dedicated long-handled transfer ladle; one suitable food thermometer whose instructions permit measuring hot liquid across the needed range; enough exact closable clean storage for all intended drinking water, each with a maker-stated maximum fill temperature; and a separate personal cup for each person. Before departure, the ladle, thermometer probe, storage and cups must be washed, sanitised and dried under their exact maker/current public-health instructions, then packed closed or wrapped against dirty contact. Loss of known-clean state stops the route; the guide supplies no improvised field sanitising dose. Use the collector to centre-fill the dry pot below its safe fill level while keeping the upper inner wall and rim dry. If untreated water wets that reserved band, stop. The submerged lower inner pot surface is heat-exposed during the full-surface rolling boil, where vigorous bubbling continuously disturbs the whole surface; the reserved upper band, rim, outside and lid remain untreated or uncertain, and the lid is not used. After manual-directed stove/fuel shutdown, keep the pot level and cool it fully uncovered on a stable non-combustible heat-safe surface in a calm place protected from contamination. Measure the centre without the probe touching any pot surface. Contact with the upper band, rim or outside stops the route; lower heat-exposed inner-surface contact is avoided and recorded for review. Transfer only when the stable reading is no more than both 50°C and the storage maker's maximum fill temperature. Keep the pot level; ladle all intended drinking water without touching any pot surface or storage opening; leave unreachable residue as waste; never tilt or pour. Close storage using outside surfaces only, then pour into a protected personal cup without openings touching. Never drink from the ladle/storage, re-dip after contact or return a mouth-used cup/leftover drink. Parks Victoria supports rolling boil, cooling and drinking, while S-458 supports 50°C only as a conservative scald-reduction ceiling. This exact bush workflow remains an untested synthesis; the result is provisional for germs only, not a guarantee.

The Australian Gas Association's public April 2023 directory listed certificate 6088 for PocketRocket 2 models including `09884`, but that dated directory is not proof that a newly bought unit remains approved. The purchase gate requires the exact unit's current directory/certificate entry and conformity marking, not historical model-name resemblance [S-289].

## Non-consumption test protocol

Use potable tap water and food-safe dye only. Do not drink natural water, make home microbial challenge water, add chemicals/algae/faecal material, intentionally damage a pressurised fuel component or conduct any test during a TFB or adverse fire conditions.

1. **Identity and paperwork:** record exact models, labels, manuals, caps/taps, approval marks, manufacture/expiry information and recall searches.
2. **Blind role recognition:** in daylight and safe simulated darkness, every intended user identifies clean versus dirty by sight and touch before opening either.
3. **Leak/access:** fill each water vessel; test upright/inverted carriage, ordinary pack compression, wet/gloved opening, one-handed retrieval where intended, and a conservative low-height handling drop onto a safe surface. Stop if the product/manual makes the test unsafe.
4. **Dirty-to-clean dye workflow:** use coloured potable water on the dirty side and clear potable water on the clean side. Observe hands, threads, caps, outlet, ground contact, storage and the first-aid compartment. Redesign after any transfer to the clean opening.
5. **Loss scenarios:** remove one bottle, puncture/isolate the flexible reserve, make the vehicle inaccessible and assign one user reduced hand function. Confirm the remaining plan is explicit rather than silently adequate.
6. **Vehicle storage:** secure filled water containers upright and inspect for restraint, tap/vent leakage, odour, contamination and access. Temperature-log the cargo environment before setting a storage/rotation schedule. Do not include fuel cylinders, lithium batteries or medicines in a deliberately heated test.
7. **Controlled stove boil:** only on a lawful low-risk day at an approved outdoor site with competent supervision and at least 10 L of immediately deliverable extinguishing water in usable vessels separate from drinking water still needed, or an immediately usable connected hose. Leak-check and assemble exactly as the manual states. With potable water and food-safe dye only, test the untreated collector, dry exact pot, centre filling below the safe level, dry-band preservation, full-surface rolling-boil recognition, loaded stability, flame control, steam, exact shutdown, level movement to a stable non-combustible heat-safe surface and protected open cooling. Test the exact protected-clean thermometer's response/stability in the centre without pot contact, the lower-of-50°C-or-maker-max-fill gate and the warning that 50°C is not proof of safe handling. Any dry-band, stability, thermometer or temperature-gate failure is a failed run. Do not substitute a campfire.
8. **Fault injection without damage:** repeat with a missing stand, inaccessible ignition, low fuel, wind beyond the manual/operating plan, wrong pot, inaccessible fire water and a declared TFB scenario. The expected result is `do not use`, not improvisation.
9. **Clean storage and cups after boiling:** use only the dedicated ladle, suitable thermometer, exact maker-temperature-rated storage sufficient for all intended drinking water and separate cups that were prepared and packed protected before departure under their exact instructions. After the temperature gate passes, keep the pot level and ladle all intended drinking water from the centre without touching any pot surface or storage opening. Contact with the upper band, rim or outside is a failed run; lower heat-exposed inner-surface contact is avoided and recorded for review. Leave unreachable residue as waste; never tilt or pour. Close storage using outside surfaces only, then pour into a protected cup without openings touching. Never drink from the ladle/storage, return a mouth-used cup or leftovers, or re-dip after contact. Observe scald, dry-band loss, drip/slosh, spill, surface contact, open-cooling, temperature and cross-contamination failures with potable water and food-safe dye only. Never pour hot water into a storage item above its exact maker limit.
10. **Comprehension:** a second person uses only the draft diagram and must correctly identify quantities as planning examples, vehicle water as supplemental, the TFB-purpose limit, dirty/clean separation and hard source rejection.

## Purchase and publication gate

No item or instruction advances until all applicable checks pass:

- current official route/land-manager and weather inputs used for an actual trip plan;
- exact container materials, closure, cleaning and temperature instructions retained;
- split-reserve plan demonstrably accessible to the individual user;
- complete treatment evidence and exact chemical-product contradiction resolved under `EP-011`;
- current stove approval/mark, manual, recall, fuel, vessel, ignition and extinguishing-water system verified;
- Victorian fire/legal and water-health reviewers approve the exact wording, including the TFB meal-purpose boundary;
- remote-clinical reviewer approves hydration-planning and excessive-intake wording;
- observed critical-error tests pass with the intended user(s);
- diagrams use exact tested equipment and show stop states; and
- sources, test dates and product versions are rechecked before every edition and trip where relevant.

## Rejected shortcuts

- one universal litres-per-day number;
- counting water left in a vehicle as water retained on the person;
- one large vessel as the whole reserve;
- a hydration bladder or soft cell as the only clean-water container;
- changing a clean vessel to dirty use midway through the emergency;
- relying on colour alone for dirty/clean distinction;
- assuming “food grade”, “BPA free” or “leakproof” proves field reliability;
- assuming a filter screws onto a bottle because marketing says it fits “most filters”;
- pouring boiling water into an unapproved flexible container;
- treating an item as “clean” because it looks clean, or inventing a field sanitising dose after contamination;
- using a close-fitting clean cover on a raw-water-wetted pot rim;
- collecting untreated water directly in the boiling pot or proceeding after the reserved upper-inner-wall/rim dry band is wetted;
- guessing that water is cool enough, using no suitable protected-clean food thermometer, proceeding without the exact storage maker's maximum fill temperature, or treating 50°C as proof of safe handling;
- tilting or pouring from the pot, forcing unreachable residue into storage, or allowing probe/ladle contact with any pot surface without recording it, or proceeding after contact with the upper band, rim or outside;
- drinking from the transfer ladle or re-dipping it after mouth, hand or dirty contact;
- substituting a campfire without an exact reviewed grate, pot support and sustained-boil cycle;
- using a stove inside or partly inside shelter;
- adding a windscreen against the stove manual;
- treating an old certification directory as current product approval;
- counting the same ten litres as both potable reserve and immediately ready extinguishing water;
- treating a TFB gas-cooker meal exception as permission to boil water solely for treatment; or
- proving safety by drinking test or natural water.

## Present outcome

The architecture is sufficiently defined to create a procurement-and-test worksheet, but no universal capacity, collector, container, stove, pot, thermometer, transfer ladle, temperature-rated storage/personal-cup set, preparation/sanitising process, fuel, boiling sequence or field drinking workflow is independently approved. The exact-stove sequence is an actionable but provisional germs-only route behind a warning; the collector/dry-pot/dry-band, temperature-check and transfer geometry remain unvalidated, and 50°C is not proof of safe handling. Campfire boiling is unavailable. Official guidance supports rolling boiling and correctly used treatment products, but no compact minimal-equipment or no-equipment route has passed this guide's validation gate. The next safe work is to resolve one exact no-flame product system plus exact ignition/extinguishing-water candidates and convert the water system into a versioned physical test sheet when purchases are authorised.
