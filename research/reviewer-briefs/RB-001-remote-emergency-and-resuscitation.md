# RB-001 — Remote-emergency and resuscitation review

**Required reviewer:** Australian-registered clinician with current emergency and remote/wilderness practice, working with an accredited Australian resuscitation clinician/instructor where qualifications do not overlap. IM-199 also requires an Australian epilepsy clinician or emergency clinician with current epilepsy first-aid expertise. IM-205 also requires an Australian paediatric emergency clinician, or documented current paediatric-emergency competence where qualifications overlap. Failed-contact decisions require remote/SAR input.  
**Review object:** dated emergency-core prototype plus exact linked diagrams, including the visibly unapproved IM-190 severe-bleeding card, IM-194–IM-195 choking cards, IM-197–IM-198 drowning cards, IM-199 seizure safety-and-timing card and IM-205 serious-deterioration card.  
**This brief does not ask one clinician to approve toxicology, product-specific allergy care or SAR operations outside their competence.**

## Decisions requested

1. Approve or replace one first-response flow that reconciles current ANZCOR DRSABCD with immediately obvious catastrophic bleeding.
2. Confirm the observable boundary between unresponsive normal breathing and abnormal breathing for a stressed lay reader, including gasps and recovery-position rechecks.
3. Confirm adult/child/infant scope, whether the first prototype can safely omit infant mechanics, and how drowning/contamination exceptions link without duplicating CPR.
4. Review IM-190's recognition, early-000, direct-pressure, embedded-object, one-or-two-pad, kit/no-kit and ongoing-observation wording. Decide whether the card's direct-pressure scope is safe and learnable, including the unresolved lone-rescuer junction with abnormal breathing/CPR.
5. Decide whether manufactured tourniquet and haemostatic-dressing content belongs in this pocket guide; reject detailed techniques that cannot be retained from a page without hands-on training. IM-190 currently states a trained-product boundary only and withholds placement, tightening, improvised construction, haemostatic application and wound packing.
6. Confirm movement language where airway, bleeding, spine/head injury, temperature exposure and difficult terrain intersect.
7. Resolve condition-specific position conflicts, especially breathing difficulty, suspected anaphylaxis, unconscious normal breathing, seizure recovery and undifferentiated serious illness.
8. Confirm the shared delayed-rescue monitoring spine and whether any generic interval is warranted. Current proposal has no fixed interval.
9. Confirm that numerical observations and consumer devices cannot clear a serious condition or delay action; define any narrow device role retained.
10. Confirm current bystander CPR stopping criteria and reject or amend the proposed prohibition on a wilderness elapsed-time rule.
11. Review the paired kit/no-kit path on every in-scope page, including what has no safe substitute.
12. Review IM-194 and IM-195 against current Australian choking guidance. Confirm the effective-cough/ineffective-cough fork, early 000 call, up-to-five checked back blows followed by up-to-five checked chest thrusts, continued alternation only while the person remains responsive and blocked, and the visible-object-only/no-blind-sweep/no-abdominal-thrust boundaries.
13. Reconstruct the cards on adult, child and infant manikins. Confirm the adult/child and infant body positions, support, angle, hand shape and anatomical targets; decide whether a static card can safely communicate those targets without implying a force level or replacing practical training. A paediatric resuscitation reviewer must specifically approve the under-one-year route and the one-year age transfer.
14. Confirm that choking enters the canonical CPR flow only when the person is unresponsive **and** not breathing normally. Test the transition timing and verify that no “unconscious” shorthand, standing blow/thrust continuation or page turn delays response/breathing assessment and CPR.
15. Confirm the post-clearance boundary: when observation is sufficient, which persistent cough, wheeze, noisy breathing, breathlessness, coughing blood, fever or other signs require medical assessment, and whether any symptom or apparently normal examination must never be used as a lay clearance test.
16. Review IM-197's secure-land talk/reach/throw path and its craft boundary. Confirm that the diagrams cannot be read as permission to wade, swim, tow, enter swift water or make contact, and that a reader already aboard a craft will stay aboard and use only trained equipment/procedures.
17. Review IM-198's out-of-water gate, ordinary 30:2 route with breaths, obvious-obstruction/minimal-interruption branch, AED/wet-chest wording, kit/no-kit split, spinal/cold priority and mandatory 000/ambulance assessment after apparent recovery.
18. Decide what infant side-position instruction, if any, can be shown. The current card explicitly supplies no infant mechanics and must not be treated as complete for that physical step.
19. Reconstruct the out-of-water flow with wet adult, child and infant manikins plus representative AED trainers. Confirm that no reading, drying, warming, equipment retrieval or obstruction step causes a harmful CPR delay.
20. Review IM-199's immediate CPR override and active-movement sequence. Confirm that “stay, time, clear hazards, move only from immediate danger, use soft flat head protection, do not restrain, put nothing in the mouth and do not force a side roll” is accurate, readable and does not become an unshown handling method.
21. Resolve the side-position conflict across S-111–S-113, including the food/fluid/vomit exception. State exactly what a novice may do during convulsions, after movements stop and when safe positioning cannot be achieved without force.
22. Confirm every IM-199 000 trigger and its display priority: more than five minutes; another seizure before recovery; first or unknown seizure, an unknown person or no plan; water; known diabetes; possible pregnancy; injury or breathing concern; no response five minutes after movements stop; departure from the plan/usual pattern; food/fluid/vomit in the mouth; and doubt. Reconcile healthdirect's additional diabetes trigger with the other sources, and confirm that five minutes is never read as permission to wait when another trigger already applies.
23. Confirm the post-movement response-and-breathing branches. Test the handover to the canonical CPR/AED cards only when the person is unresponsive **and** not breathing normally—including gasping, absent or uncertain breathing—and to IM-188 only when the person is unresponsive and breathing normally. Decide whether any responsive post-event state can safely be described as an all clear; the current draft supplies no fixed release time.
24. Confirm the personal-plan boundary. Decide which plan-specific variation may alter the general sequence, and keep prescribed rescue medicine limited to a trained carer using the current plan and exact product instructions without reproducing dose, route, device or administration mechanics.
25. Review the kit/no-kit branches and failed-contact route. Confirm that ordinary timing and soft flat padding do not imply special equipment, that no improvised antiseizure medicine exists, and that Chapter 3 can be invoked without telling a carer to abandon the person merely to seek reception or inventing remote self-movement.
26. Review the water-event cross-link with IM-197–IM-198. The seizure card must make water an urgent 000 trigger without authorising wading, swimming, towing, contact rescue or any other hazardous entry.
27. Test IM-199 complete and as each detached semantic panel with moving adult/child manikins or actors, connected and failed contact, kit/no-kit and known-plan/no-plan cases. Record missed CPR overrides, delayed calls, restraint, forced rolls, mouth actions, hazardous water entry, untrained medicine use and false all-clear decisions.
28. Review IM-205 as a complete card and as each detached panel. Confirm its reader-facing rule—“Call 000 if there is any concern that serious illness may be present, or if the person, a parent, another carer or the first aider thinks something is wrong”—without requiring a shock/sepsis diagnosis, severe sign, complete sign set, fever, blue skin or abnormal device number, and confirm that no omitted sign or normal reading creates an all-clear.
29. Confirm IM-205's position order across abnormal breathing, unresponsive normal breathing, age-one-plus recovery-position art, withheld infant mechanics, anaphylaxis, severe breathing difficulty, active seizure, vomiting, pregnancy, suspected spinal injury, overheating and the generic conscious-person comfort branch. Reject routine leg elevation and any implied walking, carrying or self-evacuation.
30. Confirm that the project should continue to withhold generic oral water and the conditional trained/available oxygen branch from IM-205 unless an exact cross-condition, product and competence route is approved. Review the no-automatic-warming and no-direct-heat boundaries.
31. Test simultaneous abnormal breathing and life-threatening bleeding with one and two rescuers. IM-205 sets no lone-rescuer priority and must not delay either canonical action.
32. Run hot/cold, vomiting, anaphylaxis, spinal, infant, connected/failed-contact, kit/no-kit, complete/detached-card and stressed-reader scenarios. Record delayed 000/CPR/bleeding care, false all-clear, age transfer, generic intake, movement, routine legs-up, automatic blanket and invented medicine/device actions.

## Evidence and known conflicts

Review EP-002 to EP-007 and EP-009, source IDs S-057 to S-148, S-169 to S-177, S-310, S-481 and S-483, and the cross-packet audit. Known unresolved items are not presented as settled facts.

Particular attention is required for:

- DRSABCD versus catastrophic bleeding;
- direct pressure versus a missed bleeding point, loose pad stack or embedded object;
- adult/child back-blow and chest-thrust anatomy versus a diagram that could be mistaken for upright striking or an abdominal thrust;
- ANZCOR's head-low infant position versus St John's firm-surface simplification, and the exact under-one/one-year-or-older handover;
- ANZCOR's unresponsive-**and**-not-breathing-normally CPR gate versus abbreviated “unconscious” wording;
- apparent obstruction clearance versus a retained airway foreign body and persistent respiratory symptoms;
- weak/very-low-certainty tourniquet evidence, device dependence and the decision to withhold improvised and wound-packing mechanics;
- rescue breaths in drowning versus compression-only fallback messaging;
- secure-land non-contact rescue versus craft, wading, swimming, towing and contact-rescue scope;
- adult/child side positioning versus the deliberately withheld infant mechanics;
- drowning-specific AED timing and wet-chest wording within one guideline family, pending independent technical check;
- contaminated mouth/airway versus rescuer exposure;
- burn cooling versus systemic hypothermia;
- oral fluid/glucose across heat, cold, stroke, poisoning, shock and diabetes;
- airway care versus spinal movement;
- ongoing care during a delayed retrieval.
- seizure side-position wording during convulsions versus after movements, including food/fluid/vomit in the mouth;
- a five-minute ambulance trigger versus earlier water, pregnancy, injury, breathing, first/unknown, no-plan, repeat, delayed-recovery, changed-pattern or doubt triggers;
- personal seizure-plan variation versus universal danger recognition;
- unresponsive normal breathing versus abnormal, gasping, absent or uncertain breathing after movements stop;
- seizure-in-water urgency versus the prohibition on hazardous entry; and
- continuing care during failed two-way contact versus leaving merely to seek reception.
- current ANZCOR serious-illness wording versus secondary-source generic recovery-position, warmth and conditional-water wording;
- age-one-plus recovery-position art versus deliberately withheld infant side-position mechanics;
- ANZCOR's conditional trained/available oxygen branch versus the absence of a reviewed exact oxygen capability in this personal guide; and
- simultaneous abnormal breathing and life-threatening bleeding without an approved lone-rescuer task-priority rule.
