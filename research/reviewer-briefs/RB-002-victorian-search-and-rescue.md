# RB-002 — Victorian search-and-rescue operational review

**Required reviewer:** current or recently current Victorian search-and-rescue/police-aligned practitioner with wilderness lost-person experience; additional emergency-communications expertise where needed. A Victorian outdoor/weather and exact-equipment shelter reviewer must jointly decide the shelter-specific items rather than treating operational review as engineering approval.  
**Review object:** dated emergency-core prototype, communications panels, stay/move diagrams, Chapter 5 shelter prose and IM-196, Chapter 9 navigation/signalling prose and IM-184/IM-185/IM-206.  
**Scope boundary:** the review concerns safe public guidance, not disclosure of sensitive operational tactics.

## Content IDs in scope

- `FIRST-001` to `FIRST-003`;
- `MOVE-001`;
- `COMMS-001` to `COMMS-004`;
- `BEACON-001` to `BEACON-003`;
- `SHELTER-002`, `SHELTER-004` to `SHELTER-006` and `SHELTER-011`, including IM-196 where stopping timing, site choice, carried-equipment gates, findability and a casualty intersect;
- `EVAC-001` to `EVAC-006` and the rescue/findability aspects of `MON-001`, `MON-004`, `MON-005`.
- `SIG-001` to `SIG-008`, including IM-184's reporting boundary, IM-185's three-short-blast wording and IM-206's combined five-mode safe-site synthesis.

## Decisions requested

1. Define when a person who cannot find the way back and needs police/search-and-rescue help should call 000, without encouraging delay or non-emergency misuse.
2. Approve or replace the initial stop/think/last-certain-location wording and the exact boundary for a short controlled retrace.
3. Define post-contact movement exceptions and how a group records/communicates any necessary relocation.
4. Resolve Victoria Police “never separate” against generic first-aid advice that another rescuer may seek help. The current guide withholds a lone-messenger exception.
5. Confirm the minimum information and order for a bush 000 call, including uncertain coordinates, road/trail landmarks and condition changes.
6. Confirm 112/no-coverage wording and whether accessibility or two-way satellite services need separate panels.
7. Review the PLB activation threshold for a deteriorating lost-person scenario and the relationship between activation, deployment, staying findable and shelter.
8. Confirm that return-link acknowledgement is not rescue arrival and that a genuine beacon remains transmitting until rescue services advise otherwise.
9. Confirm the five-way movement architecture: lifesaving reposition, minimum live-hazard move, supported movement, organised evacuation and technical rescue.
10. Decide whether any self-walkout, private-vehicle rendezvous or no-contact messenger decision can be made safe in a static public guide. Current proposal contains no universal rule.
11. Review IM-196's exact-equipment, prior-practice, physical-capacity, site, weather and no-build gates. Confirm that the card cannot be read as permission to invent a knot, anchor, dimension, tree attachment, no-equipment shelter or natural-material method.
12. Confirm when shelter setup must stop because it would require climbing, unsafe effort, abandoning essential care, leaving an injured companion, losing findability or remaining under a failing support.
13. Review IM-206 as one complete card and as each detached panel. Confirm whether the five unnumbered modes are safe public wording, remain visibly parallel and optional, keep every signal at one safe known or reported place, and do not imply technical independence, detection, confirmed contact or rescue.
14. Confirm the distress-beacon leave-on wording, three-short-blast route to IM-185, bright-contrast placement limits, sunlight-mirror boundary, controlled-light/power language and generic strobe/NVIS caution. Reject any move merely for signal, unsupported product transfer, fire/smoke/flare/pyrotechnic/laser route or ground-air construction.

## Scenarios to test

- solo and group; adult with child; companion unable to move;
- location known, uncertain and completely lost;
- mobile call connects through another network, fails entirely or drops after location transfer;
- two-way satellite contact versus one-way PLB only;
- PLB acknowledgement followed by a long delay;
- approaching darkness, cold rain, heat, smoke/fire, rising water and unsafe trees;
- a short retrace that becomes unclear;
- a responder-directed rendezvous that requires movement.
- IM-196 with the exact practised equipment available, one essential item missing or damaged, the reader unable to pitch safely, an injured companion needing continuous care, benign weather worsening, runoff or pooling developing, an exit blocked, and an attachment beginning to fail; use inert equipment and safe staged conditions only.
- IM-206 with every mode available, only one safe mode, no working contact route, no spare contrast, no whistle/mirror/light, shared phone/light power, a beacon already activated, a possible aircraft/light/sound response, a site that becomes unsafe and each detached panel presented alone.

## Required output

- decision label and exact wording for every in-scope content ID;
- approved public-safe diagrams or corrections to the movement/findability architecture;
- explicit list of scenarios where static-book advice must defer to live direction;
- any statement that must be attributed directly to Victoria Police, AMSA or another service;
- recheck trigger for operational, network, app, beacon or service changes;
- post-layout approval of the final quick-reference sequence.
- joint outdoor/weather/exact-equipment disposition of IM-196, including whether each failed gate correctly ends at no-build or safer-shelter action and whether any visual cue creates an unsupported construction claim.
- explicit disposition of IM-206's five-mode grouping, complete/detached panels, beacon wording, safe-site rule, shared-failure limits and 90%/60% confidence labels.
