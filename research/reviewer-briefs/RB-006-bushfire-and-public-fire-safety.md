# RB-006 — Victorian bushfire and public fire-safety review

**Prepared:** 7 September 2026  
**Status:** Brief only; no person or organisation contacted.  
**Required expertise:** Current Victorian public bushfire-safety or fire-behaviour expertise, with search-and-rescue input where route or movement advice is involved. Clinical reviewers retain medical decisions. A response from an agency employee must not be described as agency endorsement without permission.

> **29 September 2026 update:** Review the current D-059 and D-061 wording against S-484–S-485 and the S-488–S-489 trapped-car comparison. Resolve, do not merely validate, the conflict between CFA's 22 September close-front engine trigger, CFA's 17 September untimed fan/engine shutdown, FRV's 15 July before-approach engine shutdown and older still-live CFA close-front air-conditioning wording. Test safe turn-back and parking, front-facing only when manoeuvring is safe, exact vehicle controls, dry pure-wool blanket use, mask/fire-gas limits, the subjective post-front hand/air comparison and the absence of a universal exit route. The on-foot review must also cover lower-ground qualification, burnt-ground hazards and the no-water-entry-method boundary.

## Defined review scope

- EP-015 and BUSHFIRE-001–012.
- Shared movement, weather, communication and medical instructions only where the fire section touches them.
- EP-012 and the ignition/fire-control assessment for the separate question of using fire, not surviving a bushfire.
- Planned IM-060 and IM-061. No factual final artwork exists yet.

## Decisions requested

1. Approve a clear distinction between leaving before exposure and last-resort action when no safe departure is available. Define language for uncertain conditions without inventing a route.
2. Resolve the exact ordinary-enclosed-car sequence, including stop-site choice, avoiding hot-exhaust ignition, blanket material, visibility, ventilation, engine and what changes if the car fails.
3. Reconcile the car webpage, CFA V6 and interstate exit wording. Can a novice use a reliable cue? If not, supply the safest supported wording and explicit limits.
4. Define what can usefully be said to a person already on foot with no known shelter. Do not assume they can identify the fire front, slope behaviour or a safe clearing.
5. Distinguish an unknown bush hut or toilet from a prepared building and a designated refuge. State what to do if shelter conditions worsen, without teaching untrained structural firefighting.
6. Decide whether water-body refuge can be described usefully without encouraging drowning, flood entry or trapping. Current proposal provides no immersion method.
7. Resolve protective clothing and wool-blanket wording. Identify substitutions that must not be suggested. Coordinate mask statements with a respiratory/occupational-health reviewer.
8. Confirm the local meaning and lookup method for Neighbourhood Safer Places and Community Fire Refuges; do not rely on a permanent list or guaranteed opening/staff.
9. Review post-fire movement and lingering hazards. No universal all-clear or safe burned-ground claim is proposed.
10. Confirm which non-burning paper/prop scenarios can test understanding. Exact factual diagrams need approval again after layout.

## Separate fire-use questions

Check the Total Fire Ban meal-preparation wording against water-treatment-only use, the exact stove/manual and fire-control arrangements. Keep campfire extinguishment, gas-leak/canister fire and bushfire response separate. Do not turn possession of water, a fire blanket or an extinguisher into permission to fight an expanding fire.

## Required response

For each content ID: accept for later layout review, amend with exact words, reject, refer or cannot determine. Record the sources, date, exact file version, assumptions and recheck trigger. A general opinion that the section “looks fine” does not approve it.
