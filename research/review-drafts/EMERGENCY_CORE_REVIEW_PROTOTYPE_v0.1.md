## System 1 — What the guide covers and following rescuers' directions

> **REVIEW COPY — NOT EMERGENCY GUIDANCE**

**Content IDs:** META-001, META-005, EVAC-001  
**Status:** project controls approved; emergency wording requires specialist review

### Candidate wording

This guide is for an unexpected emergency in the Victorian bush, regardless of whether you arrived on foot, by vehicle, by boat or for another activity.

When you can exchange messages with a Triple Zero call-taker, police, a search-and-rescue team or another responsible rescue service, follow their directions for your emergency. Tell them promptly if the person's condition, your location or nearby dangers change. If they tell you to move or meet them somewhere, repeat the instructions back to check that you understood.

The book is a prompt, not a replacement for first-aid training, a personal medical plan, an equipment manual or professional assessment.

### Reviewer decisions

- Is “responsible rescue service” sufficiently clear for a lay reader?
- Should the live-direction rule appear on every emergency spread or only in a persistent footer/tab?
- What should the reader do if messages are delayed, incomplete or contradictory?

## System 2 — First minutes

> **REVIEW COPY — NOT EMERGENCY GUIDANCE**

**Content IDs:** FIRST-001 to FIRST-003, BLS-001, BLEED-001, EVAC-001  
**Status:** specialist review required; the order of urgent actions is not settled

### Candidate page heading

**Something has gone seriously wrong: check for danger and start getting help.**

### Candidate common actions

1. **Danger:** Look for immediate danger to yourself, the person and the group. Do not become another injured person. Reduce the danger where possible. Move only as far as immediate safety or lifesaving care requires.
2. **Response and severe bleeding:** Check whether the person responds. Look for obvious bleeding that is immediately life threatening.
3. **Get help:** Call 000 for a serious or urgent emergency. Ask for the service you need. Give the location and emergency first. If another person can call, keep someone watching the injured or ill person. Do not casually split a lost group.
4. **Airway and breathing:** Use the current Australian basic life support method to open and protect the airway and check whether breathing is normal. `[INCOMPLETE]` The exact method is not yet on this page.
5. **CPR or other urgent care:** If the person does not respond and is not breathing normally, go to the single shared CPR and defibrillator page. Otherwise control life-threatening bleeding and go to the page for the injury or illness.
6. **Protect and keep checking:** Protect the group from further harm from the surroundings. Help rescuers find you and report any change.

### Unresolved order of actions

`[DECISION REQUIRED — BLS-001]` The Australian and New Zealand Committee on Resuscitation (ANZCOR) first-aid sequence, known as DRSABCD, and St John's advice about immediately obvious catastrophic bleeding must be combined into one clear sequence. This draft does not yet explain whether treating bleeding at step 2 changes the order of airway and breathing care. A medical reviewer must settle the exact wording. Test that wording under time pressure using a training manikin and bleeding simulator, not a real injury.

### Explicit exclusions

- `[EXCLUDED]` No lone-messenger exception.
- `[EXCLUDED]` No technical carry or generic walkout instruction.
- `[EXCLUDED]` No pulse, blood-pressure, oxygen-saturation or clinical score gate.

## System 3 — Get help and say where you are

> **REVIEW COPY — NOT EMERGENCY GUIDANCE**

**Content IDs:** FIRST-001, COMMS-001 to COMMS-004, BEACON-001, EVAC-001, EVAC-003  
**Status:** specialist review required; instructions for setting up a particular personal locator beacon (PLB) are excluded

### Candidate 000 panel

- 000 is Australia's main emergency number. You can also call 112 from a mobile, but it does not put your call ahead of others, give extra coverage or connect by satellite.
- A mobile emergency call may use another available Australian carrier. If no compatible carrier has coverage, neither 000 nor 112 can connect through the mobile network.
- You cannot send an ordinary text message (SMS) directly to 000 or 112. Services for people with hearing or speech impairments, and satellite services, have separate instructions that must be checked.
- If the call connects, say which service you need. Then give your location and explain the emergency. Stay on the line, answer questions, follow directions and report changes. More questions do not mean help is being delayed.
- Say where you are even if your phone may be sending its location automatically. Give the map coordinates shown by your device, track or road name, nearby feature, direction travelled or last place you were sure of. Say which details you know and which are uncertain. Do not guess precise numbers.
- Emergency+ may display location information to read to the call-taker; it does not create mobile coverage or guarantee accuracy.

### Candidate no-mobile-contact panel

- Try the communication devices and services actually carried, following their current instructions.
- `[REVIEW]` If you cannot exchange messages and face grave and imminent or life-threatening danger, activate the registered personal locator beacon (PLB). This wording follows the Australian Maritime Safety Authority (AMSA) distress-beacon guidance; the reviewer still needs to check how it fits the lost-person advice.
- `[EXCLUDED — BEACON-002]` This prototype gives no universal antenna/deployment picture. Follow the exact beacon manual; do not make a hazardous terrain move merely for a presumed better signal.
- After activating a PLB for a real emergency, keep it transmitting until rescue services tell you otherwise. A message that the alert was received does not mean rescuers have arrived.

### Tell responders

1. Location and how certain it is.
2. What happened and the present immediate threat.
3. Number of people; age where known.
4. Whether the person responds and breathes normally, has life-threatening bleeding or is getting much worse.
5. Nearby dangers, weather, shelter and anything that may block rescuers' access.
6. Treatment, equipment or medicines already used and the times, if known.

### Reviewer decisions

- Reconcile the lost-person 000 threshold with the formal PLB threshold without encouraging delay.
- Approve location terminology for road, track, water and off-track cases.
- Decide the minimum safe wording for intermittent or delayed satellite messages.

## System 4 — Stay where rescuers can find you, or move only as needed

> **REVIEW COPY — NOT EMERGENCY GUIDANCE**

**Content IDs:** FIRST-002, FIRST-003, MOVE-001, EVAC-002 to EVAC-006  
**Status:** specialist review required; messenger, walkout and carry rules excluded

### Candidate decision panel

**A. The current place has an immediate threat or blocks lifesaving care**  
Reduce the danger where possible. If that is not enough, move only as far as needed for immediate safety or to care for the person's airway, breathing or bleeding. Check the situation again and tell rescuers where you moved.

**B. You have contacted help or activated a personal locator beacon (PLB), and the place can be made tolerably safe**  
Stay together, sheltered and where rescuers can find and see you. Keep communication devices and the beacon working as directed. Do not move without telling rescuers after reporting a position.

`[DECISION REQUIRED]` Replace “tolerably safe” with the exact danger checks approved by the search-and-rescue reviewer. Shorter wording alone cannot settle which places are safe enough to stay.

**C. You are becoming unsure of where you are but have not yet asked for rescue**  
Stop and work out the last place you were sure of. `[REVIEW]` Going a short way back may be considered only while still trying to work out your location and only if the route remains clear and safe. A Victorian search-and-rescue reviewer must approve the exact limits.

**D. A rescuer tells you to move or meet them somewhere**  
Repeat back where to go, which route to use, when to move and what dangers to expect. Check any limits caused by the person's injury or illness, and what to do if contact fails. Record where you left from and the time. Leave a message or marker only as directed by the rescuer.

**E. The plan would require rough-terrain carrying, water/rope/vehicle extraction or unsupported long-distance movement**  
These rescues need specialist skills and equipment. They are not ordinary first-aid moves. This draft does not teach how to make a stretcher, drag or carry someone, or free them from a dangerous place.

### Explicit exclusions

- `[EXCLUDED — EVAC-004]` No instruction to split a lost group or send one person for help.
- `[EXCLUDED — EVAC-005]` No symptom checklist that declares a person safe to walk out or travel by private vehicle.
- `[EXCLUDED — EVAC-006]` No tarp, branch, rope, belt or clothing stretcher.

### Reviewer decisions

- Define “immediate threat,” the acceptable short-retrace boundary and how a necessary relocation is marked.
- Decide whether any static no-contact exception is safe enough to include.
- Confirm the distinction between a minimum move, supported walking and organised retrieval.

## System 5 — Response, normal breathing, CPR and AED

> **REVIEW COPY — NOT EMERGENCY GUIDANCE**

**Content IDs:** BLS-001 to BLS-004, MON-002, MON-006  
**Status:** specialist review required; age-specific mechanics and special-context diagrams incomplete

**Terms:** CPR means cardiopulmonary resuscitation: chest compressions and rescue breaths. An AED is an automated external defibrillator, a device that checks heart rhythm and gives instructions about a shock. The exact age-specific steps and pictures are still missing from this review draft.

### Recognise the CPR branch

`[REVIEW]` Start CPR when a person does not respond and is not breathing normally. Occasional gasps are not normal breathing. Call 000, or have someone call, and follow the operator's instructions.

### Candidate common framework

- Use the current Australian and New Zealand Committee on Resuscitation (ANZCOR) sequence, known as DRSABCD. `[INCOMPLETE]` The exact sequence is still awaiting review here.
- Use 30 chest compressions followed by 2 rescue breaths, with the current technique for the person's age.
- If unwilling or unable to give breaths, continue chest compressions rather than doing nothing.
- Attach an AED as soon as it is available, follow its prompts and minimise interruptions. Do not abandon the person to retrieve equipment when that would leave CPR unavailable.
- Continue according to the current ANZCOR rules for bystanders: until the person responds or breathes normally, you are too exhausted to continue, a health professional takes over, or a health professional tells you to stop. Being in the bush does not create a fixed time after which CPR should stop.

### With a first-aid kit

- Use `KIT-002`, the selected rescue-breathing barrier, only if it is undamaged, checked for this use and immediately available. Do not pause chest compressions to search for it.
- Use `KIT-011` according to the exact trainer-tested AED. The final page must cover the approved wet-environment and solo-retrieval limits.

### Without a first-aid kit

Use this section too if the kit is missing the listed items.

- A missing barrier or AED does not delay the emergency call or chest compressions.
- The book does not propose an improvised breathing barrier or improvised defibrillator substitute.

### Special links

- Drowning: rescue breaths are important; do not use a compression-only slogan to hide the current Australian drowning flow.
- Choking: when the person loses response and is not breathing normally, stop the standing choking sequence and enter this page.
- Poison on or around the person: `[EXCLUDED — POIS-005]` poisoning and resuscitation specialists must check the exact instructions for rescue breaths and protective barriers.
- `[EXCLUDED — BLS-004]` No final adult/child/infant body-position or hand-placement illustration is present.

### Reviewer decisions

- Settle where urgent bleeding care belongs in the first-response sequence.
- Define how much CPR technique a static pocket refresher can safely contain.
- Resolve drowning, hypothermia, contamination and infant cross-links without competing algorithms.

## System 6 — Life-threatening external bleeding

> **REVIEW COPY — NOT EMERGENCY GUIDANCE**

**Content IDs:** BLEED-001 to BLEED-006, WOUND-001  
**Status:** pressing on the wound still needs specialist review; wound-packing and improvised-tourniquet techniques excluded

### Recognise and call

`[REVIEW]` Treat bleeding as life threatening when firm, continuous pressure does not control it or the person is getting worse. Call 000 early. Do not wait to measure a pulse or estimate how much blood was lost.

### Do this immediately

- Uncover only enough to find where the blood is coming from. Avoid unnecessarily exposing the person to cold.
- Press firmly on the bleeding point with your hands or a suitable pad. `[REVIEW]` The reviewer must specify suitable pad or cloth materials.
- If bleeding continues, check that you are pressing directly on the bleeding point. Do not keep adding dressings without checking why the pressure is not controlling the bleeding.
- Do not remove an object stuck in a wound. Apply pressure and padding around it, without pressing on the object. `[INCOMPLETE]` Eye injuries and wounds in other critical parts of the body need their separate reviewed pages; this is not a rule for every wound.

### With a first-aid kit

- Use gloves when immediately available without delaying pressure (`KIT-001`).
- Use a trauma pad and securing bandage under the final trainer-tested method (`KIT-003`, `KIT-004`).
- `[REVIEWER CHOICE — BLEED-004]` A manufactured arterial tourniquet is a device that stops arterial blood flow to a limb. Consider teaching its use only for life-threatening limb bleeding that cannot otherwise be controlled. The reviewer must approve one exact device, prior training, how its placement and application time remain visible, how to check that bleeding has stopped, and the rule against removal. No device has been selected.
- `[EXCLUDED — BLEED-005]` No instructions for packing a wound with material that helps blood clot (haemostatic dressing).

### Without a first-aid kit

Use this section too if the kit is missing the listed items.

- Use hands and appropriate clean dry ordinary material for focused direct pressure.
- `[EXCLUDED — BLEED-006]` No instructions for making a tourniquet with a twisting stick or other improvised parts. Without an approved manufactured device and training, care depends more on keeping pressure on the wound by hand, urgent contact and organised rescue.

### Do not do this

- Do not release pressure merely to repeatedly inspect a severe wound.
- Do not remove an embedded object.
- Do not use the strap used for taking a blood sample as a tourniquet for severe bleeding.
- Do not use severe-bleeding tourniquet instructions to treat snakebite. Snakebite bandaging is a different technique.

### Keep checking and tell the people taking over care

Keep checking whether the person responds, breathes normally, starts bleeding again or is getting too cold or hot. Record when pressure began and when any device was applied. Tell rescuers exactly what did or did not control the bleeding.

## System 7 — Suspected Australian snakebite in Victoria

> **REVIEW COPY — NOT EMERGENCY GUIDANCE**

**Content IDs:** SNAKE-001 to SNAKE-005, BITE-002  
**Status:** specialist review required; bandage sequence/material fallback and lone-person movement excluded

### Recognise and call

`[REVIEW]` Treat every known or suspected Australian snakebite as potentially life threatening even when the mark is tiny, painless or the person appears to improve after an early collapse. Call 000/activate the reviewed rescue path and bring help to the person where possible.

### Do this immediately

- Keep the person and bitten limb still. Reassure them and keep checking whether they respond and breathe normally.
- Pressure immobilisation means the reviewed pressure-bandaging method together with keeping the whole limb still. Use only the method approved by the poisoning specialist, with the specified equipment and training. `[INCOMPLETE]` The method is not yet included here.
- Leave the bandage and splint in place until the hospital team responsible for treatment directs otherwise.

`[DECISION REQUIRED — SNAKE-002]` Insert one exact, pressure-tested bandage sequence only after the reviewer selects the order, pressure cue, limb/joint coverage, splint method, non-limb boundary and completion checks.

### With a first-aid kit

- `KIT-007`: selected broad elastic pressure-immobilisation bandages.
- `KIT-008`: the selected support for keeping the limb still, fitted with as little movement as possible.
- `KIT-009`: record the site and times only as approved by the final method.

Owning a bandage does not mean you can apply it at the right pressure. The final edition must require hands-on practice before an emergency and a later check that the person still remembers the technique.

### Without a first-aid kit

Use this section too if the kit is missing the listed items.

- Keep still, call for help or activate the reviewed rescue device, and keep checking the person. A bandage never replaces keeping still.
- `[EXCLUDED — SNAKE-003]` No clothing/material wrap or improvised splint is shown until exact material and pressure testing passes.
- `[EXCLUDED — SNAKE-005]` This draft gives no rule for someone alone with suspected snakebite who cannot contact help. Victorian search-and-rescue and poisoning specialists must settle that case together.

### Do not do this

- Do not wash, cut or suck the site.
- Do not use an arterial tourniquet.
- Do not pursue, catch or kill the snake; optional photography must never delay care or require approach.
- Do not walk the person when help can come to them.

### Cross-links

The reviewed pressure-immobilisation technique (PIT) may later be linked to separately approved pages for funnel-web or mouse-spider bites, blue-ringed octopus, cone shells and sea snakes. It is not a treatment for all bites and stings.

## System 8 — Suspected anaphylaxis: a life-threatening allergic reaction

> **REVIEW COPY — NOT EMERGENCY GUIDANCE**

**Content IDs:** ANAPH-001 to ANAPH-006, ASTH-004  
**Status:** specialist review required; product-specific mechanics are versioned and not yet approved

### Recognise it

`[REVIEW]` Treat a sudden severe airway, breathing or circulation problem after a possible trigger as possible anaphylaxis. A rash or other skin sign does not have to be present.

### Do this immediately

- Follow the person's current allergy action plan from the Australasian Society of Clinical Immunology and Allergy (ASCIA). Use their correctly prescribed adrenaline product immediately.
- Call 000.
- Keep the person flat and do not let them stand or walk. `[DECISION REQUIRED]` The final visual must show exact reviewed branches for breathing difficulty, unconscious normal breathing, vomiting/pregnancy and small children without importing the asthma posture.
- If severe symptoms do not respond after five minutes and another appropriate prescribed dose/product is available, follow the person's current plan and exact product instructions.
- If the person stops responding and is not breathing normally, go to “Response, normal breathing, CPR and AED”. That page contains the shared resuscitation sequence.

### With a first-aid kit

- `KIT-010` is the at-risk person's current prescribed product(s) plus current action plan, not a generic kit medicine.
- EpiPen, Anapen, Jext and neffy each have their own instructions; they are not all given in the same way. Include a product only after checking it individually. Do not combine different products' steps, doses or pictures.

### Without a first-aid kit

Use this section too if the kit is missing the person's prescribed adrenaline.

- There is no improvised replacement for adrenaline. Call 000, use the reviewed position, remove an ongoing trigger only when safe, observe response/breathing and start CPR if required.
- Antihistamines, asthma relievers, food, drink or bush remedies are not substitutes for adrenaline.

### Asthma overlap

`[REVIEW]` If someone with a known severe allergy suddenly has breathing symptoms, and you are unsure whether it is asthma or anaphylaxis, follow the prescribed adrenaline/anaphylaxis instructions before the asthma-reliever instructions. Allergy and breathing specialists must approve the exact wording shown together on the page.

### Reviewer decisions

- Approve recognition and all position branches.
- Decide whether product mechanics belong in a static guide at all.
- Recheck availability, dose/age/weight scope, storage and instructions for every product immediately before each edition.

## System 9 — Dangerous cold and heat

> **REVIEW COPY — NOT EMERGENCY GUIDANCE**

**Content IDs:** COLD-001 to COLD-005, HEAT-001 to HEAT-004, SHELTER-001/002  
**Status:** specialist review required; food or drinks for cold illness, heat-device placement and natural-water shortcuts excluded

### Suspected hypothermia: dangerously low body temperature

**Recognise:** `[REVIEW]` Consider cold exposure along with changes in movement, speech, thinking, response, shivering and ability to manage ordinary tasks. Do not rely on one reading from an ordinary field thermometer. Shivering alone does not tell you that the person is at a safe stage.

**Shared actions:** protect the person from wind, wet surroundings and heat loss to the ground. Prepare shelter and dry insulating layers before changing wet clothing. Keep exposure and movement to a minimum during the change. If cold has seriously affected the person's ability to function, keep them resting and handle them gently. Do not make them exercise to get warm. `[REVIEW]` The exact signs and clothing-change method still need approval.

**With a first-aid kit and shelter supplies:** the final page must specify weather protection, insulation from the ground, dry layers and any layer used to reduce moisture loss (a vapour barrier). Include a warming device only after selecting the exact product and approving where to put it, what separates it from skin, what to check and when to stop.

**Without a first-aid kit or the listed supplies:** use the safest available dry insulating materials and protection from wind and rain. Keep the airway visible and do not wrap the person tightly. `[REVIEW]` The exact materials and wrapping method still need checking. Do not assume body-to-body contact replaces protective wrapping or rescue.

**Excluded:**

- `[EXCLUDED — COLD-004]` No food or glucose-drink instruction until safe-swallow, dose/type and third-source questions are closed.
- `[EXCLUDED — COLD-005 detail]` No hot-water-bottle/heat-pack body-location diagram.

### Dangerous heat illness / suspected heatstroke

**Recognise:** `[REVIEW]` Changed behaviour, confusion, collapse, a seizure, inability to continue or poor control of movement in heat are warning signs. They matter even if the person is sweating or you have no reliable thermometer.

**Shared actions:** for suspected heatstroke, call 000 and start a cooling method approved for this situation. Do not wait for a particular thermometer reading. Keep checking response and airway. `[INCOMPLETE]` The exact cooling method is not yet supplied here.

**With a first-aid kit and cooling supplies:** the final page must specify shade, clothing changes, wetting, fanning or cooling equipment for the person's age and level of consciousness, and the help available to watch them. Record what was used and how the person responded.

**Without a first-aid kit or the listed supplies:** move only to safer shade or cooler surroundings. Remove excess outer layers only as the reviewed method allows. Wet or fan the person only where the water and method can be controlled safely. Keep the emergency call and checks going. `[INCOMPLETE]` Exact methods still need review. Do not assume a river, dam or other natural water is a safe substitute for controlled cooling; no immersion technique is shown.

**Giving drinks:** `[REVIEW]` Consider a drink only when the person is fully conscious, can swallow and the approved advice for that illness allows it. Never force fluid into someone who is confused, becoming less responsive, having a seizure or unconscious. This draft gives no exact drink type or amount.

### Cross-condition warnings

- Burn cooling and whole-person hypothermia prevention must be reviewed together.
- Do not use hot-water treatment for a marine sting as a way to rewarm the whole person or treat heat illness.
- Do not use cold packs as a substitute for whole-body heat-illness cooling.

## System 10 — While waiting: keep checking, take notes and tell rescuers

> **REVIEW COPY — NOT EMERGENCY GUIDANCE**

**Content IDs:** EVAC-001 to EVAC-003, MON-001 to MON-006  
**Status:** specialist review required; form layout and generic interval unresolved

### While rescue is delayed

- Keep checking nearby dangers and weather, whether the person responds and breathes normally, and any bleeding. Also look for signs of getting worse listed on the page for their injury or illness.
- If the person stops responding and is not breathing normally, go immediately to “Response, normal breathing, CPR and AED”, the shared resuscitation page.
- Keep an unresponsive person who is breathing normally on their side under the reviewed recovery-position/airway method, using gentle handling and continuing observation.
- Protect from further heat loss, heat gain, rain, wind, ground exposure and other site hazards without hiding the face, airway, bleeding or treatment devices.
- Tell 000/rescue promptly when the condition, location, weather/access hazard or treatment response changes.
- Stay where rescuers can find you. Keep a personal locator beacon (PLB) activated for a real emergency transmitting until rescue services tell you otherwise.

`[EXCLUDED — MON interval]` No universal five- or ten-minute recheck timer. Airway/breathing awareness is continuous; other checks follow change, treatment and the reviewed condition card.

### Do not let equipment overrule the person

One normal reading cannot rule out a serious condition. That includes pulse, temperature, blood sugar, blood oxygen or blood pressure. Use a measuring device only with the training and exact purpose approved for that device and illness. Never delay a call for help or treatment to take a reading.

### Draft record for the injured or ill person

Use `KIT-030` only when it does not interrupt care. Mark unknown rather than inventing a number or diagnosis.

| Record | Detail |
|---|---|
| Person | Name/description, approximate age only if known |
| Event | What happened or what the person says is wrong; when it happened or they were found, if known |
| Location | Map coordinates, landmark or route; how sure you are; any later movement |
| Response and breathing | What was observed and how it changed |
| Injuries/signs | What you can observe; do not guess a diagnosis |
| Actions | Calls/SOS, pressure, position, cooling/warming, device or other care with times |
| Medicines/devices | Exact product, dose/action and time only if known; response |
| Background | Allergies, regular medicines, relevant history and current plan if known |
| Hazards/access | Weather, ground, water, fire, trees, traffic or chemicals; what could harm people or block rescuers |
| Change | Better, worse or new findings and when noticed |

### What to tell the people taking over care

Start with immediate dangers or changes. Say what happened, what you observed, what you did and how the person responded. Give important allergies, medicines and medical history if known, then the location and nearby dangers. Give the new carer your notes or an accurate copy. Make clear who is taking over care.

- Approve the shared observation loop and condition-page boundaries.
- Decide whether the record is short enough for a lay user and complete enough for Victorian responders.
- Define safe use of phone notes when the phone is the only communication device.
- Confirm that “unknown” fields and no fixed interval are acceptable.
