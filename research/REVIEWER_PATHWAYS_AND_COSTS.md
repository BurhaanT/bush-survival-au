**Prepared:** 4 September 2026  
**Applies to:** Victoria personal edition, emergency core first  
**Status:** research only; nobody has been contacted, appointed or represented as endorsing the guide

**7 September update:** [Two limited initial enquiries](reviewer-briefs/INITIAL_REVIEW_ENQUIRIES.md) are drafted but not sent. Begin by establishing availability, a small first scope and fees. The whole-panel allowances below are planning assumptions, not quotes, minimum spend or authority to commission work. There are now six topic briefs, including RB-006 for bushfire/public fire safety; the initial enquiries remain medical and SAR referrals only.

## Bottom line

Public information identifies Australian specialists with relevant expertise, including Victorian wilderness-response experience. We have not established who is willing or available to review this guide. We need written review of a clearly defined set of instructions, followed by checks of the final pages.

The recommended way to limit review work is to start with a small emergency section. Use only the relevant brief to ask each specialist questions within their expertise. Revise the draft, test it with readers and have the final pages checked. Allow for a revision round in any quote; repeat review or testing if safety errors remain. Do not assume one round will be enough.

This is a referral map, not a claim that any organisation or named member is willing to undertake private manuscript work. An organisation's public expertise does not imply approval, availability or endorsement.

## Sensible contact sequence

No contact should be made until the founder approves the expenditure-enquiry step.

1. Prepare one two-page invitation containing purpose, personal-use status, exact brief, expected hours, deliverables, independence/conflict requirements, confidentiality, credit preference and the statement that no endorsement is sought.
2. Approach AWEMS for the lead-clinician referral. Ask for expressions of interest, not free committee review.
3. Once the lead reviewer identifies genuine discipline boundaries, approach BSAR, the Victorian Poisons Centre/AVRU and an ASCIA-listed reviewer only for the remaining bounded questions.
4. Appoint the human-factors reviewer after the first clinical revision so usability time is not spent on wording likely to change.
5. Do not ask agencies to “approve the book”. Ask whether an appropriately qualified person may review named operational propositions in a personal capacity or whether the agency can answer a narrow policy question.
6. Record written permission before naming a reviewer, their employer or an organisation in the guide. A person's employer must not appear as an endorser without explicit institutional permission.

## Scope to quote

Quotes should separate these tasks:

- orientation and conflict declaration;
- source and evidence-packet review;
- first markup of exact content IDs and diagrams;
- one consolidated revision round;
- response to disputed or referred items;
- post-layout page check;
- permission, if any, to name the reviewer and describe their role.

Require a fixed cap or an alert at 75% of the approved hours. Supply only the relevant brief, evidence packet, cross-packet junctions and prototype pages. The source register may be supplied as a reference, but reviewers should not have to audit unrelated chapters.

## Planning budget — not quotes

There is no reliable public price schedule for this particular kind of review. The figures below are internal planning assumptions in Australian dollars, including an allowance for administration and one revision but excluding travel, physical field trials and GST treatment. Actual fixed quotes are required before engagement.

The specialist rate band is deliberately conservative. As a rough external comparator—not a manuscript-review tariff—a 2024 AMA submission recorded a then-current specialist reading-time rate of **$556.90 per hour** in a South Australian compensation scheme [S-201]. Current DVA fee schedules also show that specialist reports and extended documentation work are priced separately from ordinary clinical consultations [S-202]. These comparators justify budgeting professional time rather than expecting informal free endorsement, but they do not predict a quote.

### Scenario totals

| Scenario | What it assumes | Planning total |
|---|---|---:|
| Lean, relationship-assisted | Bounded emergency core, some reduced-rate/honorary operational input, substantial role overlap, one clean revision | **$7,000–$12,000** |
| Base paid panel | Independent paid specialists for each irreducible discipline, normal revision and post-layout check | **$16,000–$27,000** |
| Conservative/high-friction | Upper rate bands, multiple referrals, disputed junctions or significant redrafting | **$30,000–$45,000+** |

These totals are not additive full-table minima/maxima; they allow for sensible role overlap. For example, the lead wilderness clinician may also perform the resuscitation review, and a clinical toxicologist may cover both poison and envenomation. Role overlap is acceptable only when competence is documented for both scopes.

## Recommended procurement gate

Before spending money, send the same bounded brief to at least three plausible lead reviewers and request:

- relevant current practice and wilderness experience;
- which content IDs they can and cannot review;
- fixed fee or hourly rate and cap;
- estimated turnaround and number of revisions;
- professional indemnity position;
- conflicts of interest;
- whether post-layout re-review is included;
- whether they will be named, anonymous or uncredited;
- whether they permit reference to any employer or professional organisation.

Reject proposals that offer only a general read, do not mark exact propositions, cannot identify out-of-scope material, or treat a certificate/course attendance as sufficient expertise.

## Cost-control recommendation

Proceed in two decisions:

**Decision A — referral test:** authorise only non-binding enquiries using `RB-001` and `RB-002`. The goal is to learn whether a suitable lead clinician and Victorian SAR reviewer are available and what they would charge. No clinical content advances at this point.

**Decision B — emergency-core review:** commission the lead clinician first. Use their referrals to narrow the toxinology/allergy scopes before appointing the rest. Cap the first paid round at the emergency core; do not include plants, food procurement, complex improvised techniques or the whole future manuscript.

If the base paid-panel cost is disproportionate for a personal guide, the safe response is a smaller guide containing less high-consequence instruction—not lowering the validation gate. The irreducible alternative is to keep unresolved pages excluded and rely on current official first-aid resources, personal training and emergency services rather than present unreviewed derivative instructions as field-ready.

## Evidence versus inference

- **Evidence:** the listed organisations maintain relevant expertise, public professional directories or public enquiry routes [S-192–S-200].
- **Inference:** a bounded paid request is more likely to receive a useful response than a request for whole-book endorsement.
- **Assumption:** the hours and rates above are sufficient for planning; only written quotes can validate them.
- **Unknown:** reviewer availability, willingness to work on a personal-use guide, institutional permission, insurance requirements and exact cost.
