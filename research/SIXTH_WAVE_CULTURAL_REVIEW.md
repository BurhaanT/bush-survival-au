# Sixth-wave cultural review — private working edition

**Date:** 8 September 2026  
**Scope:** Samphire, Bulbine lily, common nardoo and nitre-bush only.  
**Status:** Public-source and ICIP desk review. No Traditional Owner organisation has reviewed or approved this guide.

## Outcome

Four decisions control this wave:

1. Keep the Gunaikurnai samphire record with Lake Tyers State Park on Krauatungalung Country and with the source's broad, older botanical wording. Do not transfer it to every plant called samphire or to a *Tecticornia* species.
2. Keep the Woi wurrung and Dja Dja Wurrung Bulbine-lily records separate. One Nation's language, season or food context must not be used to complete the other Nation's account.
3. Keep the Wemba Wemba and Dja Dja Wurrung nardoo records separate. Do not move the Wemba Wemba name or museum-recorded processing account onto Dja Dja Wurrung Country, and do not move Dja Dja Wurrung language onto the Wemba Wemba record.
4. Do not invent a Victorian Traditional Owner attribution for nitre-bush. The absence of a suitable public source means **attribution not established**, not “no cultural use”.

These decisions govern attribution only. They do not approve identification, harvesting, preparation, eating or medicinal use.

## Evidence-confidence language

This review uses the project's **High**, **Moderate** and **Insufficient** labels. They describe the available evidence and its limits; they are not percentages, safety probabilities or claims of Traditional Owner endorsement.

## Private-copy ICIP rule

The manuscript may preserve a brief, independently worded and clearly attributed contextual fact from a public source in this strictly private working copy only when the source's own terms allow it. Each note must keep the named people or organisation, Nation or clan, Country, source type and botanical limit together.

Private use does not:

- transfer ownership of cultural knowledge;
- turn public access into permission to reproduce text, artwork, language resources, maps, photographs or detailed practices;
- authorise harvesting on Country or elsewhere;
- permit a method from one Country to be generalised across Victoria;
- establish botanical identity, food safety, nutrition or clinical effect; or
- permit the guide to be shared, uploaded, printed for distribution, published or commercialised.

An explicit ICIP, copyright or prior-consent condition overrides the default. Any version beyond the founder's private copy requires a fresh item-by-item decision and direction from the relevant Traditional Owner organisation. Silence is not permission. This is a project governance rule, not legal advice.

## Place-specific attribution decisions

### Samphire — Gunaikurnai, Krauatungalung Country

The [Gunaikurnai and Victorian Government Joint Management Plan for Lake Tyers State Park — Krauatungalung Country](https://www.parks.vic.gov.au/-/media/3596bf1015194da59d7029ee18a2cb79.pdf) is authorised and published by the Gunaikurnai Traditional Owner Land Management Board, with input from Gunaikurnai Land and Waters Aboriginal Corporation and the government management partners. It identifies Lake Tyers as Krauatungalung clan Country and records samphire and sea asparagus under the broad name *Sarcocornia* spp. as important bush-food plants.

**Decision:** A private, high-level sentence may attribute that cultural importance to Gunaikurnai at Lake Tyers on Krauatungalung Country. Do not call it a statewide Aboriginal account. Do not add a harvesting or preparation method because the plan gives none.

**Taxonomic boundary:** The source uses *Sarcocornia* spp.; current VicFlora treats the relevant Australian *Sarcocornia* within *Salicornia*. That later taxonomic crosswalk does not prove which exact species the cultural statement meant. It cannot be reassigned to *Tecticornia*.

**Evidence confidence:** **High** for the source, place and broad cultural statement; **Moderate** for applying the later genus change to the source wording; **Insufficient** for an exact species, preparation, serving or novice field action.

**Rights:** Copyright is held by the Gunaikurnai Traditional Owner Land Management Board and the State of Victoria. The plan is public but not openly licensed for republication. Keep only the bounded private attribution; do not reproduce its wording or images. Any shared edition requires a new permission decision with GKTOLMB/GLaWAC.

### Bulbine lily — Woi wurrung record

Museums Victoria's [Plants of Milarri Garden](https://museumsvictoria.com.au/bunjilaka/about-us/the-plants-of-milarri-garden/) records **Pike** as Woi wurrung beside *Bulbine bulbosa* and gives an institutional account of the underground part and probable cooking. The page was prepared by named museum staff and warns people without proper knowledge not to eat the plants. It is a museum record, not evidence of current Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung review or permission for this guide.

**Decision:** Keep **Pike** attached to “Woi wurrung — Museums Victoria/Bunjilaka record”. Do not present it as a generic Victorian name and do not turn the museum's tentative preparation wording into a recipe.

**Evidence confidence:** **Moderate** for the bounded institutional Woi wurrung attribution; **Insufficient** for current community approval, a complete preparation method or safe field use.

### Bulbine lily — Dja Dja Wurrung record

The [Dja Dja Wurrung seasonal calendar](https://www.mountalexander.vic.gov.au/Explore-the-shire/Castlemaine-Botanical-Gardens/Explore-the-collections/Dja-Dja-Wurrung-seasonal-calendar), created by Uncle Rick Nelson and Daikota Nelson and compiled by Uncle Rick Nelson and Karl Just with named contributors, records **Bam** for Bulbine lily and places its flowering in the Dum and Bundjil season at named sites on Dja Dja Wurrung Country. DJAARA's [2021–22 annual report](https://djadjawurrung.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/DJAARA-Annual_Report_2021_22.pdf) separately publishes **bam, barm** for Bulbine lily.

**Decision:** Keep **Bam/bam/barm**, the Dja Dja Wurrung seasonal observation and Dja Dja Wurrung Country together. Neither Dja Dja Wurrung source supplies the Woi wurrung name or the Bunjilaka food-preparation account, so those details must not be merged.

**Evidence confidence:** **High** for the public Dja Dja Wurrung name and seasonal context; **Insufficient** for a species-level eating process, safe preparation or permission to reproduce fuller cultural material.

**Rights:** [DJAARA's resources notice](https://djadjawurrung.com.au/resources/) says its resources are learning tools and must not be misused or published without prior written consent. The private copy may retain only the brief attribution above. Any sharing or fuller use requires DJAARA's prior written consent.

### Common nardoo — Wemba Wemba record

Museums Victoria's [Plants of Milarri Garden](https://museumsvictoria.com.au/bunjilaka/about-us/the-plants-of-milarri-garden/) records **Dullum Dullum** as Wemba Wemba beside *Marsilea drummondii* and gives a short institutional processing history.

**Decision:** Keep **Dullum Dullum**, Wemba Wemba and the Bunjilaka source together. Do not describe the museum page as present-day Wemba Wemba permission and do not reduce its processing history to an improvised detoxification recipe.

**Evidence confidence:** **Moderate** for the bounded museum attribution; **Insufficient** for current community approval or a safe novice process.

### Nardoo — Dja Dja Wurrung record

DJAARA's [Dhelkunya Dja Country Plan 2014–2034](https://djadjawurrung.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Dhelkunya-Dja-Country-Plan-2014-2034.pdf) records nardoo among local plants used on Dja Dja Wurrung Country and later records gathering nardoo within the community's food-and-medicine context. DJAARA's [2021–22 annual report](https://djadjawurrung.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/DJAARA-Annual_Report_2021_22.pdf) publishes **gitjawil dhurung** for nardoo.

**Decision:** Keep that broad Dja Dja Wurrung use statement and language record on Dja Dja Wurrung Country. The Country Plan says “nardoo”; it does not establish in the cited passage that every statement applies specifically to *Marsilea drummondii*. Do not attach **Dullum Dullum** or the Bunjilaka/Wemba Wemba processing account to this Dja Dja Wurrung record. “Food and medicine” is cultural context, not a clinical claim.

**Evidence confidence:** **High** for the broad, public Dja Dja Wurrung cultural statement and language record; **Insufficient** for a species-level equation, medicinal effectiveness, safe preparation or novice detoxification.

**Rights:** DJAARA's prior-written-consent notice controls any use beyond the bounded private attribution. No language list, detailed practice or source artwork is reproduced.

### Nitre-bush — no Victorian attribution established

[VicFlora](https://vicflora.rbg.vic.gov.au/flora/taxon/855ebb8a-20f0-4043-9de8-16170018bac6) establishes *Nitraria billardierei* as a Victorian native with a mainly north-western Victorian distribution and isolated occurrences near Portland. General botanical and food pages say Aboriginal people used the fruit, and sources from other states provide named cultural contexts. None located in this pass was a Victorian Traditional Owner-controlled or clearly Victorian Traditional Owner-attributed source sufficient to assign a Nation, language name, practice or preparation to this guide.

**Decision:** Do not invent a Victorian Traditional Owner attribution, borrow one from another state, infer one from the plant's range or attach a generic “Aboriginal use” sentence. If nitre-bush appears in the private manuscript, its cultural field should say: “No suitable public Victorian Traditional Owner source was located in this review.” That is an evidence-gap statement, not a denial of living or historical knowledge.

**Evidence confidence:** **Insufficient** for a place-specific Victorian cultural attribution. Botanical occurrence and fruit edibility are separate questions and do not repair this cultural evidence gap.

## Content controls carried forward

- Samphire remains education only; the Gunaikurnai account does not identify every plant sold or known as samphire.
- Bulbine underground parts remain non-actionable because harvesting is destructive, Victorian identity is unresolved for a novice and no safe field preparation has cleared the gate.
- Nardoo remains non-actionable because thiaminase and processing failure can cause serious harm. Cultural processing knowledge is not converted into a shortened recipe.
- Nitre-bush receives no invented cultural attribution. A later source must be evaluated for authority, Country, exact botanical scope, permission and safety before the decision changes.
- Cultural evidence never substitutes for botanist, toxicology, nutrition, conservation, legal or novice-comprehension review.
