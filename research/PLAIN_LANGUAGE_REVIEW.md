## Editing checks

- Prefer a visible action over an abstract label: “keep checking” rather than “monitor”; “tell rescuers” rather than “handover”; “when to call for help” rather than “escalation threshold”.
- Keep necessary terms, but explain them locally. Examples include personal locator beacon (PLB), hypothermia and pressure immobilisation.
- Keep warnings and unresolved decisions beside the affected instruction. In particular, do not hide the unresolved order of severe-bleeding and airway care, the snakebite bandage method, movement limits or cooling methods.
- Use **With a first-aid kit** and **Without a first-aid kit**. Explain that the second section also applies when a kit is missing the required item. Shelter and cooling sections name their additional supplies separately.
- Preserve exact quantities, product identities, scientific names, source IDs, official names, uncertainty and excluded techniques. This pass did not replace the source register or reinterpret a scientific result.
- Mark incomplete instructions as incomplete. A phrase such as “use the reviewed method” cannot pass as a finished practical instruction when that method is absent.

## What remains technical, and why

Source titles, quotations, scientific names, product identifiers, clinical terminology in specialist questions, study methods and register IDs remain exact where needed to compare evidence. The old commercial reports and decision history remain historical records, not current book prose. The rule still applies if their material is reused.

Detailed evidence tables are research working material, not text ready to paste into the book. Any wording drawn from them must be translated for the reader and checked against its sources before use. This review does not claim that every sentence in every research file has been rewritten.

## Before any page becomes usable

Ask a new reader to explain when the page applies, what to do first, what comes next, when to stop and what to do without the listed item. Use harmless props where a demonstration helps. Record errors and any hints given. Have specialists check the exact safety wording and any later changes. Revise and retest when an error could cause harm.
