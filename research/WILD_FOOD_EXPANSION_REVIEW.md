# Wild-food and Traditional Owner knowledge expansion review

**Date:** 7 September 2026  
**Applies to:** illustrated working manuscript 0.4, chiefly chapter 11  
**Result:** useful working expansion, not approval to identify or eat a wild plant

## What changed

The chapter no longer relies on two short plant examples. It now contains five pictured, species-specific profiles:

1. native raspberry (*Rubus parvifolius*);
2. native pigface (*Carpobrotus rossii*);
3. Warrigal greens (*Tetragonia tetragonoides*);
4. muntries (*Kunzea pomifera*); and
5. cherry ballart (*Exocarpos cupressiformis*), presented for advanced recognition rather than as a useful calorie source.

It also contains a non-harvest murnong page and an exact coast-wattle example that explains why a generic “wattleseed” recipe is unsafe. Eleven photographs show different views of the five pictured species. Each image is source-identified, rights-recorded and visually inspected. None has been authenticated by the project's own botanist, and none is represented as a complete identification plate.

This is deliberately not an encyclopaedic list. A short, honest set is more useful than many names with weak identity, toxicity, preparation, conservation or cultural evidence.

## Gate used for every entry

An entry was allowed only when the draft could state all of the following clearly:

- the exact current taxon or a visible warning about taxonomic uncertainty;
- Victorian habitat or distribution from an authoritative flora source;
- the exact usable part rather than whole-plant “edibility”;
- preparation and stop conditions supported separately from the botanical description;
- important uncertainty, lookalikes and missing diagnostic views;
- legal and contamination limits;
- a source-backed energy value, or a direct statement that no defensible value was found; and
- the cultural authority and Country where Traditional Owner knowledge was used.

Taste is included only as an expectation after identification. A skin rub, smell, animal feeding, cooking or tiny taste is not an identification or detoxification test.

The calorie comparison in the chapter uses kilojoules and kilocalories and explains the conversion. For scale, it compares two weighed examples with FSANZ's 8700 kJ average-adult food-label reference while stating that this is not a personal survival requirement. It does not promise that a wild patch will supply 100 g, prescribe a ration or substitute dry-weight figures for wet fruit.

## Image review

The nine new photographs were obtained from Wikimedia Commons source pages with CC BY or CC BY-SA reuse terms. The files are local so the reader does not depend on a network connection. They show:

- native raspberry stem/prickles and whole scrambling habit;
- native pigface leaves and stems;
- Warrigal greens habit and a detached upper leaf;
- muntries flowers and fruit;
- cherry ballart fruiting branch and tree habit.

Each file was opened and checked against its source description and intended caption. This confirms that the file is readable and that the caption does not claim a feature the picture cannot show. It does not authenticate the species. The exact creator, source, licence, resolution, checksum and limitation are in [IMAGE_REGISTER](../IMAGE_REGISTER.md), with canonical reader metadata in `book/assets/manifest.json`.

Several potential images were rejected. A common-name or file-page label was not accepted where the underlying taxon conflicted with current Victorian taxonomy. No AI-generated plant illustration was used. The missing lookalike plates remain visible requirements rather than being simulated.

## Aboriginal knowledge and ICIP

The editorial approach changed from generic “Aboriginal use” to named Country, named source and a limited purpose. The controlling source is the Federation of Victorian Traditional Owner Corporations' *Traditional Owner Native Foods and Botanicals Strategy*. It frames this knowledge as Indigenous Cultural and Intellectual Property and calls for Traditional Owner leadership, consent and benefit rather than extraction.

The current chapter therefore:

- gives high-level murnong context separately for Wadawurrung, Gunditjmara, Taungurung, Dja Dja Wurrung and Wurundjeri Woiwurrung sources;
- preserves **Muurang** and the broader *Microseris* wording used in the Gunditjmara source rather than silently forcing it into one modern taxon;
- mentions muntrie continuity in a Gunditjmara plan without claiming that its word necessarily maps to the exact specimen pictured;
- does not transfer names or practices from one Country to all Victoria;
- does not reproduce cultural images, medicinal recipes, harvest locations, seasonal calendars or restricted knowledge; and
- records that public access to a document is not blanket permission to republish its knowledge.

This personal edition still respects ICIP and copyright. Any shared or published edition must reopen scope, wording, permission, attribution and benefit-sharing with the relevant Traditional Owner organisations. Cultural authority does not replace botanical or toxicological review; each answers a different question.

## Legal and environmental boundary

The chapter now tells the reader to use carried food first and not leave a safe, findable position to chase minor calories. Collection is conditional on law, landholder permission and current restrictions. Regulation 41 of Victoria's National Parks Regulations 2024 prohibits taking or damaging vegetation in a park. Forest produce on public land and protected flora have separate permission and licensing controls. An emergency is not represented as a general collecting exemption.

Roadside spray, contaminated runoff, floodwater, mine areas, animal waste and uncertain land history are treated as reasons to reject a specimen. Digging, bark stripping and taking threatened plants are excluded.

## Checks completed

- Botanical names and Victorian context compared with VicFlora and separate flora or government sources.
- Food-part and preparation claims checked separately from botanical descriptions.
- Fresh and dry nutrition bases kept separate; exact values were not moved between species.
- Aboriginal material traced to Traditional Owner organisations or Victorian Traditional Owner governance sources.
- Image file, source page, licence, intended caption and visible limitations recorded.
- Warning boxes placed before the preparation advice they qualify.
- Plain-language pass completed while retaining scientific names, conditions and uncertainty.

## Work still required before field reliance

1. A Victorian botanist checks each final plate, caption, taxon, feature list, distribution statement and lookalike stop rule.
2. A toxicology or qualified food-safety reviewer checks edible part, preparation, allergy/health warnings and exclusions.
3. Relevant Traditional Owner organisations decide whether the attributed cultural context is appropriate and whether any fuller collaboration is wanted.
4. Missing diagnostic photographs are sourced or commissioned: whole plant, leaf surfaces and attachment, stem/bark, flowers, ripe fruit with scale and matched lookalikes.
5. Untrained readers attempt identification exercises using verified specimens or photographs, with no tasting or consumption.
6. The final print/PDF proof is checked for colour, crop, caption association, scale and low-light readability.

Until those checks pass, the chapter is a well-sourced personal learning draft—not permission to identify a new emergency food from the book alone.
