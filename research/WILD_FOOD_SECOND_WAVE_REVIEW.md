# Wild-food second-wave review — working edition 0.5

**Date:** 7 September 2026  
**Scope:** Ruby saltbush, prickly currant-bush, lilly pilly and the European-blackberry complex; four further candidates deliberately withheld.  
**Status:** Author research, source comparison, rights checks and standalone-image inspection only. No botanist, food-safety specialist, nutritionist or Traditional Owner organisation has reviewed the assembled pages.

## Outcome

The chapter now gives the reader more than a list of plant names. Each new pictured entry places the following in one sequence: the features to check together, Victorian range, several source-identified views, the exact food part, conditional preparation, expected taste, honest energy context, lookalike or contamination limits and a stop rule.

That is still not a safe first-time field-identification system. Photographs can hide scale, underside features, natural variation, hybrids and lookalikes. All eating instructions begin only **after** the plant is already known with certainty, access is lawful and the specimen is uncontaminated. Unknown plants are not tested by tasting, rubbing on skin, watching animals or cooking.

This pass adds 13 plant photographs. Together with the earlier set, edition 0.5 contains 24 plant photographs across nine food-relevant species. It does not claim that all nine are actionable foods: cherry ballart remains recognition/education only, and blackberry is rejected on public land and conditional only at a private patch whose treatment history is confirmed by the responsible person.

## What the numbers can and cannot tell the reader

The book uses food energy to stop small fruit being mistaken for a dependable ration. It does not prescribe a daily survival target.

| Food | Source basis | Illustrative 250 g arithmetic | What the book says |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Ruby saltbush fruit | Energy not determined in the located older table | Not calculable | Tiny flavour supplement; no ration claim |
| Prickly currant-bush fruit | No suitable species-specific fresh result located | Not calculable | Refreshment only; seed and tiny-fruit mass make adequacy especially uncertain |
| Lilly pilly fruit | Historical 89 kJ/100 g raw-fruit value | About 223 kJ / 53 kcal | Low energy; not a serving recommendation, and safe raw amount is unknown |
| Commercial raw blackberry proxy | Current FSANZ value of 192 kJ/100 g including fibre | About 480 kJ / 115 kcal | Scale comparison only; public-land fruit is rejected and field yield is not promised |

The 8700 kJ figure used elsewhere in the chapter is the Australian food-label reference for an average adult. It is not a minimum, a ration or an estimate for this reader. At the proxy values above, 250 g lilly pilly is about 2.6% and 250 g blackberry about 5.5% of that label reference. Personal needs vary with body, age, work, temperature, injury, health and rescue delay. The arithmetic therefore illustrates scale; it does not decide what a person should eat.

| European blackberry | Thicket habit; flowers; ripe/unripe fruit | Measured cane/prickles; leaf upper and underside; attached core after picking; matched native brambles; treatment/site-history information cannot be supplied by an image |

The botanical requirement remains several diagnostic views **plus** a matched comparison and plain-language key. Adding more attractive photographs without closing these gaps would create volume, not reliable identification.

## Traditional Owner knowledge and attribution

Aboriginal knowledge is treated as authoritative within its own source domain: the named knowledge holders, Country, language and practice. It is not flattened into generic “Aboriginal bush tucker,” and another organisation's account is not used to fill a Victorian attribution gap.

- **Girrkitj:** attributed to DJAARA and Dja Dja Wurrung Country/Djandak. DJAARA states that the knowledge remains Dja Dja Wurrung intellectual property and its resource is not to be republished without consent. The guide also says it draws on several sources, does not guarantee every statement's accuracy and has not tested the documented traditional uses. This personal draft contains only a short attributed summary. Any sharing, publication or fuller method must return to DJAARA for scope, wording, consent and benefit.
- **Morr:** recorded as Woi wurrung in Museums Victoria's Bunjilaka guide. It is a bounded institutional record, not direct Traditional Owner approval, and is not presented as a universal Victorian name.
- **Wanduin:** recorded specifically as the Gunaikurnai name for the lilly-pilly fruit in the Bunjilaka guide. It is a bounded institutional record, not direct Traditional Owner approval. The separate Wreck Bay account remains separate.
- **Pike** and **Dullum Dullum:** the Bunjilaka guide places these with Woi wurrung and Wemba Wemba respectively. They appear only in the withheld-candidate explanation; a historical processing reference is not converted into an improvised recipe.

Respect does not mean asking Traditional Owner material to carry claims it does not make. Cultural authority controls cultural knowledge. VicFlora still controls the working botanical account; food-safety, toxicology, nutrition and medicine need their own qualified evidence and review. A future shared edition needs collaboration, not extraction from publicly visible pages.

## Contradictions and decisions

1. **More species versus safe completeness:** many Victorian plants have documented food histories, but a long name list would not help a stranded beginner identify or prepare them. Four entries were expanded only where identity, food part, images and stop rules could be discussed together; four were withheld.
2. **Highly respected knowledge versus invented transfer:** the founder asked that Aboriginal knowledge receive strong attention and respect. The manuscript therefore prefers named Traditional Owner sources and exact attribution. It does not rank every knowledge source as interchangeable or use cultural esteem to bypass consent, botanical identity or clinical evidence.
3. **Useful calories versus false precision:** a number is given only when the species/record, edible part and fresh/dry state are known. Missing energy stays missing. Historical lilly-pilly and commercial-blackberry values are visibly qualified.
4. **Blackberry food versus spray risk:** edibility does not make a public-land thicket acceptable. The public-land branch is rejected; private-land fruit is conditional on permission and exact-patch treatment confirmation from the responsible person. Unknown history means stop.
5. **More angles versus false authentication:** multiple photographs reduce some ambiguity but do not authenticate the pictured plant or the reader's specimen. Captions state the location, taxonomic and missing-view limits.

## Release blockers

Before any of these pages can be labelled field-ready:

1. A Victorian botanist must review each final image, feature list, distribution statement and matched lookalike panel together.
2. A food-safety/toxicology reviewer must approve the exact usable part, preparation, contaminant rejection and health warnings.
3. A nutrition professional must check every energy value, unit, edible-part basis, calculation and adequacy explanation.
4. The relevant Traditional Owner organisation must decide the wording, attribution, ICIP scope and permission for any version shared beyond this private working copy.
5. Untrained readers must be tested with verified photographs or specimens without eating anything. A dangerous false-positive identification is a test failure.
6. Current Victorian land, protected-flora, weed and site-treatment rules must be rechecked for the final edition and at actual collection.

Successful file tests, repeated web sources and high evidence confidence do not complete those gates. The field-ready flag remains **NO**.
