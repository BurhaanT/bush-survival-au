# Fish-atlas image rights and provenance packet

**Checked:** 29 September 2026  
**Scope:** acquired external image candidates IM-177–IM-183  
**Edition boundary:** personal, local, non-commercial working edition  
**Release status:** working evidence only; `FIELD_READY_BUILD=NO`

## Decision

Seven external files were retained for the incomplete Victorian fish-recognition and legal-disposition atlas. Each retained file has a traceable display or file page, named creator, explicit licence or public-domain statement, local integrity record and image-specific limitation. None is a complete identification key, food-safety clearance, legal-disposition decision or expert endorsement.

The two Fishes of Australia photographs, IM-177 and IM-181, are licensed **CC BY-NC-SA 3.0**. They are therefore restricted to this personal non-commercial edition unless the project obtains separate permission or replaces them. The ShareAlike conditions must be preserved if the working edition is shared within the licence. IM-178 and IM-179 are recorded as public-domain items on their file pages. IM-180, IM-182 and IM-183 use the exact Creative Commons versions stated below.

All seven retained external files are unchanged. Automatic display scaling is not an image modification. The hashes below identify the local retained files; a matching hash does not independently authenticate the source specimen or its species identification.

## What this packet does not clear

The rights records do not clear factual identification. Before field reliance, the image set still needs:

1. a qualified fish-identification review of every caption and visible feature;
2. a matched carp mouth/barbel view, verified carp–goldfish hybrid example and better modern leather-form evidence;
3. redfin dorsal-spine and opercular-spine hazard views plus juvenile, dull and folded-fin examples;
4. a reviewer-approved non-colour black/yellowfin-bream separator, if one can be made reliable for a novice, and an explicit hybrid stop rule;
5. testing against verified difficult images, with every dangerous false positive treated as a release blocker;
6. a fresh legal and health-warning check for the exact water and date; and
7. a commercial-use rights review and replacements for non-commercial assets if the project ever leaves the personal edition.

Until those gates pass, the photographs remain supporting recognition candidates only. An unknown fish stays unknown; one image, colour patch, fin or common name never authorises eating or a species-specific legal action.
