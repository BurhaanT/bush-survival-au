# Victorian Bush Survival Field Guide

> **Archived commercial assessment:** Commercial development was parked on 4 September 2026. This document is retained as background and no longer controls whether the personal edition proceeds.

## Gate 1 — Market opportunity assessment

**Assessment date:** 3 September 2026  
**Recommendation:** **MODIFY — proceed to a small paid-validation prototype, not full production.**

## Executive conclusion

There is a credible product opportunity, but the concept in the master brief is not yet a commercially justified book.

The encouraging evidence is that Australia-specific outdoor reference books are active sellers, readers repeatedly value a compact book they can carry in a vehicle or pack, and the current market is fragmented. Survival books, bush-food identification, Aboriginal plant knowledge and highly visual international manuals largely sit in separate products. Customer reviews expose a real gap for a modern, visually clear, Australian-specific guide that tells an inexperienced person what to do first.

The difficult evidence is just as important. Existing Australian paperbacks mostly sell for about A$19–30, while a 192-page premium-colour KDP paperback costs about A$18.74 to print in Australia before royalty. At a customer price of A$49.95, it returns only about A$8.51 per copy. Amazon KDP does not currently offer its lower-cost standard-colour option on Amazon.com.au. A trustworthy guide also carries unusually high development costs: botanical accuracy, medical review, field testing, illustrations, photography, indexing and respectful Indigenous collaboration cannot be replaced by inexpensive AI output.

The best commercial hypothesis is therefore narrower and more urgent than the original concept:

> **A decision-first, illustrated Victorian bush emergency field guide for ordinary bushwalkers, campers and 4WD families: what to do in the first minutes, first night and until rescue when someone is lost, injured or stranded.**

It should not try to be an encyclopaedia of bushcraft, bush foods, traditional knowledge and long-term wilderness living. Its strongest promise is “what to do first, in Victoria, when things go wrong.” A small, carefully governed section of high-confidence plants and natural materials may support that promise. It should not lead the marketing proposition.

The commercial answer is therefore:

- **GO** to a 15–20-page prototype, customer interviews, price testing and print quotes.
- **NO-GO for now** on a full manuscript, large illustration commission or series.
- Commit to production only after people demonstrate willingness to pay A$39.95–49.95 and the unit economics improve through a short-run/direct-sales plan or a materially smaller book.

## 1. Evidence in favour

### Active demand in adjacent Australian categories

The strongest current signal is not a survival title but Tim Low's *Wild Food Plants of Australia*. On the research date its Amazon Australia listing showed A$25.99, 327 ratings at 4.7 stars and a one-day Books rank of #530, including #1 in Foraging and Survival & Emergency Preparedness. The rank may have been lifted by a Father's Day promotion and is only a snapshot, but the rating count and longevity demonstrate durable interest in portable, Australia-specific natural-history guidance. [Amazon listing](https://www.amazon.com.au/dp/0207169306)

Samantha Martin's Indigenous-authored *Bush Tukka Guide* showed A$22.75, 23 ratings and a one-day Books rank of #3,498. Museums Victoria describes it as a bestselling 248-page guide with photographs, maps and toxicity warnings. [Amazon listing](https://www.amazon.com.au/dp/1741178274) · [Museums Victoria](https://store.museumsvictoria.com.au/collections/books/products/bush-tukka-guide-2nd-edition)

Two direct Australian survival/bushcraft competitors also showed active demand: Richard Graves' *Australian Bushcraft* ranked #6,170 with 13 ratings, while Bob Cooper's *Outback Survival* ranked #15,017 with 132 ratings. These ranks cannot be converted responsibly into annual sales without sustained history, but they show that relevant physical books still transact. [Graves listing](https://www.amazon.com.au/dp/176079127X) · [Cooper listing](https://www.amazon.com.au/dp/0733637515)

### A substantial reachable audience

The latest AusPlay release, summarised by Bushwalking Victoria, puts Victorian bushwalking participation at approximately 937,035 people, or 16.1% of the state population. The Caravan Industry Association's 2024 report records 3.9 million overnight caravan/camping trips and A$2.0 billion of spend in Victoria. These figures do not establish book demand, but they show that the proposed customers are not a microscopic enthusiast group. [AusPlay](https://www.ausport.gov.au/clearinghouse/research/ausplay/results) · [Bushwalking Victoria summary](https://bushwalkingvictoria.org.au/category/news-and-information/news/) · [Caravan and Camping State of Industry report](https://www.rmscloud.com/hubfs/APAC%20Field%20Marketing%20FY26/Caravan%20and%20Camping%20State%20of%20Industry%202025.pdf)

### Reviews describe the product gap in the customer's language

Readers value Australian relevance, clear instructions, mental preparation and a book suitable for the glovebox or pack. A Goodreads reviewer explicitly wished for a deluxe Cooper edition with full-colour plant and animal photographs. Reviews of Tim Low praise the compact form and photographs, while reviews of Martin praise the maps and warnings. [Goodreads — Outback Survival](https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/15785508-outback-survival) · [Australian Hiker — Wild Food Plants](https://australianhiker.com.au/gear/wild-food-plants-of-australia/)

The complaints are even more revealing:

- A Graves review says too much of the book concerns shelters that could take weeks to build—poor guidance for someone who is actually lost.
- Reviews of American benchmark *Bushcraft 101* say it is US-specific, has too few useful illustrations and often describes a technique without enough visual construction detail.
- Reviews of Low say a reader who does not already know a plant's name lacks an identification pathway, and that some images, seasonality, descriptions and preparation guidance are insufficient.
- A DK handbook is praised for visual explanation but criticised for being too heavy to carry and sometimes too shallow.
- Cooper is praised as a practical vehicle reference but criticised for print/paper quality, limited colour imagery and at least one potentially outdated medical claim.

These complaints support a guide organised around emergency decisions and field tasks, with illustrations selected for action or identification—not a longer collection of bushcraft lore. [Amazon — Australian Bushcraft](https://www.amazon.com.au/dp/176079127X) · [Amazon — Bushcraft 101](https://www.amazon.com.au/dp/1440579776) · [Google Play — Bushcraft 101 reviews](https://play.google.com/store/books/details/Bushcraft_101_A_Field_Guide_to_the_Art_of_Wilderne?hl=en-US&id=kDvsDQAAQBAJ) · [BuyWisely — Wild Food Plants reviews](https://buywisely.com.au/au/product/wild-food-plants-of-australia-tim-low) · [Amazon — The Survival Handbook](https://www.amazon.com.au/dp/0241437482)

### The use case is real and matches official safety advice

Victoria Police lists poor planning, changing weather, hypothermia or heat illness, equipment failures, navigation errors, fatigue, insufficient water and over-reliance on technology among common factors in people becoming lost. Its advice emphasises stopping and thinking, calling 000, shelter, remaining visible and generally staying in place—exactly the decision sequence the proposed product should make usable under stress. [Victoria Police outdoor and bush safety](https://www.police.vic.gov.au/outdoor-and-bush-safety)

## 2. Evidence against

### The intended retail price collides with inexpensive incumbents

The relevant Australian guides cluster around A$19–30: Cooper A$19.19 at the snapshot, Graves A$24.99, Low A$25.99 and Martin A$22.75. DK's 320-page, fully illustrated hardback was A$20.98. A new independent guide may be better targeted, but customers will still compare it with these prices. A$49.95 is a substantial premium for an unknown author and brand.

### KDP is poorly suited to the proposed colour book in Australia

KDP's current Australian paperback formula for a regular-trim premium-colour book over 40 pages is A$2.42 plus A$0.085 per page. A 192-page book therefore costs A$18.74 to print. The lower-cost standard-colour option is not available on Amazon.com.au. At a customer-facing price of A$49.95 including GST, the approximate KDP royalty is only A$8.51. [KDP paperback printing costs](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201834340) · [KDP colour options](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/GX56BFPW4BKNPGFW) · [KDP paperback royalties](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201834330) · [KDP taxes and GST](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/GJX5NGJX75QBTJM9)

This does not make KDP useless. It is valuable for proofs, no-inventory availability and international long-tail sales. It does mean KDP should not be assumed to be the best core manufacturing channel for a colour field guide.

### Trustworthiness is expensive

A visual guide that is safe enough to use in an emergency needs professional editing, a skilled information designer, botanical verification, medical review, legal/update checks, field testing and illustration or photography with verified diagnostic accuracy. Indicative Australian freelance editorial rates are A$70–90 an hour for newer editors and A$100–150 for established editors. The Australian Society of Authors' FY2026–27 illustration guidance lists A$547 for a colour half-page book illustration and A$778 for a full page; 30 half-page pieces alone indicate about A$16,410. [IPEd pay rates](https://www.iped-editors.org/about-editing/editors-pay-rates/) · [ASA illustration rates](https://www.asauthors.org.au/rates-of-pay/book-illustrations/)

A responsible full production could plausibly require A$35,000–100,000 or more in external cash costs. That range is an early planning assumption, not a quote. At A$8.51 contribution per KDP colour sale, A$35,000 alone requires roughly 4,120 copies before advertising, overheads, returns and tax. The high end requires nearly 11,800 copies.

### Indigenous knowledge cannot be treated as sourced content alone

The original positioning proposes “properly sourced traditional Aboriginal knowledge.” For a commercial work, attribution and citations are necessary but not sufficient. The AIATSIS Code says Indigenous people have rights to own, control, maintain and authorise the use of their cultural and intellectual property. Victorian organisations have also warned that native-food and medicinal knowledge is too often commercialised without co-design or benefit sharing. [AIATSIS Code for Ethics](https://aiatsis.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-02/aiatsis-guide-code-ethics-jan22.pdf) · [AIATSIS ethical publishing guidance](https://aiatsis.gov.au/sites/default/files/2020-09/ethical-publishing-guidelines.pdf) · [Federation of Victorian Traditional Owner Corporations](https://fvtoc.com.au/sections/native-foods-botanicals-strategy/) · [Victorian Aboriginal Heritage Council](https://www.aboriginalheritagecouncil.vic.gov.au/taking-care-culture-discussion-paper/plants-and-animals)

Authentic co-creation could become valuable differentiation. It also introduces governance, consent, relationship, remuneration and timeline requirements that must be resolved before it becomes a product claim. If that pathway is not ready, it is better to omit this component from the first prototype than to extract it superficially.

### Comprehensiveness conflicts with portability

The proposed subject list can readily become a 300-page, 500-gram book. That would repeat a common competitor problem: useful at home, less useful in a day pack. It would also make plant coverage too shallow for safe identification while still overwhelming the emergency sequence. A field guide needs ruthless exclusions.

### Direct demand remains unproven

No reliable public evidence was found for Australian search volume for “Victorian bush survival,” nor for sustained weekly ranks or unit sales of the direct competitors. Rating counts are cumulative and category ranks are snapshots. A commercially honest conclusion cannot convert those signals into a forecast. The central unknown is whether enough ordinary outdoor users will pay A$39.95–49.95 for this specific promise.

## 4. Market gaps worth testing

The market gap is not “another Australian survival book.” The most defensible gaps are:

1. **Decision order under stress.** A clear first five minutes / first hour / first night sequence, plus stay-versus-move logic and rescue visibility.
2. **Victorian specificity.** Alpine cold, wet temperate forest, Mallee heat, coastal exposure, fire restrictions, local emergency communications and regional resource differences.
3. **Action-quality visual explanation.** Shelter, signalling, navigation and first-aid diagrams that show the sequence, completion test and common failure—not decorative imagery.
4. **Identification workflow.** A very small set of common, high-value species organised by visible diagnostic features, season and region, with dangerous lookalikes—not an unsafe mini-encyclopaedia.
5. **Modern and maintainable safety content.** Current Australian guidance, visible legal warnings, a dated edition and an online corrections/update page.
6. **Physical usability.** High contrast, robust indexing, thumb tabs, quick-reference covers and a strict weight target.
7. **Authentic Indigenous collaboration.** Potentially meaningful, but only if led by consent, co-design, attribution and benefit sharing. This is a partnership model, not a content-mining feature.

## 5. Likely target customer

### Primary buyer

The most attractive segment is the ordinary beginner-to-intermediate Victorian outdoor participant: a bushwalker, camper or 4WD family who plans trips, owns basic emergency gear and wants one trusted reference in a pack or vehicle. This customer values reassurance, clarity and Australian relevance more than advanced primitive-living skills.

The purchase is likely to occur before a trip, as a practical gift or after a near miss—not during an emergency. The book therefore has to sell preparedness while being genuinely usable under stress.

### Secondary buyers

- Parents and partners buying an outdoors gift, especially near Father's Day and Christmas.
- Rural vehicle owners, prospectors, hunters and fishers whose activities overlap remote travel.
- Overseas visitors spending time outside cities, provided the language remains approachable.
- Training providers, outdoor clubs, visitor centres and retailers seeking a Victorian safety reference.

Hardcore survivalists and bushcrafters are easy to target but are a smaller, more demanding audience. They should not control the product architecture. Interest in Aboriginal knowledge should not be treated as an acquisition segment unless a legitimate partnership defines the work.

## 6. Recommended positioning

### Recommended working promise

**Victoria Bush Emergency Field Guide — What to Do First When You Are Lost, Injured or Stranded**

Alternative test title:

**Lost in the Victorian Bush — The Illustrated First Minutes, First Night and Rescue Guide**

Position it as an emergency decision tool, not a comprehensive wilderness-living manual. The word “survival” can remain in metadata and supporting copy, but “bush emergency” and “what to do first” are more differentiated and less theatrical.

### Product boundaries

The first edition should:

- devote roughly 60–70% to communication, decisions, rescue, thermal protection, shelter, water, injuries, navigation and regional/seasonal adaptation;
- include a compact quick-reference section accessible in seconds;
- limit plants and natural materials to perhaps 20–30 high-confidence, genuinely useful entries;
- keep food correctly low in the emergency hierarchy;
- exclude actionable trapping, indiscriminate wildlife harm and dangerous historical methods;
- date legal and medical guidance and maintain a public corrections page;
- treat any Indigenous material through a separately agreed collaboration and governance pathway.

Victoria is a useful proof-of-concept because specificity is the differentiator. “South-eastern Australia” could expand the addressable market, but it risks diluting authority and multiplying ecological and Traditional Owner contexts. Test both labels with customers; do not decide a national series now.

## 7. Recommended format

### Prototype specification to test

- **Primary format:** printed, full-colour field guide.
- **Trim:** 6 × 9 inches (152 × 229 mm), which remains KDP regular trim and provides more diagram space than 5.5 × 8.5.
- **Extent:** target 176–192 pages, with a hard ceiling around 216 pages.
- **Weight target:** under approximately 400 grams after a physical proof.
- **Construction:** matte laminated cover; water-resistant stock or a durable field edition is a later short-run option, not an initial assumption.
- **Navigation:** thumb-indexed colour bands, contents by emergency, regional index, species index and “do this now” inside covers.
- **Visual rule:** every image must help the reader decide, identify or perform.
- **Digital:** an offline phone-friendly companion PDF/ePub can add value, but the image-heavy Kindle file must be tested for delivery cost and navigation. It is secondary to print.

Do not publish the botanical core in black and white; colour may be safety-critical. If price testing rejects the full-colour edition, the safer response is a shorter emergency-essentials book with fewer species—not degraded identification imagery.

KDP premium colour should be used for proofs and no-inventory availability. Obtain short-run digital/offset quotes at 250, 500 and 1,000 copies, and compare a direct-to-consumer model. General bookshop distribution is challenging because distributors commonly require a 40–50% discount, and returns can erase thin margins. [Australian Society of Authors on distribution](https://www.asauthors.org.au/distribution/) · [IngramSpark user guide](https://www.ingramspark.com/hubfs/downloads/user-guide.pdf)

KDP's 70% option is now available in Australia from A$3.99 to A$15.99, less digital delivery at A$0.15 per megabyte. ### Scenario model—not a sales forecast

Using A$49.95 premium-colour print contribution of A$8.51 and A$14.99/15 MB Kindle contribution of A$7.96:

| First-year scenario | Print units | Kindle units | Approx. contribution before ads and overhead |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Conservative | 300 | 75 | A$3,150 |
| Base | 900 | 250 | A$9,649 |

## 9. Major risks

| Risk | Why it matters | Early mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Market too narrow | Broad outdoor participation may not translate into a A$40–50 Victorian book purchase. | Paid landing-page and preorder test before manuscript. |
| Price/value mismatch | Incumbents cost A$19–30; premium price needs obvious utility. | Compare prototype and price points in realistic purchase settings. |

## 10. Cheap validation experiments

The point of validation is willingness to pay, not compliments.

### Experiment A — representative prototype

Create 15–20 finished pages containing:

- the lost-person first five minutes decision tree;
- a first-night alpine or wet-forest scenario;
- one shelter sequence;
- emergency water and signalling pages;
- a current snakebite quick-action page reviewed by a qualified Australian clinician;
- three genuinely diagnostic plant comparison panels reviewed by a Victorian botanist.

This is enough to reveal whether the design is usable and credible without paying for an entire book.

### Experiment B — offer and price test

Build two simple landing-page variants:

1. Victoria-specific, full-colour guide at A$39.95.
2. The same offer at A$49.95, or a “south-eastern Australia” positioning split test.

Use cover mock-ups and real prototype spreads. Collect an email only after showing the price. Stronger still, take a refundable A$5 reservation or a preorder. Indicative pass thresholds, set before launch:

- at least 150 qualified sign-ups at a blended cost of A$6 or less;
- at least 20 paid A$5 reservations or 10 full-price preorders;
- landing-page email conversion of at least 8% from qualified traffic;
- no collapse in intent at A$39.95–49.95.

These thresholds are hypotheses, not industry facts. Their purpose is to prevent post-hoc rationalisation.

### Experiment C — problem interviews

Conduct 12–20 short interviews across bushwalkers, family campers, 4WD travellers and gift buyers. Ask about the last outdoor book or safety product actually purchased, near misses, where a guide would be carried, trusted authorities and the maximum credible price. Show the prototype late in the interview so it does not lead the answers.

### Experiment D — retailer test

Show a one-page sell sheet and prototype to 8–12 Victorian outdoor stores, visitor centres, bushwalking clubs or training providers. Seek three written expressions of interest that state quantity, wholesale discount and acceptable retail price. Friendly enthusiasm without a quantity is not validation.

### Experiment E — small traffic test

Spend A$300–600 across tightly targeted Meta/Google placements and one or two appropriate community sponsorships. Do not spam Facebook or Reddit groups. Track click-through, page conversion, paid reservations, audience and message. Search interest and Amazon rank for the competitor set should be recorded weekly for 8–12 weeks to replace the current one-day snapshot.

### Experiment F — manufacturing reality check

Order a KDP premium-colour proof and obtain like-for-like Australian or regional short-run quotes at 250, 500 and 1,000 copies. Test colour accuracy, page opacity, rain/sweat handling, binding, final weight and packing/fulfilment cost.

## 11. Gate recommendation

### **MODIFY — conditional GO to validation only**

The project is worth a controlled A$4,000–12,000 prototype-and-validation phase. It is not yet worth a 176–250-page production commitment.

The original concept is too broad and its assumed KDP colour economics are unattractive. The opportunity becomes more promising when it is narrowed to a Victorian emergency decision guide, when Indigenous content is governed through genuine collaboration rather than content sourcing, and when manufacturing is treated as a channel decision rather than “KDP by default.”

Do not authorise more than A$10,000 of cumulative external production spend until all of the following are true:

- customers demonstrate paid intent at A$39.95 or above;
- the prototype performs well in timed usability tests;
- at least 20 paid reservations or 10 full preorders and three credible retail/partner expressions of interest are obtained, or a stronger equivalent signal;
- a medical reviewer, botanical reviewer and Indigenous collaboration pathway are scoped and costed;
- physical print quotes support at least A$12 contribution through Amazon/retail-compatible channels or A$18 through direct sale;
- the base-case three-year contribution can recover the full production budget without relying on an unvalidated future series.

If those thresholds fail, stop or pivot to a much smaller emergency quick-reference product. That would be a useful result, not a failed book.

## 12. Next three actions

1. **Define and commission the prototype.** Lock the decision-first promise, 15–20-page content brief, visual system, page/weight ceiling and two scoped expert reviews.
2. **Run paid customer validation.** Launch the two offer/price variants, interview customers and test retailer demand against pre-declared thresholds.
3. **Resolve the operating model.** Obtain proofs and print/fulfilment quotes, then open an appropriately paid Indigenous partnership conversation. Return for Gate 2 only if demand, contribution margin and governance all pass.

## Evidence limitations

- Amazon prices, ratings and ranks are a one-day Australian snapshot and can change hourly.
- No paid keyword tool, BookScan data or publisher sell-through data was available. Unit sales have not been inferred from rank.
- Outdoor participation counts overlap and do not represent unique prospective purchasers.
- Production budgets and direct-sale margins are planning assumptions pending supplier quotes.
- Customer-review themes are directional qualitative evidence, not a statistically representative survey.
- All legal, medical, botanical and platform facts must be rechecked immediately before publication.
