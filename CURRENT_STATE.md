## Where we are

Commercial work is set aside. We are making a personal guide covering Victoria, whatever activity brought the reader into the bush.

On 2 October 2026, D-076/R-498/S-499 established the cross-book life-first rule. A genuine immediate threat of death or serious injury takes priority over ordinary Victorian activity rules, but only to the extent necessary, proportionate and least harmful when no safer lawful course is reasonably available. This is a stricter editorial trigger than the general statutory test in *Crimes Act 1958* (Vic) s 322R; it is not a survival exemption, prospective permission, immunity or prediction that a defence will succeed. Sections 322G, 322I, 322R and 322S frame a fact-specific statutory defence and abolish common-law necessity. Emergency contact, responder direction, existing technical/welfare/contamination gates, stopping when the threat ends and later factual disclosure remain controlling. Edition 0.15 carries that distinction in the front matter, first-minutes, plant, water, fire, signalling, fishing and wildlife guidance and on eleven affected detached visuals. ## Current book definition

**Victoria Bush Emergency Field Guide — What to Do First When You Are Lost, Injured or Stranded**

Primary user: the founder and people accompanying them in Victoria, regardless of whether they arrived by foot, vehicle, boat or another activity. The guide remains understandable to an untrained, stressed reader. It begins with the current emergency rather than the recreational activity and includes only survival techniques and natural-resource entries that pass the evidence gate.

Practical first-aid entries provide “with kit” and “without kit” paths, with independent review still required. The no-kit path uses defensible measures and ordinary materials where supported. It states when missing medicine or equipment cannot be replaced.
