## Visual risk rules

- AI-generated imagery may be used only for non-factual concept exploration. It must not be used as the identification reference for plants, fungi, animals, tracks, medical procedures, knots or safety-critical construction.
- A beautiful whole-plant photograph is insufficient if the diagnostic leaf, bark, flower, fruit or scale is missing.
- Illustrations must show uncertainty and “stop” conditions where a novice cannot distinguish a lookalike safely.
- Maps must record data date, projection/generalisation and what absence means.
- Medical diagrams require the same sign-off as the words they accompany.
- For the explicitly unapproved working edition only, D-031–D-056 permit source-checked, rights-recorded assets with visible limits before independent sign-off. This does not approve medical teaching accuracy, recovery-position, snakebite-bandaging, severe-bleeding, choking or drowning skill, casualty movement, navigation/signalling performance, carried-sheet geometry, shelter-air safety, water/fire performance or botanical identification from an incomplete image.
- Indigenous cultural material, artwork, language, Country maps or motifs require explicit authority and agreed attribution; a generic “Aboriginal style” is prohibited.

## Later asset strategy to compare

1. Original field photography commissioned to a shot list.
2. Existing images licensed from botanical institutions, photographers and government sources.
3. Original vector diagrams commissioned under a reusable series-ready licence.
4. Carefully verified public-domain or Creative Commons assets with permanent licence records.

Selection should be based on diagnostic/action value, total rights cost and reviewer effort—not on image acquisition price alone.
