## How the reader finds advice

Organise the book by the reader's problem, not as a bushcraft course. The reader should find the first useful instruction within seconds.

The guide works whatever brought the reader into the bush. Mention hiking, camping, 4WD, fishing or another activity only when it changes the safest action. First-aid entries put shared urgent actions first, then **With a first-aid kit** and **Without a first-aid kit**. Name the supplies needed; the second section also applies when a kit is missing them.

Apply the [plain-language rules](EDITORIAL_STYLE_GUIDE.md) to all existing and future sections, including the planned headings below. Page counts remain planning estimates, not targets that justify adding filler or cutting safety detail.

## Inside covers / emergency cards — 4 pages

- “Do this now” lost-person sequence
- 000 / PLB / phone / satellite communication choices
- Stay versus move decision tree
- Snakebite, catastrophic bleeding and immediate danger quick actions
- Signal pattern and rescue visibility checklist

## Part 1 — First minutes and first hour — 24–30 pages

1. Stop, breathe, think and check what has happened
2. Immediate dangers and life threats
3. Choosing how to call for help
4. Stay, go a short way back or move: a checked decision guide
5. Help rescuers find you
6. Group, child and vehicle scenarios
7. The first-hour checklist

## Part 2 — Survive the first night — 32–38 pages

1. Victoria's cold and heat risks
2. Choosing a place and checking its dangers
3. Clothing, insulating layers and heat loss to the ground
4. Rapid shelters by available material and environment
5. Wet forest, alpine, Mallee and coastal variations
6. Fire: when it helps, when it is unlawful or dangerous, and safer alternatives
7. Night-time signalling and morale

The fire material has two distinct parts: bushfire threat and last-resort shelter, then the separate decision about using a stove or lighting a fire. On-foot, enclosed-car, building and designated-refuge cases require different reviewed wording. EP-015 and RB-006 record the outstanding decisions; no route, shelter or protective item is guaranteed safe.

## Part 3 — Injury, illness and getting medical help — 24–30 pages

1. First-aid priorities and limits
2. Serious deterioration, severe bleeding and known-cause emergencies
3. Snakebite
4. Broken bones, sprains and being unable to move
5. Hypothermia and heat illness
6. Burns, bites, stings and allergic reaction
7. Dehydration and water-related illness
8. When trying to get out yourself is more dangerous than waiting

All medical content requires current Australian sources and independent clinical review.

## Part 4 — Water, navigation and rescue — 26–32 pages

1. Water planning and risk trade-offs
2. Finding likely water by region and landscape
3. Collection, treatment and contamination
4. Map, compass and terrain orientation
5. Phone maps and technology failure
6. Safe movement, route marking and turnaround rules
7. Ground-to-air, light, sound and contrast signalling
8. Understanding how searchers look for people

## Part 5 — Victorian environments — 20–26 pages

- Alpine and High Country
- Wet forests, Otways and Gippsland
- Central and north-east forests
- Grampians/Gariwerd and western ranges
- Mallee and Wimmera
- Coast, estuaries and major rivers
- Seasonal calendars and rapidly changing hazards

This part should change decisions, not become regional travel writing.

## Part 6 — Carefully limited natural resources — 24–30 pages

- 20–30 high-confidence plants/materials at most
- Whole-plant pictures and close-ups that show how to distinguish the species
- Victorian distribution and season
- Emergency relevance and safe use
- Dangerous lookalikes and explicit “do not use” conditions
- A small number of materials for making cord or rope, containers or insulating layers
- Food kept behind more urgent survival needs

No species is approved for the field edition until it passes the [species register](SPECIES_REGISTER.md) and safety review. D-031–D-041 permit conditional, visibly unapproved working entries during research. Brief public Traditional Owner material may appear only as named, Country-specific context with its ICIP limits; it is not represented as an authorised contribution. Any fuller method, image or shared edition needs the relevant organisation to define scope and authority.
